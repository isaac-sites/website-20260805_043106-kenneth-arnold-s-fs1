## Introduction

The witness reports and police sightings are the foundation of the Belgium radar/visual sightings of 1989–1990. Before fighter aircraft, radar discussions and official investigations entered the story, the case developed through observations by ordinary residents and Belgian gendarmes (police officers). The most influential reports came from the Eupen area on 29 November 1989, when two gendarmes reported seeing a large triangular object with lights, followed by additional observations from other people in the region. Over the following months, hundreds of reports were collected, many describing similar triangular shapes, bright lights and apparently silent movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aliencouncil.com/cases/00056-belgian-wave-1989/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aliencouncil.com">[The Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aliencouncil.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">00056 belgian wave 1989</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CouncilBelgian UFO wave — November 1989 to April 1990 · The CouncilJuly 14, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 14, 2026</span></span></span>

These accounts made the Belgian wave unusual because the witnesses were not a single isolated group. Reports came from civilians, police officers and military personnel, creating a record that investigators could compare across locations and dates. ## Gendarmes and civilian witnesses

The first major sighting of the Belgian wave occurred near Eupen in eastern Belgium on the night of 29 November 1989. Two gendarmes on patrol reported seeing a large triangular object moving slowly at low altitude. Their account became a turning point because police officers were generally regarded by the public as more reliable observers than anonymous witnesses, and because their report was made in an official capacity rather than through a later recollection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-be-sobeps-vague-ovni-volume-1-sobeps-vague-ovni-belge-volume-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl be sobeps vague ovni volume 1 sobeps vague ovni belge volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencySOBEPS Vague d&#x27;OVNI sur la Belgique, Volume 1 (1989–1991), SOBEPS (Société Belge d&#x27;Étude des Phénomènes Spatiaux) · 1991...</span></span></span>

According to accounts collected by the Belgian Society for the Study of Space Phenomena (SOBEPS), the gendarmes described a dark triangular structure with lights positioned around its edges and a central red light. They reported that the object appeared to move silently and slowly, and they contacted local authorities while the observation was taking place. SOBEPS later documented the incident as part of its wider investigation into the Belgian wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-be-sobeps-vague-ovni-volume-1-sobeps-vague-ovni-belge-volume-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl be sobeps vague ovni volume 1 sobeps vague ovni belge volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencySOBEPS Vague d&#x27;OVNI sur la Belgique, Volume 1 (1989–1991), SOBEPS (Société Belge d&#x27;Étude des Phénomènes Spatiaux) · 1991...</span></span></span>

The importance of the Eupen event was not only the status of the first witnesses, but the number of later reports connected to that night. Investigators recorded multiple witness groups in the area, including additional police personnel, who described a broadly similar object. The reports helped establish the recurring image that became associated with the Belgian wave: a large triangular form, three bright lights and unusual low-speed movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Belgian_Wave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Belgian Wave &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiki Disc Belgian Wave &#124; Wiki Disc</span></span></span>

As the sightings spread across Belgium, civilian reports became the largest part of the evidence base. SOBEPS collected witness statements, drawings and descriptions during the wave, eventually publishing extensive case files covering the events. Estimates of the number of people who reported sightings vary, but the episode involved thousands of accounts, making it one of the largest organised collections of UFO reports in Europe at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aliencouncil.com/cases/00056-belgian-wave-1989/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aliencouncil.com">[The Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aliencouncil.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">00056 belgian wave 1989</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CouncilBelgian UFO wave — November 1989 to April 1990 · The CouncilJuly 14, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 14, 2026</span></span></span>

## Shared descriptions across Belgium

One reason the Belgian wave attracted lasting attention was the repeated appearance of similar details in independent accounts. Many witnesses described:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a large triangular or boomerang-like shape;
* three bright white lights arranged near the corners;
* a red light near the centre or underside;
* slow movement or apparent hovering;
* little or no audible engine noise.

</div>

The repeated descriptions were significant because they suggested that many observers were reporting a similar visual impression rather than completely unrelated objects. The pattern was particularly notable during the early months of the wave, when reports from different areas often contained comparable descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uaprad.org/cases/belgian-ufo-wave-1989-1990/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uaprad.org">[UAPRAD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uaprad.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Belgian UFO Wave &#124; UAP Case Research &#124; UAPRAD</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belgian UFO Wave &#124; UAP Case Research &#124; UAPRAD...</span></span></span>

However, similarity between reports is not the same as proof of a single physical object. Investigators have pointed out that witnesses can influence each other, especially during a highly publicised event. Once the triangular-object description became widely known, later observers may have interpreted ambiguous lights through the existing narrative. Skeptical analyses therefore distinguish between the credibility of individual witnesses and the question of what caused the observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufouap.net/en/cases/belgian-ufo-wave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufouap.net">[UFO Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufouap.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">belgian ufo wave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ArchiveBelgian UFO Wave: 1989-1990 Sightings, Radar Claims and Explanations &#124; Global UFO Archive...</span></span></span>

The strongest witness cases are generally those where observers reported events before widespread publicity, where multiple people independently described the same scene, or where official records were created close to the time of observation. The Eupen reports fit this category because they occurred at the beginning of the wave and involved police witnesses. Later cases are more difficult to assess because media attention had already shaped public expectations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-be-sobeps-vague-ovni-volume-1-sobeps-vague-ovni-belge-volume-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl be sobeps vague ovni volume 1 sobeps vague ovni belge volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencySOBEPS Vague d&#x27;OVNI sur la Belgique, Volume 1 (1989–1991), SOBEPS (Société Belge d&#x27;Étude des Phénomènes Spatiaux) · 1991...</span></span></span>

## The role of police sightings in public confidence

Police involvement changed how the Belgian wave was perceived. In many UFO cases, witness credibility becomes a central dispute because reports rely on unknown individuals describing unusual experiences. The Belgian case was different because some early witnesses were serving gendarmes who reported the event through normal channels. ## Limits of eyewitness evidence

Eyewitness reports are valuable because they record what people actually perceived, especially when observations are made by several people in different locations.
