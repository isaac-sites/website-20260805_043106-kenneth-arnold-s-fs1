## Introduction

The Coyne helicopter sighting of 1973 remains one of the better-documented unidentified aerial reports of its era partly because it generated a formal military record rather than existing only as a later recollection. The central document is a U.S. Army “Near Midair Collision with UFO Report” concerning Army helicopter 68-15444, filed after the crew reported an encounter near Mansfield, Ohio, on 18 October 1973. The report preserved the crew’s account, flight circumstances, and unusual observations, but it did not identify the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/coynereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Coyne case, Mansfield, Ohio, 1973, the official reportNovember 23, 1973...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 1973</span></span></span>

The documentation is important because it establishes that the event was treated as an aviation incident by the [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-c33b11/' | relative_url }})’ own organisation. It does not, however, function as proof of what the object was. The records confirm that a trained military aircrew reported an unexplained encounter; they do not independently verify the object’s origin, capabilities, or physical nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/coynereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Coyne case, Mansfield, Ohio, 1973, the official reportNovember 23, 1973...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 1973</span></span></span>

## The original military report

The surviving Army document is a “Near Midair Collision with UFO Report” prepared through Army Reserve flight channels. It identifies the aircraft as Army helicopter 68-15444 and records that the incident occurred at approximately 2305 hours near Mansfield, Ohio. The report lists the crew as Captain Lawrence J. Coyne, First Lieutenant Arrigo Jezzi, Staff Sergeant Robert Yanacsek, and Staff Sergeant John Healey, members of the 316th Medical Detachment (Helicopter Ambulance) associated with the Cleveland U.S. Army Reserve flight facility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/coynereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Coyne case, Mansfield, Ohio, 1973, the official reportNovember 23, 1973...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 1973</span></span></span>

According to the report, the helicopter was returning from Columbus to Cleveland at about 2,500 feet when crew member Robert Yanacsek observed a red light on the horizon. The document states that the object appeared to converge on the helicopter at the same altitude and at an estimated speed “in excess of 600 knots”, creating what the crew perceived as a possible collision risk. Captain Coyne then took control and initiated a descent as an evasive manoeuvre.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/coynereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Coyne case, Mansfield, Ohio, 1973, the official reportNovember 23, 1973...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 1973</span></span></span>

The report also records the later stages of the encounter as described by the crew: a close approach by a large object, unusual lighting effects, and a reported climb of the helicopter despite the crew’s stated control inputs. These details became the basis for later investigations, but the military document itself remains a record of reported observations rather than a technical determination of cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/coynereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Coyne case, Mansfield, Ohio, 1973, the official reportNovember 23, 1973...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 1973</span></span></span>

### Why the document mattered

The significance of the Army report was not that it classified the event as extraterrestrial or confirmed an extraordinary aircraft. Its value was that it created a contemporaneous record containing details that could be compared with later interviews, witness statements, and investigative analyses.

Many UFO reports from the period relied on memory gathered years later. The Coyne case was different: investigators could examine a near-contemporary aviation report produced by military personnel during normal flight operations. This helped make the case a reference point for researchers studying pilot and military witness reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/731018mansfield_report.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report...</span></span></span>

At the same time, the report had limits. It contained no recovered material, instrument recording from the object, radar confirmation publicly linked to the event, or independent measurement of the object’s performance. The strongest evidence remained human testimony and the consistency of those accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/lore/encounters/us-coyne-helicopter-1973" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Transparency Coyne Helicopter Encounter · UFO Transparency</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Transparency Coyne Helicopter Encounter · UFO Transparency</span></span></span>

## Later UFO investigations and archives

After the Army report became known, the case attracted attention from civilian UFO research organisations, particularly the Center for UFO Studies (CUFOS) and investigators associated with the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP). CUFOS investigator Jennie Zeidman examined the incident and collected additional witness information, including accounts from people who reported seeing related lights from the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/hynek-biography/j-allen-hynek-biography/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies J. Allen Hynek Biography</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesJ. Allen Hynek Biography - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

The case was especially significant for CUFOS because it appeared soon after the organisation’s creation in 1973. J. Allen Hynek, a former scientific consultant to the U.S. Air Force’s Project Blue Book, regarded the incident as an important example of a well-reported but unresolved encounter. CUFOS later presented the case as one of its notable investigations because of the number of witnesses and the aviation context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/hynek-biography/j-allen-hynek-biography/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies J. Allen Hynek Biography</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesJ. Allen Hynek Biography - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

NICAP’s later case files also preserved the Army report and related information, presenting the incident as a high-quality unidentified case because of the military witnesses, reported close-range observation, and available documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/731018mansfield_report.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report...</span></span></span>

The timing of the case also shaped its later history. The U.S. Air Force’s Project Blue Book investigation had ended in 1969, with its records transferred to the National Archives in the following years. As a result, the Coyne incident did not pass through the same official Air Force UFO investigation system that handled earlier cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue Book Termination &#124; National ArchivesDecember 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2019</span></span></span>

This distinction is sometimes misunderstood. The absence of a Project Blue Book file does not mean the event lacked official documentation; rather, it occurred after the closure of that particular programme. ## What official documentation can and cannot prove

The Coyne records provide strong evidence for several limited conclusions. They show that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A U.S. Army Reserve helicopter crew reported an unusual aerial encounter on 18 October 1973.
* The crew submitted a formal report through military channels.
* The witnesses were trained aviation personnel operating a military aircraft.
* Later investigators had access to a documented event rather than relying solely on an informal story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/coynereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Coyne case, Mansfield, Ohio, 1973, the official reportNovember 23, 1973...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 1973</span></span></span>

</div>

The records do not prove:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The object’s identity.
* That the object represented technology beyond known aviation.
* That the encounter involved a non-human craft.
* That every reported physical effect occurred exactly as described.

</div>

This distinction is central to the case’s continuing debate. Supporters argue that the combination of military documentation, multiple witnesses, and the apparent aviation hazards make the incident unusually credible among UFO reports. Skeptical analyses focus on the fact that the evidence remains observational, with no physical evidence or independent technical record publicly available to confirm the reported object’s characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/lore/encounters/us-coyne-helicopter-1973" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Transparency Coyne Helicopter Encounter · UFO Transparency</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Transparency Coyne Helicopter Encounter · UFO Transparency</span></span></span>

        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);

        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');

        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {

          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });

    }

      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
