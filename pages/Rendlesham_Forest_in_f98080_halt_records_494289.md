## Introduction

Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt’s investigation is the part of the [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest-incident-1980/' | relative_url }}) incident that left the clearest official paper trail. As deputy commander of RAF Woodbridge, Halt did not witness the first reported encounter personally, but he led a follow-up investigation after reports from security personnel and produced the memorandum “Unexplained Lights”, dated 13 January 1981. He also made a contemporaneous audio recording while investigating unusual lights near Rendlesham Forest. These two records became the central documentary evidence in later debates because they link witness testimony to a named military officer, an official report, and a surviving field recording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/ufo-files-national-archives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National ArchivesMay 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2018</span></span></span>

The records show that US Air Force personnel reported unusual events and that the incident was formally documented. They do not, however, provide an official confirmation that a non-human craft was present. The Ministry of Defence assessed the report as containing no defence significance and did not identify the observations as a military threat. The lasting dispute concerns how to interpret Halt’s observations: as evidence of an unexplained aerial event, as misidentified ordinary phenomena, or as a mixture of several observations and uncertainties.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1997-10-14/debates/96394ce5-f0db-4448-af50-43a69fbf3c57/LieutenantColonelCharlesHaltMemorandum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum - Hansard - UK ParliamentOctober 14, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 14, 1997</span></span></span>

## The December 1980 investigation

### Why Halt entered the case

Halt became involved after reports from personnel at RAF Woodbridge during the Christmas period of 1980. The earlier events involved security staff investigating unusual lights near Rendlesham Forest, and claims were later made about a physical object, ground impressions, and unusual readings. Halt’s role was different: he organised a second investigation intended to examine the reported site and gather information rather than simply rely on [witness accounts]({{ 'witnesses-1bd40b/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/aug-2011-research-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Research Notes 6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Research Notes 6</span></span></span>

According to Halt’s later memorandum, his team entered the forest equipped with instruments including a Geiger counter to examine the area where earlier witnesses had reported possible landing marks. The investigation produced observations that became central to the case: alleged radiation readings, a bright light seen through the trees, and additional lights in the sky. The value of the episode for researchers is not that it settled the question of what happened, but that it created a contemporaneous record of how a senior military officer interpreted events while they were unfolding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/aug-2011-research-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Research Notes 6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Research Notes 6</span></span></span>

The timing of the investigation and the later written report also became part of the debate. Halt’s memorandum was sent to the UK Ministry of Defence in January 1981, several weeks after the reported events. Supporters argue that the delay reflects normal military reporting procedures and the need to produce a formal account; critics point to the gap as a possible source of memory distortion and reconstruction. The National Archives records that the memo was sent to Defence Secretariat 8 at Whitehall and later became part of the released government file on the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/aug-2011-research-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Research Notes 6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Research Notes 6</span></span></span>

## The memo and recorded observations

### What the “Unexplained Lights” memorandum contains

Halt’s memorandum is the most important official document connected with the Rendlesham investigation. It describes reports of a strange object from earlier witnesses, including a triangular appearance, lights of different colours, movement through the forest, and alleged depressions in the ground. It then records Halt’s own investigation, including observations of unusual lights and the use of measuring equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/ufo-files-national-archives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National ArchivesMay 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2018</span></span></span>

The memorandum’s importance comes from its status as a military document rather than from its ability to prove a particular explanation. It records what personnel reported and what Halt believed he observed, but it does not contain independent confirmation that the reported object was a spacecraft or that the physical effects had an extraordinary cause. This distinction is central to the wider Rendlesham debate: a reliable record of a report is not the same as proof of the reported interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1997-10-14/debates/96394ce5-f0db-4448-af50-43a69fbf3c57/LieutenantColonelCharlesHaltMemorandum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum - Hansard - UK ParliamentOctober 14, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 14, 1997</span></span></span>

### The field recording

Halt’s audio tape is unusual because it captures an investigation in progress rather than a later recollection. The recording contains Halt and other personnel discussing observations, equipment readings, and lights seen during the forest investigation. It is not a recording of the original reported encounter involving the first witnesses; it documents the later investigation led by Halt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ARendelsham.ogg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Rendelsham.ogg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Rendelsham.ogg - Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

For supporters of the case, the tape is significant because Halt appears to react in real time rather than narrating events years later. They argue that his comments show a trained officer confronting something he could not immediately explain. For sceptical analysts, the recording demonstrates uncertainty rather than extraordinary evidence: the observers were attempting to interpret distant lights under difficult night-time conditions, and the tape does not provide measurements or imagery that independently identify the source of those lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufocasebook.com/Halt.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufocasebook.com">[UFO Casebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufocasebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Casebook Colonel Charles Halt Audio Tape Transcription</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Casebook Colonel Charles Halt Audio Tape Transcription</span></span></span>

The recording and memorandum together form the strongest documentary package within the Rendlesham case. They establish that an investigation occurred and that unusual observations were reported, but the documents leave open the crucial question of cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/aug-2011-research-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Research Notes 6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Research Notes 6</span></span></span>

## How officials interpreted the report

### The Ministry of Defence assessment

The UK Ministry of Defence received Halt’s memorandum because RAF Woodbridge was a US Air Force facility on British territory. Government officials reviewed the report through the normal channels used for possible aerial threats. The later official position was that the incident did not represent a defence concern and did not require further action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1997-10-14/debates/96394ce5-f0db-4448-af50-43a69fbf3c57/LieutenantColonelCharlesHaltMemorandum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum - Hansard - UK ParliamentOctober 14, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 14, 1997</span></span></span>

The released files are significant because they show what officials did — and did not — conclude. The records demonstrate that the case was examined and preserved, but they do not reveal an official finding that a craft had landed, that technology had been recovered, or that an extraordinary explanation had been confirmed. The National Archives material includes Halt’s memorandum, Ministry of Defence discussions, and related correspondence, allowing researchers to examine the administrative response directly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/aug-2011-research-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Research Notes 6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Research Notes 6</span></span></span>

### Why the records remain disputed

The controversy around Halt’s investigation comes from the difference between documentation and interpretation. Few researchers dispute that Halt wrote the memo or that the tape exists. The disagreement concerns what those records demonstrate.

Supporters of an unexplained-event interpretation emphasise several features: the involvement of military personnel, Halt’s senior position, the existence of an official memorandum, and the unusual combination of reported lights, environmental observations, and recorded testimony. They argue that the case deserves attention because it is better documented than many UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/ufo-files-national-archives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National ArchivesMay 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2018</span></span></span>

Sceptical explanations focus on the limits of the evidence. Possible explanations proposed by critics include misidentified astronomical objects, the nearby Orford Ness lighthouse, ordinary lights, and difficulties judging distance and movement at night. From this perspective, the records are valuable historical evidence of a confusing incident but do not demonstrate that an unknown craft was involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The most notable encounter involved U.S. Air Force personnel Jim Penniston, John Burroughs, and Charles Halt, who reported strange lights...</span></span></span>

The lasting significance of the Halt investigation is therefore documentary rather than conclusive. His memorandum and recording transformed Rendlesham from a collection of witness accounts into a case with an identifiable official record. They remain the main evidence base for examining what military personnel reported in December 1980, while leaving the ultimate explanation unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/aug-2011-research-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Research Notes 6</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Research Notes 6</span></span></span>
