## Introduction

Charles L. Moody’s 1975 encounter near Alamogordo, New Mexico, became notable because it combined two elements that have shaped many later UFO abduction reports: a close-range sighting of an unusual object and a period of unexplained missing time. According to Moody’s account, he was watching the Perseid meteor shower in the desert on 13 August 1975 when he saw a metallic, disc-shaped object descend nearby, approach his vehicle, and appear to interfere with his ability to leave. He later said he lost roughly an hour and a half of memory before the object departed and his vehicle became usable again. The missing-time episode became the foundation for his later claim that he had been taken aboard the craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.integralworld.net/pdf/Truth.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: integralworld.net">[integralworld.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">integralworld.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 30, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 30, 2006</span></span></span>

The importance of Moody’s account lies less in proving a particular explanation and more in the structure of the report itself. ## The meteor watch and strange craft

### A quiet desert observation becomes a close encounter

Moody’s reported experience began as an ordinary astronomical observation. He had driven into the desert near Alamogordo, close to Holloman Air Force Base, to watch a meteor shower. Case summaries place the sighting at around 1:15–1:20 a.m., when Moody noticed a glowing object descending from the sky. He described it as a dull metallic or glowing disc, approximately 40–50 feet across and around 18–20 feet thick, although different retellings vary slightly in their measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufoac.com/abduction-of-airborne-sergeant-charles-moody.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoac.com">[ufoac.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoac.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abduction of airborne Sergeant Charles L. Moody</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abduction of airborne Sergeant Charles L. Moody</span></span></span>

According to Moody’s testimony, the object first appeared at a distance before dropping to a low altitude and moving towards him. He reported that it seemed to wobble as it descended, then stabilised and approached slowly. He described seeing a rectangular illuminated area on the object and shadows inside that resembled human figures. The account recorded by the Aerial Phenomena Research Organization (APRO) includes Moody’s description of a high-pitched sound and a window-like opening on the craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/A.P.R.O.%20Bulletin/apro_june_1976.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">apro june 1976</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The A.P.R.O. The sighting became one of the strongest parts of the case narrative because Moody provided a sequence rather than a single observation: descent, approach, attempted escape, vehicle failure, sensory effects, and loss of awareness. For investigators interested in witness testimony, this type of structured account can be useful because it allows individual claims to be examined separately rather than treating the entire event as one unexplained incident. However, the detail of a report does not by itself establish whether the interpretation of the event is correct.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/A.P.R.O.%20Bulletin/apro_june_1976.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">apro june 1976</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The A.P.R.O. ## The vehicle failure and missing time

### The unexplained gap after the encounter

One of the defining features of Moody’s story was his claim that his car failed when he attempted to leave. He said he entered the vehicle and tried to start the engine, but it would not operate. During this period, he reported hearing a high-pitched humming or buzzing noise from the object. He then described a sensation of numbness spreading through his body, after which his memory became incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufoac.com/abduction-of-airborne-sergeant-charles-moody.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoac.com">[ufoac.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoac.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abduction of airborne Sergeant Charles L. Moody</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abduction of airborne Sergeant Charles L. Moody</span></span></span>

Moody’s next clear memory was of the object leaving. He said that after it rose into the sky and disappeared, his car started again. When he returned home, he realised that much more time had passed than he could account for. Case records associated with APRO describe the missing interval as approximately one and a half hours, while later summaries sometimes give slightly different estimates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/HUMCAT/HUMCAT_Index_1975.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">HUMCAT Index 1975</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO Studies1975 Addenda...</span></span></span>

In UFO literature, “missing time” became a recurring feature of alleged abduction reports, referring to a period when a witness cannot remember events between two known points. In Moody’s case, the missing period was not initially filled with a detailed narrative; his later memories developed after the encounter. This distinction is important because the original sighting account and the later reconstruction of events are separate parts of the case history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.integralworld.net/pdf/Truth.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: integralworld.net">[integralworld.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">integralworld.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 30, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 30, 2006</span></span></span>

The missing-time claim is also where interpretations of the case begin to diverge. Those who regard Moody’s account as significant argue that the combination of a specific time gap, reported physical sensations, and subsequent memories makes the event difficult to dismiss as a simple misidentification. More cautious investigators note that a memory gap, especially during a stressful or unusual experience, does not automatically identify what caused it. A missing interval is evidence that something was not remembered; it is not, by itself, evidence of a particular explanation.

## Moody’s reported memories afterward

### From uncertainty to an abduction narrative

After the encounter, Moody reported physical effects that became part of the case record. Sources describing the incident state that he later experienced lower-back pain and a rash, after which he attempted to recover memories of the missing period. Accounts vary on the exact process, but UFO researchers have generally described his later recollections as emerging over the following weeks rather than being available immediately after the sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufoac.com/abduction-of-airborne-sergeant-charles-moody.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoac.com">[ufoac.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoac.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abduction of airborne Sergeant Charles L. Moody</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abduction of airborne Sergeant Charles L. Moody</span></span></span>

Moody eventually reported that the missing time involved an encounter with beings associated with the object. In later versions of the story, he described figures approaching his vehicle, a struggle, paralysis or loss of control, and an examination aboard the craft. These details placed the case within the wider 1970s wave of alleged abduction reports, which often included themes of medical examination, communication with occupants, and incomplete memories later reconstructed by the witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.llresearch.org/books/secrets_of_the_ufo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.llresearch.org">[LL Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.llresearch.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">LL Research Secrets of the UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LL Research Secrets of the UFO</span></span></span>

The transition from the initial sighting to the later abduction account is the central interpretive issue in the Moody case. The first part of the report concerns an observed object and a claimed time gap; the second concerns memories of events during that gap. Supporters see the later memories as an explanation of the missing period. Sceptics focus on the fact that later recollections are more difficult to independently verify and may be influenced by expectations, questioning, or the cultural setting of UFO reports during the 1970s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/A.P.R.O.%20Bulletin/apro_june_1976.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">apro june 1976</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The A.P.R.O. ## Why Moody’s account remains disputed

The Moody sighting remains influential in UFO history because it combines a trained military witness, a close-range observation, and a classic missing-time claim. The case was investigated within UFO research circles, including organisations such as APRO, but no publicly available physical evidence has confirmed that an extraterrestrial craft or abduction occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/nmexico/newmexicosightings.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">newmexicosightingsSeptember 3, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 3, 2005</span></span></span>

The strongest argument in favour of taking the report seriously is that Moody provided a detailed, consistent narrative and did not present the experience simply as an abstract belief. His account included specific environmental details, a sequence of actions, and reported after-effects. The strongest criticism is that the case still depends primarily on one witness’s interpretation of events that were not independently observed or physically documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/A.P.R.O.%20Bulletin/apro_june_1976.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">apro june 1976</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The A.P.R.O. For that reason, Moody’s desert encounter remains best understood as a significant historical UFO report rather than a solved event. The sighting and missing-time account illustrate the central challenge of close-encounter cases: a detailed testimony can be genuinely important for understanding human experience while still leaving open the question of what actually happened in the desert near Alamogordo.
