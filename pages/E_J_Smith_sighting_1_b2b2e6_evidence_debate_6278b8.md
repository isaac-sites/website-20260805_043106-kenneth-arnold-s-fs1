## Introduction

The Flight 105 sighting remains debated because the available evidence is unusually strong for an unidentified observation but weak for proving what the objects actually were. On 4 July 1947, United Air Lines Captain Emil J. Smith, First Officer Ralph Stevens, and stewardess Marty Morrow reported seeing several disc-like objects after take-off from Boise, Idaho. Their professional aviation experience, multiple-witness testimony, and extended observation period made the case more influential than many contemporary reports. However, the lack of physical evidence, radar confirmation, photographs, or a surviving technical record means that the case remains an identification problem rather than a confirmed explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. The central question surrounding Flight 105 is therefore not whether the crew saw something unusual, but whether the unusual appearance can be explained by ordinary causes. Investigators have considered aircraft, balloons, birds, atmospheric effects, optical illusion, and the influence of the intense 1947 “flying saucer” publicity. Supporters of the case argue that the witnesses were capable observers who rejected conventional explanations after examining the objects closely. Critics argue that even reliable witnesses can misidentify distant objects under unusual lighting and perception conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

## What Evidence Exists for Flight 105

### Reported observations and missing records

The strongest evidence for Flight 105 comes from the consistency and quality of the witness reports. Stevens first noticed objects that appeared to be another aircraft and reportedly used the DC-3’s landing lights in an attempt to signal them. After closer observation, Smith and Stevens concluded they could not identify normal aircraft features such as wings or tails. They described multiple objects, including a larger object among a group, moving in formation and later disappearing before another group was seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

The sighting also included an independent witness inside the aircraft. Smith called stewardess Marty Morrow to the cockpit, where she reportedly observed the same objects. This gave the case a level of corroboration that was unusual among early UFO reports. The observers were not simply repeating a rumour or interpreting a single brief flash of light; they were attempting to identify objects during an extended aviation observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

However, the evidence record has important gaps. No photographs were taken, no recovered material was associated with the sighting, and no independent aircraft, radar, or ground observer confirmed the objects. The crew attempted to communicate the observation through aviation channels, but the available record does not provide external confirmation of the objects’ identity or behaviour. This absence of physical and instrumental evidence is the main limitation when assessing the case today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

The surviving documentation is also shaped by later investigation rather than a complete original evidence package. UFO researchers preserved accounts from Smith and other sources, while later official UFO programmes assessed similar reports using broader categories of explanation. This means the case is largely evaluated through testimony and retrospective analysis rather than a complete contemporaneous investigation file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. ## Which Conventional Explanations Were Considered?

The main conventional explanations for Flight 105 focus on misidentification rather than deliberate error or fabrication. The Air Force’s broader evaluation of UFO reports in the early investigation period emphasised that many sightings could result from ordinary objects viewed under unusual conditions, including aircraft, balloons, birds, or perceptual effects. The Flight 105 case has often been discussed within this framework because the observation occurred during twilight, a period when judging distance, size, and movement can be difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

One possibility is that the crew observed conventional aircraft at a distance. A pilot’s ability to recognise aircraft is normally a strength, but distant objects viewed against the sky can lose identifying features. Without instruments such as radar, even trained observers may have difficulty estimating speed, size, and range. A small object nearby can appear large, while a distant aircraft can appear unusual if lighting conditions obscure its structure.

Another possibility is that the objects were balloons or other airborne objects. The late 1940s saw increasing public attention to unusual objects in the sky, and official investigators considered balloons among common sources of mistaken UFO reports. The challenge for this explanation is that Smith and Stevens believed the objects displayed organised movement and formation changes inconsistent with what they expected from balloons. Whether those impressions accurately reflected the objects’ behaviour cannot now be independently verified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

A further explanation is perceptual influence. The sighting occurred shortly after Kenneth Arnold’s widely reported June 1947 observation, which helped create the “flying saucer” phenomenon and generated a surge of reports. Investigators later suggested that public awareness of saucer reports could influence how ambiguous objects were interpreted. Supporters of Flight 105 respond that Smith had initially been sceptical of saucer reports and that his aviation background made him less likely to accept an unusual explanation without careful observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. ## Why the Case Remains Unidentified

Flight 105 remains unresolved because the evidence sits between two difficult positions. The witnesses were credible enough that dismissing the entire event as a simple invention is difficult, but credibility alone cannot determine the nature of an observed object. A trained pilot can accurately report that an object was present while still being unable to identify it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. The case is also limited by what investigators did not obtain. A modern investigation would ideally combine eyewitness accounts with radar data, photographs, flight records, atmospheric information, and independent observations. Flight 105 lacks most of these sources, leaving researchers dependent on human perception and memory. This creates uncertainty even when the observers themselves appear reliable.

Supporters of an unidentified interpretation point to the combination of factors: several witnesses, aviation expertise, a reported duration of around ten minutes or more, and the claim that the objects did not resemble known aircraft.
