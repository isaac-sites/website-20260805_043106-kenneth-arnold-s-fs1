## Introduction

The physical evidence in the Lady case is the central reason the 1967 Colorado horse death became one of the most discussed [animal mutilation]({{ 'mutilation-legacy/' | relative_url }}) reports. The surviving record is not a preserved forensic file with extensive laboratory documentation; instead, it consists mainly of witness descriptions, photographs, newspaper accounts, later investigations, and a veterinary examination conducted after the carcass had already deteriorated. The unusual appearance of the remains — especially the reported removal of tissue from the head and neck, the lack of visible blood, and claims of unusually clean cuts — drove speculation that the death had no ordinary explanation. Later investigators, however, found that the available evidence did not require an extraordinary cause and identified several conventional possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[history.denverlibrary.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs and a Horse Called Snippy &#124; Denver Public Library Special Collections and ArchivesOctober 20, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2020</span></span></span>

The evidence trail around Lady therefore presents two competing interpretations. Supporters of an unexplained-event interpretation focus on the reported condition of the carcass and the apparent precision of the injuries. Skeptical and official reviews focus on the weaknesses of the physical record: delayed examination, disturbance of the site, lack of preserved samples, and the ability of scavengers and natural decomposition to alter a carcass after death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>

## The reported condition of the remains

When Lady was found near Alamosa, Colorado, in September 1967, witnesses described a scene that appeared highly unusual. The horse’s head and neck were reported as having lost most of their skin and flesh, with exposed bone described as unusually clean or bleached in appearance. Accounts also stated that the damage appeared sharply defined, leading some observers to describe the injuries as “surgical” or unusually precise. These descriptions became the foundation of later claims that the animal had been subjected to a deliberate and unexplained procedure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilation_of_%22Snippy%22_the_horse" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span></span></span>

Several additional observations contributed to the mystery surrounding the carcass. Witnesses reported that there was little or no visible blood around the body, that nearby vegetation appeared disturbed, and that there were marks or depressions in the area that some interpreted as possible landing or equipment traces. Other reported details included an unusual smell around the remains and claims that exposed bones changed colour over time. These observations were important in shaping public interest, but most were based on personal testimony rather than controlled forensic documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[history.denverlibrary.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs and a Horse Called Snippy &#124; Denver Public Library Special Collections and ArchivesOctober 20, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2020</span></span></span>

The condition of the remains also created a major interpretive problem: the carcass was not examined immediately under controlled conditions. By the time investigators became involved, the scene had attracted considerable attention and visitors, reducing the value of any potential trace evidence. The later Condon Committee investigation noted that the area around the carcass had been disturbed by numerous visitors, making reconstruction of the original scene difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>

## Veterinary examination and findings

The most significant formal examination of Lady’s remains came through the investigation connected with the University of Colorado’s Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects, commonly known as the Condon Committee. Investigators Wadsworth Ayer and colleagues reviewed the case after it had gained public attention because of claimed links between the horse’s death and UFO reports in the area. A veterinarian examined the remains, but the examination was limited because the carcass was already too old for a complete post-mortem analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>

The veterinary findings challenged the idea that the condition of the remains necessarily indicated an unknown cause. The examination found indications of a serious infection in one hind leg that could have weakened or killed the horse. Investigators also reported evidence consistent with a cut to the neck, possibly made by someone attempting to deal with an injured or dying animal. Once an opening existed, scavenging birds and other animals could have removed exposed tissue from the head and neck area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>

The [Condon investigation]({{ 'official-inquiry/' | relative_url }}) also addressed claims about missing internal organs and the unusual appearance of the carcass. Its conclusion was that the available evidence did not support the claim that Lady’s death resulted from an abnormal or unknown cause. Instead, investigators considered ordinary biological processes, including scavenger activity after death, sufficient to explain much of the reported condition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>

This interpretation remains disputed among some UFO researchers because it relies heavily on evidence gathered after the scene had already changed. The lack of an immediate, complete forensic examination means that neither the original injuries nor the exact sequence of events can now be reconstructed with certainty. The disagreement is therefore not only about what happened to Lady, but also about how much confidence should be placed in observations made after the fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>

## Limits of the surviving evidence

The strongest physical limitation in the Lady case is the absence of a complete forensic record. There was no documented chain of evidence comparable to a modern criminal investigation, no preserved tissue archive available for later testing, and no immediate scientific examination of the entire scene before public attention altered the location. This makes many of the most frequently repeated details difficult to verify independently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>

The case also illustrates how unusual appearances can be interpreted differently depending on the observer’s assumptions. A rancher familiar with livestock deaths may see a carcass that does not resemble normal experience, while a veterinary investigator may consider delayed decomposition, scavenging, and injury patterns that occur after death. Both perspectives depend on the same physical object, but they emphasise different parts of the evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>

The reported absence of blood and the clean appearance of the exposed areas remain among the most frequently cited features of the case. However, without immediate examination, it is difficult to determine whether those features resulted from the original injury, environmental exposure, scavenging, or later changes to the remains. The evidence can demonstrate that Lady’s carcass appeared unusual to those who found it, but it does not provide a definitive mechanism for how the damage occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/condon/case32.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - Condon Report, Case 32: Horse Death...</span></span></span>
