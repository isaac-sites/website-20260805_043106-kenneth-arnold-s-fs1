## What happened at Loch Raven Dam in 1958?

### The reported encounter

According to the case file, Small and Cohen were travelling by car near Loch Raven Dam at around 10:30 p.m. on 26 October 1958. They reported seeing a luminous, flat, egg-shaped object positioned above a bridge. The Project Blue Book documentation described the object as approximately 75–100 feet long, glowing, and hovering before ascending vertically. The report recorded that the object was visible for roughly a minute before disappearing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1958-10-7201675-NOfBaltimore-Md.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1958 10 7201675 NOfBaltimore Md</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons1958-10-7201675-NOfBaltimore-MdJanuary 6, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 6, 2015</span></span></span>

The most unusual part of the account was the claimed interaction with the vehicle. The witnesses said that as they moved closer, their car suddenly stopped functioning, with the failure described as if the ignition had been switched off. They were unable to restart it while the object was present. After the object flashed brightly and departed, they reported that the vehicle returned to normal operation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/581026lochravendam_report2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportOctober 26, 1958...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 26, 1958</span></span></span>

The witnesses also described physical sensations during the event. They reported feeling heat from the object and later claimed that their skin was reddened or irritated on the side facing the light. Later retellings of the case describe the men seeking medical attention, although the available case summaries do not provide evidence of lasting injury or medical findings that independently confirmed radiation, burns, or another physical effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/lochravendam.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Loch Raven Dam case, 1958...</span></span></span>

## How Project Blue Book investigated the case

The Loch Raven report entered the Air Force’s Project Blue Book system as case number 6148. Blue Book was the US Air Force investigation into UFO reports between 1952 and 1969, created to evaluate whether sightings represented a national security concern or evidence of unknown technology. The Loch Raven incident appeared among the programme’s unresolved cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://isaackoi.com/ufo-history/ufo/19581026-loch-raven-dam-incident/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: isaackoi.com">[Isaac Koi Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">isaackoi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">19581026 loch raven dam incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Isaac Koi Archive1958.1026 Loch Raven Dam incident &#124; Isaac Koi Archive...</span></span></span>

The Air Force investigation recorded the main elements of the witnesses’ story: a hovering egg-shaped object, a vehicle malfunction, a bright flash, heat sensations, and a rapid vertical departure. The surviving report did not identify a conventional cause and did not conclude that the object was extraterrestrial. Instead, it remained an “unknown” case within the Blue Book classification system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/lochravendam.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Loch Raven Dam case, 1958...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. An “unknown” classification meant investigators had not been able to match the report to an available explanation using the information they had. It did not represent official confirmation of an alien craft or proof that the reported object violated known physics. Project Blue Book’s unresolved cases included reports where available evidence was insufficient for a confident identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/bluebook/bluelist.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Complete List of Project Blue Book&#x27;s Unsolved Cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Complete List of Project Blue Book&#x27;s Unsolved Cases...</span></span></span>

## What evidence supports the UFO interpretation?

Supporters of the UFO interpretation point mainly to the combination of details reported by the two witnesses:

* **Two-person testimony:** The event was not a single-observer sighting. Small and Cohen provided a broadly consistent account of the object, the vehicle failure, and the departure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/581026lochravendam_report2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportOctober 26, 1958...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 26, 1958</span></span></span>
* **Reported electromagnetic effects:** The alleged failure of the car’s electrical system is one of the features that has made the case especially interesting among UFO researchers. Similar claims appear in other historical UFO reports, although they are difficult to verify without instruments or physical records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/581026lochravendam_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report...</span></span></span>
* **Contemporary investigation:** The event was reported close enough in time to receive official attention rather than existing only as a later anecdote. The Project Blue Book paperwork provides a contemporary record of the claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1958-10-7201675-NOfBaltimore-Md.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1958 10 7201675 NOfBaltimore Md</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons1958-10-7201675-NOfBaltimore-MdJanuary 6, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 6, 2015</span></span></span>

For UFO researchers, the combination of a close-range sighting, alleged effects on machinery, and a government investigation makes Loch Raven one of the stronger cases from the 1950s. However, “stronger” in this context refers to the quality of the report compared with many anonymous or poorly documented sightings, not to proof of an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/581026lochravendam_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report...</span></span></span>

## What remains uncertain about the incident?

The largest weakness in the case is the lack of independent evidence. There are no known photographs, instrument readings, radar confirmations, recovered materials, or environmental measurements that could test the claims. The investigation depended heavily on reconstructing what the two witnesses experienced and remembered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1958-10-7201675-NOfBaltimore-Md.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1958 10 7201675 NOfBaltimore Md</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons1958-10-7201675-NOfBaltimore-MdJanuary 6, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 6, 2015</span></span></span>

The car failure is also difficult to evaluate. A vehicle stopping briefly at night could have many ordinary causes, including electrical faults, ignition problems, battery issues, or driver error. The available case record does not demonstrate that the failure occurred through an external force rather than a coincidental mechanical problem. Similarly, the reported heat and skin effects are witness statements rather than medically documented evidence of a specific cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/581026lochravendam_report2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportOctober 26, 1958...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 26, 1958</span></span></span>

Another limitation is that the exact physical circumstances are not fully recoverable. Later accounts often repeat estimates of the object’s size, height, distance, and behaviour, but those measurements were based on human observation during a short, stressful nighttime event. Such estimates can be useful as testimony while still carrying uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/lochravendam.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Loch Raven Dam case, 1958...</span></span></span>

## Competing explanations and the unresolved status

A conventional explanation has not been firmly established, which is why the case remains popular in UFO literature. At the same time, the absence of an identified explanation does not eliminate ordinary possibilities. Researchers examining historical UFO reports often distinguish between “unexplained” and “unexplainable”: a report may remain unresolved because the available information is incomplete rather than because the event requires a new physical explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/bluebook/bluelist.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Complete List of Project Blue Book&#x27;s Unsolved Cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Complete List of Project Blue Book&#x27;s Unsolved Cases...</span></span></span>

Possible non-extraordinary interpretations include misidentification of a conventional light source or aircraft, a coincidental vehicle malfunction, or a combination of separate events that were connected in the witnesses’ memory. The difficulty is that the short duration of the sighting and the absence of independent observations leave little evidence with which to test these possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/lochravendam.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: The Loch Raven Dam case, 1958...</span></span></span>

The extraterrestrial interpretation also faces the same evidence problem. The report describes an unusual object, but the available documentation does not provide proof of its origin, technology, or physical nature. ## Why the Loch Raven Dam case remains famous

The Loch Raven Dam incident has lasted in UFO history because it combines several elements that attract continued attention: a named location, identifiable witnesses, a contemporary government file, an alleged interaction with a vehicle, and a conclusion that stopped at “unknown.” It represents the type of case that helped shape public interest in UFO investigations during the 1950s, when reports of unusual aircraft and unexplained aerial phenomena were receiving increasing official attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://isaackoi.com/ufo-history/ufo/19581026-loch-raven-dam-incident/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: isaackoi.com">[Isaac Koi Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">isaackoi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">19581026 loch raven dam incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Isaac Koi Archive1958.1026 Loch Raven Dam incident &#124; Isaac Koi Archive...</span></span></span>

Ultimately, the case is best understood as a well-documented historical UFO report rather than a proven encounter with an extraterrestrial vehicle. The available evidence supports the fact that Small and Cohen reported a strange experience near Loch Raven Dam; it does not independently establish what the object was.
