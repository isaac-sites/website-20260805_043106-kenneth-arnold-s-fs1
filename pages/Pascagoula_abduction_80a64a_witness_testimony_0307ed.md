## Introduction

The testimony of Charles Hickson and Calvin Parker remains the central evidence in the [Pascagoula abduction]({{ 'pascagoula-abduction-1973/' | relative_url }}) case because almost every later debate depends on a single question: were the two men accurately reporting an extraordinary event, or were they sincerely mistaken about what happened? On 11 October 1973, the two Mississippi shipyard workers said they encountered a strange craft while fishing on the Pascagoula River and were taken aboard by unusual beings. Their immediate report to police, the recording made while they believed they were unobserved, and their long-term consistency became key reasons many investigators considered them credible. At the same time, critics have argued that credibility as witnesses does not prove the interpretation they placed on their experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://countryroadsmagazine.com/art-and-culture/people-places/the-pascagoula-abduction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: countryroadsmagazine.com">[Country Roads Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">countryroadsmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Country Roads MagazineAn Interview with Calvin Parker: The Pascagoula Alien Abduction - Country Roads MagazineSeptember 21, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 21, 2021</span></span></span>

The lasting importance of Hickson and Parker’s accounts is therefore not simply whether they were believed. Their testimony provides a useful case study in how investigators evaluate unusual reports: separating honesty from accuracy, early statements from later memories, and personal experience from explanations about what caused it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/10581338/files/Reliability_I-09_Nickell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Reliability of UFO Witness Testimony</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Reliability of UFO Witness TestimonyJune 15, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2025</span></span></span>

## The night of the reported encounter

Hickson, aged 42, and Parker, aged 19, were fishing near the Pascagoula River when they reported seeing a glowing object and encountering three strange figures. Their account described a craft hovering near the riverbank, followed by beings that appeared to move without walking and that allegedly immobilised the two men before examining them. Parker later said he lost awareness during part of the event, while Hickson described remaining conscious throughout more of the experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.co.uk/articles/the-pascagoula-abduction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.co.uk">[Sky HISTORY TV channel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on history.co.uk.</span></span></span>

The men’s behaviour immediately afterwards became one of the strongest arguments used by supporters of the case. Rather than seeking publicity, they went to the Jackson County Sheriff’s Office to report what they believed had happened. Sheriff’s personnel initially treated the story with scepticism, but the men continued to give the same basic account during questioning. A key detail was that officers left them alone in an interview room while a recorder continued running. The resulting private conversation has often been presented as evidence that they were genuinely frightened rather than performing a prepared story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lib.usm.edu/spcol/exhibitions/item_of_the_month/march_2014.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.usm.edu">[University of Southern Mississippi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.usm.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on usm.edu.</span></span></span>

The recording is important because it captures the witnesses close to the time of the alleged event, before extensive media attention and later interviews could influence the public version of their story. However, it only demonstrates their emotional state and belief in the experience; it does not independently establish that the cause was an extraterrestrial encounter. This distinction has remained at the centre of the credibility debate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lib.usm.edu/spcol/exhibitions/item_of_the_month/march_2014.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.usm.edu">[University of Southern Mississippi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.usm.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on usm.edu.</span></span></span>

## Hickson and Parker's changing accounts

### The areas of consistency

One reason the Pascagoula case attracted serious attention was that Hickson and Parker maintained several core details over decades of interviews. These included the location of the encounter, the presence of a strange object, their inability to move, the appearance of the beings, and the claim that they were subjected to an examination before being returned to the riverbank. Contemporary investigators such as J. Allen Hynek and James Harder interviewed the men separately and regarded their accounts as showing signs of sincerity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascagoula_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pascagoula incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pascagoula incident</span></span></span>

The men’s stories also contained distinctive details rather than only general claims. Hickson described the beings as unusual, almost mechanical-looking figures rather than the more familiar “grey alien” image that later became popular in UFO culture. The University of Southern Mississippi’s oral history archive preserves a 1979 interview in which Hickson again described the encounter and its impact on his life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lib.usm.edu/spcol/exhibitions/item_of_the_month/iotm_june_2022.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.usm.edu">[University of Southern Mississippi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.usm.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on usm.edu.</span></span></span>

For supporters, these repeated details suggest that the men were recalling a genuine experience. They argue that maintaining a complex story under repeated questioning would have been difficult if it had been deliberately invented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://countryroadsmagazine.com/art-and-culture/people-places/the-pascagoula-abduction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: countryroadsmagazine.com">[Country Roads Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">countryroadsmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Country Roads MagazineAn Interview with Calvin Parker: The Pascagoula Alien Abduction - Country Roads MagazineSeptember 21, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 21, 2021</span></span></span>

### Where critics found weaknesses

Sceptical investigators have focused on differences between early statements and later retellings. One major issue concerns Parker’s memory of the event. Early accounts included his statement that he had lost consciousness or awareness during part of the encounter, meaning much of his description depended on what he later recalled or what Hickson reported. Critics argue this creates uncertainty about how much of the shared account came from direct experience versus reconstruction after the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascagoula_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pascagoula incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pascagoula incident</span></span></span>

Other critics have pointed to changes in Hickson’s descriptions of the creatures and the encounter over time. Joe Nickell, a researcher associated with the Committee for Skeptical Inquiry, argued that the case could be explained through psychological factors rather than a physical abduction. His analysis proposed that the experience may have involved a hypnagogic state — a transitional condition between waking and sleeping in which vivid hallucination-like experiences can occur.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/10581338/files/Reliability_I-09_Nickell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Reliability of UFO Witness Testimony</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Reliability of UFO Witness TestimonyJune 15, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2025</span></span></span>

These criticisms do not necessarily require that Hickson and Parker were deliberately fabricating the story. A central point in [the debate]({{ 'the-debate-9bcaa4/' | relative_url }}) is that sincere witnesses can still misinterpret unusual experiences, especially when those experiences involve fear, confusion, altered awareness, or incomplete memories.

## Credibility, sincerity, and uncertainty

The strongest argument in favour of Hickson and Parker has always been their apparent sincerity. Several investigators who met them shortly after the incident believed they were genuinely affected by what they described. The private recording, their willingness to report the event to police, and their continued public accounts all contributed to the view that they were not simply inventing a story for attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lib.usm.edu/spcol/exhibitions/item_of_the_month/march_2014.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.usm.edu">[University of Southern Mississippi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.usm.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on usm.edu.</span></span></span>

However, witness credibility has limits. In investigations of unusual events, researchers generally distinguish between three questions:

1. **Did the witnesses believe their account?**

Evidence from their behaviour suggests that Hickson and Parker believed something frightening had happened.
2. **Did they accurately perceive what happened?**

This is harder to establish because perception and memory can be affected by stress, fear, and unusual states of consciousness.
3. **Was their interpretation correct?**

The conclusion that the event involved extraterrestrial beings requires evidence beyond testimony alone.

This separation explains why the Pascagoula witnesses continue to divide opinion. Supporters see two ordinary men whose immediate reactions and long-term consistency make deception unlikely. Sceptics see a case where honest testimony may still have resulted from a mistaken interpretation of an unusual experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zenodo.org/records/10581338/files/Reliability_I-09_Nickell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zenodo.org">[Zenodo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zenodo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Reliability of UFO Witness Testimony</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Reliability of UFO Witness TestimonyJune 15, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why the witnesses remain central to the case debate

Hickson and Parker matter because their testimony contains both strengths and unresolved problems. Few alleged UFO encounters have such a detailed record of early reporting, private conversation, later interviews, and preserved personal testimony. The University of Southern Mississippi archive, for example, provides researchers with a historical record of Hickson discussing the event and its aftermath years later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lib.usm.edu/spcol/exhibitions/item_of_the_month/iotm_june_2022.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.usm.edu">[University of Southern Mississippi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.usm.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on usm.edu.</span></span></span>

At the same time, the case lacks independent physical evidence that could confirm the men’s interpretation of events. There is no verified craft material, biological evidence, or outside observation that directly supports the abduction claim. The main evidence remains the witnesses themselves: their memories, their reactions, and the consistency of their accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nicap.org/731011pascagoula_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report...</span></span></span>

The enduring significance of Hickson and Parker’s testimony is therefore not that it conclusively proves or disproves an abduction. Instead, the case remains a lasting example of the difficulty of evaluating extraordinary eyewitness claims: people may be sincere, consistent, and deeply affected by what they experienced, while the ultimate explanation for that experience remains uncertain.
