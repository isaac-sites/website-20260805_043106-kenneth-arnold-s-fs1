## Introduction

The orb object recorded by a US MQ-9 drone over the Middle East in 2022 remains unidentified, but the lack of an identification does not mean that the object demonstrated extraordinary capabilities. The most plausible explanations remain within the range of ordinary airborne objects and sensor-related effects: a balloon or other lightweight object, debris, a distant aircraft viewed under unusual conditions, or an image affected by the limits of the drone’s sensing system. The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) assessed that the object was real and did not show anomalous behaviour, but it could not determine exactly what it was because the available information was insufficient for a firm attribution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/Multimedia/videoid/880273/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War Multimedia &#124; U.S. Department of War</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarMultimedia &#124; U.S. Department of WarJuly 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 12, 2022</span></span></span>(https://www.war.gov/Multimedia/videoid/880273/)

## Ordinary objects that appear unusual

### A balloon remains a leading conventional possibility

One of the most frequently considered explanations for orb-shaped UAP reports is a balloon or balloon-like object. Balloons can appear metallic or spherical because many scientific, commercial, and military balloons use reflective materials or have surfaces that catch sunlight. At long distances, their shape can be difficult to judge, especially when viewed through electro-optical or infrared sensors rather than by the naked eye. AARO specifically lists commercial and scientific balloons among common causes of UAP reports because they can lack obvious identifying features and appear unusual from certain angles or under certain conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

For the Middle East orb, a balloon explanation would fit several aspects of the observation: the apparent smooth shape, lack of visible propulsion, and the absence of clearly unusual manoeuvres. A balloon does not need to accelerate or change direction dramatically to create a confusing image; a combination of wind, camera movement, and limited depth information can make a slow-moving object appear more mysterious than it is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

However, a balloon has not been confirmed as the answer for this specific case. AARO’s unresolved classification means analysts did not have enough evidence to match the object confidently to a known balloon, aircraft, or other source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

### Aircraft viewed from an unfamiliar angle

A second possibility is a conventional aircraft or drone appearing unusual because of viewing geometry. Aircraft are often easiest to identify when their wings, fuselage, exhaust, or navigation lights are visible. A distant aircraft viewed head-on, from below, or through a sensor operating outside normal human vision can lose those familiar characteristics and appear as a simple bright or reflective shape. AARO identifies commercial and military aircraft as common sources of misidentified UAP reports for exactly this reason.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

The orb video does not show clear features that would allow analysts to classify the object as an aircraft. That absence is part of why it remains unresolved. But the lack of visible aircraft features is not the same as evidence that no conventional aircraft explanation exists; it may simply reflect the limits of the available image.

### Debris and airborne clutter

Small pieces of airborne debris can also produce unusual-looking sensor returns. Lightweight materials such as plastic film, reflective fragments, or other windborne objects may move unpredictably and have surfaces that strongly reflect light or infrared energy. AARO notes that airborne clutter, including windborne debris and other small objects, can appear anomalous because sensors may capture movement without enough context to identify the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

This explanation is less specific than the balloon or aircraft possibilities because the video alone does not reveal the object’s construction. It remains part of the wider category of ordinary objects that become difficult to classify when recorded from a single viewpoint.

## Sensor and perspective effects

### Why a small object can look like a large mystery

A major challenge in analysing the orb is that the video does not provide an easy way to judge the object’s true size or distance. A small nearby object and a larger distant object can occupy a similar number of pixels in a camera image. Without reliable range information, estimates of size, speed, and manoeuvrability can become highly uncertain.

Human perception also relies on depth cues such as perspective, relative motion, and background references. Research on visual perception shows that motion and perspective cues strongly influence judgments of size and distance, meaning an object’s apparent scale can differ significantly from its actual dimensions when those cues are limited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17076062/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects of motion parallax and perspective cues on perceived size and distance - PubMed...</span></span></span>

This matters for UAP videos because apparent speed is often calculated from how quickly an object crosses the camera frame. A nearby object drifting slowly can appear to move quickly across the image, while a distant object travelling faster can appear almost stationary. A camera operator’s tracking movement can add further confusion.

### Infrared imagery can remove identifying details

The Middle East object was captured by a military sensor system rather than a standard consumer camera. Infrared and other specialised imaging systems are valuable for surveillance but can also produce ambiguous imagery. They may show heat contrast or reflected energy without providing the colour, texture, or shape information that a human observer would normally use to identify an object.

AARO has repeatedly highlighted limited sensor information as a major reason many UAP cases remain unresolved. In its explanation of UAP investigations, the office notes that many objects are classified as unidentified because sensors did not collect enough information for positive identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

A useful comparison is another AARO case involving MQ-9 imagery, where additional footage, longer focal length imagery, and flight data allowed investigators to identify an object as a commercial aircraft and attribute an apparent trailing effect to a sensor artefact. That case demonstrates how additional context can transform an unusual-looking recording into an ordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

## Why no explanation is confirmed

The unresolved status of the Middle East orb is mainly a data problem rather than evidence that conventional explanations have failed. AARO’s public description of the case states that the object did not display anomalous behaviour, but remained unidentified because of limited available information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/Multimedia/videoid/880273/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War Multimedia &#124; U.S. Department of War</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarMultimedia &#124; U.S. Department of WarJuly 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 12, 2022</span></span></span>(https://www.war.gov/Multimedia/videoid/880273/)

Several factors prevent a final identification:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* **No complete three-dimensional track:** A single camera view makes it difficult to calculate exact distance, speed, and acceleration.
* **Limited supporting data:** Additional radar, satellite, weather, or flight information could potentially narrow the possibilities.
* **Ambiguous appearance:** A smooth orb shape is not unique to one type of object and can result from distance, lighting, or sensor limitations.
* **Short observation window:** Brief recordings provide fewer opportunities to detect patterns that reveal an object’s identity.

</div>

The most important distinction is between “unidentified” and “unexplainable”. The Middle East orb is unidentified because investigators have not matched it to a specific object. It is not considered confirmed evidence of technology beyond known capabilities. AARO’s broader approach is to resolve cases when enough information becomes available, while recognising that some observations remain open because the original data cannot answer every question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

For this case, the evidence currently supports a cautious conclusion: an ordinary object or sensor-related explanation remains entirely possible, but the publicly available recording does not contain enough information to prove which one is correct. The unresolved nature of the orb reflects the limits of incomplete observation rather than a demonstrated anomaly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/Multimedia/videoid/880273/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War Multimedia &#124; U.S. Department of War</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarMultimedia &#124; U.S. Department of WarJuly 12, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 12, 2022</span></span></span>(https://www.war.gov/Multimedia/videoid/880273/)
