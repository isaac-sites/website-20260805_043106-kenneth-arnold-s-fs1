## Introduction

The police witness accounts are the central human record of the [Red Bluff sighting]({{ 'red-bluff-sighting-1960/' | relative_url }}) of 1960. On the night of 13 August 1960, California Highway Patrol officers Charles A. Carson and Stanley E. Scott reported a prolonged encounter with an unusual illuminated object while travelling on Hoag Road near Corning, California. Their account became influential because it came from trained law-enforcement observers who initially believed they were seeing an aircraft accident rather than an unexplained phenomenon. The officers’ report described a low-flying object, sudden changes in movement, unusual lights, and later attempts to follow it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO...</span></span></span>

The value of the Carson–Scott testimony lies less in proving a specific explanation and more in showing why the case remained debated. Their account provides a detailed timeline of what two witnesses said they saw, while later disagreements over radar confirmation, interpretation, and possible conventional explanations left the event unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO Case Directory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO Case Directory...</span></span></span>

## The Hoag Road encounter

### The first sighting: a suspected aircraft emergency

According to the officers’ report, Carson and Scott were travelling along Hoag Road east of Corning while looking for a speeding motorcyclist at around 11:50 p.m. Pacific time. They noticed an object ahead of them that initially appeared to be a large aircraft descending towards the ground. Their first assumption was not that they were seeing something mysterious, but that an aeroplane might be crashing and that they needed to assess the situation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO...</span></span></span>

The officers stopped their patrol vehicle and got out to obtain a clearer view. In Carson’s account, the object’s apparent approach created the impression of an aircraft in distress, but the subsequent movement changed their interpretation. They reported that the object descended to roughly 100–200 feet above the ground, then abruptly reversed direction and climbed to about 500 feet before stopping.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO...</span></span></span>

This opening sequence became one of the most frequently cited parts of the case because it involved a transition from an ordinary explanation — a possible aircraft accident — to a reported manoeuvre the officers considered inconsistent with known aircraft. The claim depended entirely on eyewitness observation, however, because no publicly available flight data or physical trace has established what the object was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO Case Directory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO Case Directory...</span></span></span>

### A close observation from the patrol car

After the initial movement, Carson and Scott reported that the object was clearly visible. Carson’s written statement described a glowing round or oval form with red lights at each end and intermittent white lights between them. Later accounts based on the police report said the object appeared silent despite its apparent movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO...</span></span></span>

The officers also described the object as moving in ways they interpreted as highly unusual, including rapid changes of direction and hovering. Their report stated that they attempted to approach or follow it, but that the object appeared to remain at a distance or move in relation to their position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO...</span></span></span>

The encounter became notable in UFO literature because the witnesses were not simply reporting a distant light. Their account involved an extended observation period, apparent proximity, and a sequence of movements rather than a single unexplained flash or brief sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO Case Directory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO Case Directory...</span></span></span>

## The reported movements and pursuit

### From observation to attempted tracking

The officers’ description developed into a moving encounter rather than a stationary sighting. They reported that the object moved away from them and that they attempted to keep track of it. According to later summaries of the police report, they described the object as capable of moving vertically and horizontally and of changing direction quickly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO...</span></span></span>

One of the more controversial parts of the account involved reports of a red beam of light and possible effects on communications. UFO researchers who collected the case later highlighted claims of radio interference and unusual interactions between the patrol vehicle and the object. These details came from witness testimony rather than independently recorded measurements, making them important parts of the narrative but difficult to verify historically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO...</span></span></span>

The officers’ account also included a later phase in which another similar object was reportedly observed. Other local law-enforcement witnesses were said to have seen unusual lights that night, adding to claims that the event involved more than one observer. However, the details of these additional observations are less documented than Carson and Scott’s primary report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/600813dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Bluff UFO Case Directory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Red Bluff UFO Case Directory...</span></span></span>

## Why Carson and Scott became the defining witnesses

The importance of Carson and Scott came from the combination of their occupations, the formal nature of their report, and the level of detail they provided. Police witnesses have often carried particular weight in UFO investigations because they are trained to record events, judge distances, and make observations under official circumstances. In the Red Bluff case, the officers’ professional status became one of the main reasons the sighting received continued attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/ufoe/section_7.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Section 7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Section 7...</span></span></span>

Their testimony was preserved through law-enforcement reporting channels and later circulated among UFO investigators. NICAP (the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena) highlighted the case as an example of a police-witness encounter and published summaries based on the officers’ statements and follow-up correspondence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/ufoe/section_7.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Section 7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Section 7...</span></span></span>

At the same time, witness credibility does not automatically determine the identity of an observed object.
