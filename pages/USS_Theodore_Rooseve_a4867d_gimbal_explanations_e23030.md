## Introduction

The central dispute over the USS Theodore Roosevelt “Gimbal” video is not whether the footage is genuine, but what the footage actually represents. The U.S. Navy recording is authentic, and the object remains officially listed as an unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP), but the short infrared clip does not by itself prove that the object displayed extraordinary flight capabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

Most of the modern debate has concentrated on three possibilities. One interpretation argues that the apparent rotating “craft” is primarily an infrared imaging effect produced by a bright heat source, possibly the exhaust of a conventional aircraft. Another argues that the video shows a real object whose movement, thermal signature, or reported behaviour cannot be easily explained by ordinary aircraft. A third position is more cautious: the available public footage is insufficient to determine the object’s identity because key data, including raw sensor files and full mission information, are not publicly available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/gimbal-ufo-a-new-analysis.12333/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gimbal UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gimbal UFO - A New Analysis &#124; MetabunkMarch 13, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2022</span></span></span>

The lasting disagreement comes from a mismatch between what the video clearly shows and what it cannot establish. The recording shows an unusual-looking infrared target; it does not provide enough information to independently reconstruct the object’s speed, distance, shape, or capabilities. That gap has allowed competing explanations to persist.

## The infrared glare explanation

The most influential conventional explanation is that the Gimbal object’s unusual shape and apparent rotation are caused largely by infrared glare rather than by the physical rotation of an aircraft. This hypothesis was developed most prominently by sceptical analyst Mick West and others examining how infrared targeting systems display bright heat sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/the-shape-and-size-of-glare-around-bright-lights-or-ir-heat-sources.10596/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Shape and Size of Glare Around Bright Lights or IR Heat Sources &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Shape and Size of Glare Around Bright Lights or IR Heat Sources &#124; MetabunkMarch 30, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 30, 2019</span></span></span>

Infrared cameras do not simply show an object’s visible outline. They detect thermal energy, and a very hot source can create a larger, distorted image than the actual emitting area. A distant aircraft viewed from behind, with its engines producing a strong heat signature, could appear as a large glowing shape rather than as a recognisable aeroplane. In this interpretation, the visible “body” of the object may be obscured by the sensor’s response to the heat source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/the-shape-and-size-of-glare-around-bright-lights-or-ir-heat-sources.10596/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Shape and Size of Glare Around Bright Lights or IR Heat Sources &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Shape and Size of Glare Around Bright Lights or IR Heat Sources &#124; MetabunkMarch 30, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 30, 2019</span></span></span>

The key argument concerns the famous moment when the object appears to rotate. Supporters of the glare explanation argue that the rotation is not the craft turning like a flying disc. Instead, they propose that the infrared image is changing orientation because of the way the ATFLIR targeting system stabilises and processes its image while tracking a target. ATFLIR systems use complex optical arrangements, including mechanisms designed to keep imagery correctly oriented during aircraft movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1394494B1/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: patents.google.com">[Google Patents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">patents.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google PatentsEP1394494B1 - Precision optical alignment system - Google PatentsOctober 12, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 12, 2005</span></span></span>

Under this interpretation, the “gimbal” effect is a property of the sensor system interacting with a bright infrared source. The object may have been a conventional aircraft, but the camera presentation made it appear more exotic than it was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/gimbal-ufo-a-new-analysis.12333/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gimbal UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gimbal UFO - A New Analysis &#124; MetabunkMarch 13, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 13, 2022</span></span></span>

This explanation has strengths. It addresses the most visually striking feature of the clip: the apparent rotation without an obvious change in flight path. It also fits a known category of infrared imaging problems, where heat sources can produce misleading shapes and apparent motion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/the-shape-and-size-of-glare-around-bright-lights-or-ir-heat-sources.10596/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Shape and Size of Glare Around Bright Lights or IR Heat Sources &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Shape and Size of Glare Around Bright Lights or IR Heat Sources &#124; MetabunkMarch 30, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 30, 2019</span></span></span>

However, the glare explanation is not universally accepted. Critics argue that it relies on assumptions about the target’s distance, orientation, and infrared signature that cannot be confirmed from the public video alone. They also argue that the wider encounter involved additional information reportedly available to the pilots, including radar observations, which is not contained in the released clip.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08773" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reconstruction of Potential Flight Paths for the January 2015 Gimbal UAP...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 2015</span></span></span>

## Claims of unusual movement

The strongest argument for an unresolved or genuinely unusual object comes from interpretations that treat the infrared image as representing a physical object rather than primarily a sensor artefact.

Some researchers have attempted to reconstruct possible flight paths from the available footage and from pilot descriptions of the encounter. A 2023 analysis by Yannick Peings and Marik von Rennenkampff examined possible trajectories under the assumption that the object was relatively close to the F/A-18 aircraft. The authors argued that, under those assumptions, the reported behaviour could imply rapid changes in direction that would be difficult for conventional aircraft to perform. They also discussed the competing interpretation that the video could instead show infrared glare from a distant jet exhaust.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08773" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reconstruction of Potential Flight Paths for the January 2015 Gimbal UAP...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 2015</span></span></span>

This side of the debate places greater weight on contextual information beyond the 34-second clip. Former Navy personnel associated with the Roosevelt encounters have described radar and visual observations connected to the broader 2014–2015 incidents, arguing that the video should not be analysed in isolation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08773" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reconstruction of Potential Flight Paths for the January 2015 Gimbal UAP...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 2015</span></span></span>

Supporters of an unusual-object interpretation also point out that the object was tracked by a military targeting system operated by trained aviators rather than captured by an ordinary camera. For them, the combination of military sensors, pilot reactions, and the object’s appearance makes a simple misidentification less satisfying.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/does-it-matter-that-the-dod-released-those-ufo-videos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These videos, previously surfaced through The New York Times in 2017, were not initially authorized by the Pentagon, though they were ack...</span></span></span>

The main weakness of this argument is that the public evidence is incomplete. The released video does not include the full radar picture, telemetry, infrared metadata, or a detailed measurement of the object’s range and velocity. ## What remains unresolved

The Gimbal debate continues because both sides are analysing a limited dataset. The available footage confirms that Navy personnel recorded something they could not immediately identify, but identification requires more than an unusual appearance. It requires reliable information about distance, movement, sensor behaviour, and possible conventional sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

The Gimbal video is not evidence that an extraordinary craft has been proven, but neither has every aspect of the encounter been conclusively explained from the released material. The enduring debate is therefore less about whether the video is real and more about whether the visible anomaly comes from the object itself, the sensor system observing it, or the limits of analysing a short recording without the underlying flight data.
