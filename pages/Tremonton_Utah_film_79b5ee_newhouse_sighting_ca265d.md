## Introduction

Delbert C. Newhouse’s 1952 sighting is the human foundation of the Tremonton, Utah film case. The event became historically important because the observer was not an untrained civilian describing a distant light, but a US Navy photographic specialist who used a 16mm colour camera to record what he believed were unusual aerial objects. On 2 July 1952, while travelling with his family near Tremonton, Utah, Newhouse stopped his car after his wife noticed a group of bright objects in the sky. He filmed the objects for roughly 30 feet of motion-picture film, creating one of the most examined pieces of UFO-related photographic evidence from the early Cold War period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/tremonton.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Tremonton, Utah, UFO Color Film of July 2, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1952</span></span></span>

Newhouse’s account gave the Tremonton film unusual weight among contemporary UFO reports. ## Newhouse’s background and credibility

Delbert C. Newhouse was a Navy warrant officer and experienced photographer whose career involved military photographic work. Investigators considered this background significant because he understood cameras, lenses, exposure, and image capture better than the average witness. His use of a Bell & Howell Automaster 16mm camera with a telephoto lens was one reason the footage received serious attention from military analysts rather than being treated simply as an anecdotal UFO report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spookyvalley.com/events/1952-tremonton-film/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spookyvalley.com">[Spooky Valley]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spookyvalley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spooky Valley Tremonton UFO Film &#124; Spooky Valley</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spooky ValleyTremonton UFO Film &#124; Spooky ValleyJuly 2, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1952</span></span></span>

The credibility question in the Tremonton case has therefore always been slightly different from many other UFO reports. The main issue was not whether Newhouse was sincere or whether he had actually filmed something. Later investigators generally accepted that he genuinely observed unusual objects and created a genuine film record. The disagreement centred on what the recorded objects represented. A reliable witness can accurately report an observation while still lacking the information needed to identify a distant object in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case49.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 49: Tremonton, Utah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 49: Tremonton, Utah - Movie Film...</span></span></span>

Newhouse’s own testimony reflected this limitation. He reported that he could not determine the objects’ exact size, altitude, speed, or distance because there was no fixed reference point in the sky. This became a major analytical problem: without a known object for scale, a small nearby object and a large distant object can produce similar appearances on film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/tremonton.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Tremonton, Utah, UFO Color Film of July 2, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1952</span></span></span>

## The Utah highway sighting and camera footage

The sighting occurred on the morning of 2 July 1952 as Newhouse travelled with his wife and two children along a highway near Tremonton. According to his account, his wife first noticed a group of objects that she could not identify. Newhouse stopped the vehicle, retrieved his camera from the car, and began filming a formation of bright objects moving in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/tremonton.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Tremonton, Utah, UFO Color Film of July 2, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1952</span></span></span>

Newhouse described seeing approximately ten or twelve objects moving in a loose formation. He said they appeared bright and unusual, later comparing their shape to two saucers joined together. During filming, he followed the group and also recorded an individual object that separated from the formation and crossed his field of view several times.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/tremonton.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Tremonton, Utah, UFO Color Film of July 2, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1952</span></span></span>

The physical characteristics of the recording made it particularly influential. The footage was shot on daylight colour film, and Newhouse used a telephoto lens that allowed the objects to be captured as visible forms rather than simple points of light. Contemporary UFO investigators regarded this as an important distinction because the film provided something that could be examined frame by frame rather than relying only on memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/utah6.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Tremonton, Utah UFO Color Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Tremonton, Utah UFO Color Film - NICAP Synopsis...</span></span></span>

However, the same characteristics that made the film valuable also created uncertainty. The camera recorded brightness, movement, and shape, but it did not provide reliable measurements of range. A distant bird reflecting sunlight, for example, could appear very different from its true form when enlarged through a telephoto lens. Later analyses focused heavily on this question: whether the unusual appearance resulted from an unusual object or from ordinary objects seen under unusual photographic conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case49.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 49: Tremonton, Utah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 49: Tremonton, Utah - Movie Film...</span></span></span>

## Why the filming event became a major UFO case

The importance of Newhouse’s filming event came from the combination of witness quality and physical evidence. During the early 1950s, many UFO reports consisted only of verbal testimony. The Tremonton case added a surviving visual record, and the involvement of Navy and Air Force analysts gave it a level of attention few sightings received. The film was examined through military channels and became one of the better-known photographic cases associated with Project Blue Book.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosdex.com/locations/tremonton-utah" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosdex.com">[Disclosdex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosdex.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tremonton, Utah &#124; Disclosdex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tremonton, Utah &#124; DisclosdexJuly 2, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1952</span></span></span>

The film also illustrates a recurring difficulty in aerial identification: photographic evidence can be real without being conclusive. The existence of the film proves that Newhouse captured something unusual in appearance, but it does not by itself establish the nature of the objects. Analysts had to reconstruct factors that were not recorded, including distance, lighting conditions, object shape, and movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case49.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 49: Tremonton, Utah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 49: Tremonton, Utah - Movie Film...</span></span></span>

## Limits of witness observations

Newhouse’s account remains one of the strongest parts of the Tremonton case because it provides a clear chain from observation to recording. He saw something unusual, deliberately filmed it, and preserved a physical record. Yet witness expertise cannot remove all observational uncertainty, especially in cases involving distant aerial objects.

The central weakness was the absence of scale. Newhouse could describe the apparent appearance and movement of the objects, but he could not establish whether they were close and small or distant and large. This uncertainty affected estimates of speed and manoeuvring, two of the features that made the footage appear unusual to early viewers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/tremonton.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: the Tremonton, Utah, UFO Color Film of July 2, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1952</span></span></span>

Later investigators studying the film considered natural explanations, particularly birds such as gulls reflecting sunlight. The Condon Report’s analysis concluded that observations of similar bird behaviour provided strong support for identifying the filmed objects as birds, although the case had attracted debate because of the unusual appearance of the images and the earlier difficulty of reaching an identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case49.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 49: Tremonton, Utah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 49: Tremonton, Utah - Movie Film...</span></span></span>
