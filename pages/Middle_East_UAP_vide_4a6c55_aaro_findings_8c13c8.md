## Introduction

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) assessment of the 2022 Middle East UAP video reached a careful but limited conclusion: the object was not identified, but the available evidence did not show that it behaved in a way requiring an extraordinary explanation. AARO treated the case as an unresolved observation rather than a demonstration of advanced technology, extraterrestrial activity, or a confirmed anomaly. The distinction was central to the office’s public message: “unidentified” describes a gap in attribution, not proof that an object is beyond known physics or engineering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDepartment of Defense Releases the Annual Report on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) &gt; U.S. Department of Def...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/)

## The official review process

AARO examined the Middle East UAP video as part of its broader mission to identify and resolve reports of unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP). Created within the US Department of Defense, AARO’s role is not simply to catalogue unexplained sightings but to determine whether reported objects can be attributed to known aircraft, drones, balloons, satellites, natural phenomena, sensor effects, or other conventional sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDepartment of Defense Releases the Annual Report on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) &gt; U.S. Department of Def...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/)

The office’s approach places emphasis on data quality. A military recording may provide a reliable timestamp, platform information, and a genuine sensor image, but those strengths do not automatically answer the most important analytical questions. Investigators need measurements such as range, velocity, trajectory, atmospheric conditions, and corroboration from other systems before drawing conclusions about an object’s capabilities. AARO has repeatedly identified insufficient or incomplete sensor data as a major reason some cases remain unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDepartment of Defense Releases the Annual Report on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) &gt; U.S. Department of Def...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/)

In the Middle East video case, the available footage showed an object crossing the field of view of a military sensor, but AARO did not find evidence that the object demonstrated unusual acceleration, extraordinary manoeuvring, or other characteristics that would separate it from ordinary explanations. The assessment therefore remained open-ended: the object’s identity was unresolved, but the evidence did not support claims of exceptional technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDepartment of Defense Releases the Annual Report on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) &gt; U.S. Department of Def...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/)

## Why AARO left the object unidentified

AARO’s conclusion was shaped by the difference between proving what something is and proving what it is not. An unidentified object can remain unresolved for several reasons: the recording may not capture enough information, the object may be too distant for accurate measurement, or key supporting data may not be available for review.

For the Middle East video, the unresolved status did not mean that AARO had confirmed something unusual. Instead, it reflected the limits of the available evidence. This distinction is important because UAP investigations often involve observations where the strongest available conclusion is simply that analysts cannot make a confident identification from the existing data.

AARO’s wider reporting supports this cautious interpretation. In its later annual reporting, the office stated that many resolved cases were attributed to ordinary objects such as balloons, birds, unmanned aerial systems, satellites, or aircraft, while unresolved cases generally remained open because of missing or insufficient information rather than because investigators had confirmed extraordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDepartment of Defense Releases the Annual Report on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) &gt; U.S. Department of Def...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/)

The Middle East video therefore became a useful case study in uncertainty management. It showed that a government investigation can acknowledge a genuine unexplained observation without moving directly from “we do not know” to “the object represents a breakthrough discovery”.

## Unidentified versus anomalous objects

One of AARO’s most significant contributions to public understanding has been separating two concepts that are often combined in popular discussion.

An **unidentified** object is one for which analysts cannot yet determine a clear origin or explanation. An **anomalous** object, by contrast, would require evidence that it displays characteristics inconsistent with known technology, natural phenomena, or measurement limitations.

The Middle East UAP video remained in the first category. AARO’s assessment did not identify behaviour that required classification as an anomalous object. This distinction reduced some of the speculation surrounding [the footage]({{ 'the-footage/' | relative_url }}) because the existence of uncertainty alone was not treated as evidence of extraordinary performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDepartment of Defense Releases the Annual Report on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) &gt; U.S. Department of Def...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/)

This framework also influenced AARO’s broader historical review of government UAP investigations. ## Why missing information matters


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The most important lesson from AARO’s assessment is that the quality of evidence determines the strength of the conclusion. A high-quality source, such as a military sensor recording, can establish that an event occurred, but it may still fail to reveal the object’s true identity.

</div>

For analysts, missing information can create several competing possibilities. A small object viewed without reliable distance data may appear to move in unusual ways because of perspective effects. A sensor image without full telemetry may not reveal whether apparent motion comes from the object itself, the observing platform, or changes in camera angle. Without additional measurements, investigators may be unable to distinguish between a conventional object viewed under unusual conditions and something genuinely unusual.

AARO’s wider investigative record reflects this challenge. The office has argued that improving reporting standards, sensor collection, and access to relevant data are essential for reducing the number of unresolved cases. Its annual reporting has continued to highlight that better information is often the deciding factor between a case that can be solved and one that must remain open.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDepartment of Defense Releases the Annual Report on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) &gt; U.S. Department of Def...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/)

For the 2022 Middle East UAP video, the official assessment therefore rests on two conclusions that exist together: the recording documents a real military observation, and the available evidence does not demonstrate anomalous technology. The unresolved label describes the limits of the investigation, not a hidden confirmation of an extraordinary explanation.
