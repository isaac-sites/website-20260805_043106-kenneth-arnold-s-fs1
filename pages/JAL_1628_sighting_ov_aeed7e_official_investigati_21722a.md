## Introduction

The [JAL 1628 sighting]({{ 'jal-1628-sighting-over-alaska-1986/' | relative_url }}) remains unresolved not because investigators ignored it, but because the official reviews produced a narrower conclusion than many later retellings suggest. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) documented the encounter, interviewed the crew, examined radar information, and preserved records, but it did not determine that the aircraft was accompanied by an unknown physical craft. Instead, the case remained an “unidentified” event in the practical aviation sense: an observation that could not be conclusively matched to a known source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/topics/uaps/textual-and-microfilm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesRecords Related to Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs) and Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAPs) at the National Archiv...</span></span></span>

Both sides have relied on parts of the same evidence, which is why the case continues to be debated decades later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/jal1628-1986/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">jal1628 1986</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP CodexJAL 1628 · UAP Codex...</span></span></span>

## FAA investigation and records

### What did the official review establish?

The FAA’s investigation began after the crew of Japan Air Lines Flight 1628 reported unusual lights and a large object during a night flight over Alaska on 17 November 1986. FAA investigators interviewed Captain Kenju Terauchi, First Officer Takanori Tamefuji, and Flight Engineer Yoshio Tsukuba. Officials reported that the pilots were experienced, rational, and not affected by alcohol, drugs, or other factors that would undermine their testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.upi.com/amp/Archives/1986/12/30/Controller-confirms-UFO-sighting/4431536302800/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upi.com">[UPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Controller confirms UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Controller confirms UFO sighting - UPI Archives...</span></span></span>

The investigation produced an important distinction: officials accepted that the crew had made a genuine report of something they could not identify, but that did not automatically confirm the existence of an unknown vehicle. FAA spokesman Paul Steucke stated that the [radar evidence]({{ 'radar-evidence-eb329e/' | relative_url }}) did not provide definitive support for the sighting, while also acknowledging that a controller had reported a radar contact associated with the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.upi.com/amp/Archives/1986/12/30/Controller-confirms-UFO-sighting/4431536302800/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upi.com">[UPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Controller confirms UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Controller confirms UFO sighting - UPI Archives...</span></span></span>

The surviving FAA records are significant because they show the government treated the event as an aviation investigation rather than simply dismissing it. The National Archives lists the FAA’s 1986 Japan Airlines Flight 1628 material among its UFO-related holdings, including records from the Federal Aviation Administration collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/topics/uaps/textual-and-microfilm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesRecords Related to Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs) and Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAPs) at the National Archiv...</span></span></span> These records include witness statements and administrative material that allow later researchers to examine how officials assessed the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufofiles.fm/documents/jal1628-733667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufofiles.fm">[ufofiles.fm]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufofiles.fm</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">JAL Flight 1628: FAA Investigation File 733667-001 Page 2 (26 JAN 1987) — OTHER — UFOFILES.FMJanuary 26, 1987...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 26, 1987</span></span></span>

### Why the radar evidence became the central dispute

Radar was the strongest argument for those who considered the event unusually well documented. Reports from the period stated that an Anchorage air traffic controller observed a radar return near the aircraft, and accounts from FAA personnel also discussed military radar information from Alaska.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.upi.com/amp/Archives/1986/12/30/Controller-confirms-UFO-sighting/4431536302800/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upi.com">[UPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Controller confirms UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Controller confirms UFO sighting - UPI Archives...</span></span></span>

However, radar did not provide a simple answer. The technical disagreement focused on whether the additional radar return represented a separate object or an artefact created by the radar system. FAA explanations included possibilities such as a “split target” or an uncorrelated return, where radar information associated with the aircraft could appear as a separate contact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/jal1628-1986/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">jal1628 1986</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP CodexJAL 1628 · UAP Codex...</span></span></span>

Supporters of an unknown-object interpretation argue that the radar data was meaningful because the contact appeared during a period when the crew was reporting visual observations and during manoeuvres intended to test whether the object behaved independently. Skeptics respond that radar anomalies are known to occur and that an unidentified radar return is not the same as confirmation of an unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/jal1628-1986/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">jal1628 1986</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP CodexJAL 1628 · UAP Codex...</span></span></span>

## Astronomical and perceptual explanations

### The Jupiter and Mars hypothesis

One of the best-known skeptical explanations came from aviation writer Philip J. Klass, who argued that the main visual observations could have involved bright astronomical objects rather than a nearby aircraft or unknown craft. This explanation proposed that Jupiter, which was very bright at the time, may have been mistaken for a large object near the aircraft, while other lights could have been stars or planets seen through changing atmospheric conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/jal1628-1986/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">jal1628 1986</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP CodexJAL 1628 · UAP Codex...</span></span></span>

The strength of this explanation is that pilots can misjudge the distance and position of bright objects in the night sky, particularly when there are few visual references. A distant object at a low angle above the horizon can appear to move relative to an aircraft because of changes in the observer’s viewpoint. The weakness is that some aspects of the crew’s description, especially their impression of a large object accompanying the aircraft and the radar reports, have led some researchers to argue that an astronomical explanation does not account for every detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/jal1628-1986/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">jal1628 1986</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP CodexJAL 1628 · UAP Codex...</span></span></span>

### Human perception and the limits of night observation

The case also highlights a broader aviation issue: experienced pilots can provide highly valuable observations, but even trained observers must interpret visual information under conditions where distance, scale, and motion are difficult to judge. At night, the absence of familiar reference points can make stationary or distant lights appear to move, approach, or maintain position relative to an aircraft.

This does not mean the JAL crew’s testimony was unreliable. The FAA’s own interviews treated the pilots as credible witnesses describing what they believed they saw. ## The continuing debate over conclusions

### Why no single explanation has become accepted

The JAL 1628 case remains contested because the evidence exists at different levels of certainty. The fact that the crew saw unusual lights is strongly supported by their reports and subsequent investigation. The fact that radar systems recorded unusual returns is also part of the historical record. The unresolved issue is what those observations represented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.upi.com/amp/Archives/1986/12/30/Controller-confirms-UFO-sighting/4431536302800/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upi.com">[UPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Controller confirms UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Controller confirms UFO sighting - UPI Archives...</span></span></span>

* **Official FAA-style interpretation:** the event was a genuine unexplained report, but the available evidence did not prove a separate physical craft. * **Skeptical interpretation:** the sighting can be explained through a combination of astronomical objects, visual misperception, and radar effects without requiring an unknown vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/jal1628-1986/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">jal1628 1986</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP CodexJAL 1628 · UAP Codex...</span></span></span>
