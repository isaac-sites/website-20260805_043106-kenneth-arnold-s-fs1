## Introduction

Project Blue Book’s review of the 1952 Salem, Massachusetts photograph did not produce a definitive identification of the objects shown in the image. The U.S. Air Force investigation examined the photograph as part of its wider effort to determine whether UFO reports represented security threats, natural phenomena, misidentifications, or evidence of unknown technology. In the Salem case, investigators considered conventional photographic explanations, including double exposure and reflections, but the record ultimately did not settle on a final explanation accepted by all analysts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>

The unresolved status of the photograph is important because the case combined several features that made photographic UFO reports difficult to evaluate: an identifiable military photographer, a claimed second witness, an original image preserved in official files, and a scene where photographic conditions themselves created uncertainty. Project Blue Book’s handling of Salem illustrates a recurring problem in UFO investigations: a case could remain unexplained without necessarily proving an extraordinary cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/disasters-and-phenomena/u2s-ufos-and-operation-blue-book.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command U-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandU-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue BookJanuary 24, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2024</span></span></span>

## The Air Force investigation record

Project Blue Book entered the Salem photograph into its files as Case No. 1501. The image was taken on 16 July 1952 by Coast Guard photographer Shell R. Alpert at the Salem Air Station. The Air Force review examined the photograph within the broader Project Blue Book system, which collected and analysed thousands of UFO reports between 1952 and 1969.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>

The investigation history shows a sequence of changing interpretations rather than a single conclusion. Early analysis reportedly considered the possibility that the image was a deliberate double exposure or photographic hoax. A later review considered the possibility that the lights were reflections, including reflections from lamps on a window surface. The case was eventually retained as unexplained in Project Blue Book records rather than being closed with a confirmed explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>

This progression reveals how Project Blue Book approached difficult photographic evidence. Investigators generally attempted to identify ordinary causes first because photographs could be affected by camera settings, glass surfaces, lighting conditions, exposure errors, and other technical factors. A photograph showing unusual lights was therefore not automatically treated as evidence of an unknown aircraft; it had to survive attempts at conventional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.secretsdeclassified.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/459832/project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretsdeclassified.af.mil">[Secrets Declassified]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretsdeclassified.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

The Salem image also differed from many weaker UFO reports because its chain of custody was relatively clear. The photograph was linked to a named Coast Guard photographer and was preserved among official Air Force UFO documentation. The National Archives later became the repository for Project Blue Book records, allowing researchers to examine the original investigative material rather than relying only on later retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

## Double exposure and reflection theories

The main technical dispute around the Salem photograph centred on whether the lights represented objects outside the building or an effect created during photography. Because Alpert took the photograph through a window, investigators had to consider whether the glass introduced reflections or optical artefacts. The window detail became one of the central reasons the image remained difficult to interpret.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/disasters-and-phenomena/u2s-ufos-and-operation-blue-book.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command U-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandU-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue BookJanuary 24, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2024</span></span></span>

A double exposure explanation would mean that the same piece of film recorded more than one image, potentially creating false light shapes. This was one of the earliest possibilities considered by Blue Book analysts. However, the later shift toward a reflection explanation indicates that investigators continued to examine alternative causes rather than immediately accepting the first interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>

The reflection theory had a stronger connection to the physical circumstances of the photograph. Bright sources outside or near a window can create repeated or displaced images on film, especially under certain lighting conditions. For critics of the UFO interpretation, the presence of glass between the camera and the scene made an optical explanation plausible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/disasters-and-phenomena/u2s-ufos-and-operation-blue-book.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command U-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandU-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue BookJanuary 24, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2024</span></span></span>

Supporters of the “unresolved” interpretation have argued that the conventional explanations did not completely account for every reported detail, particularly the claim that Alpert and another Coast Guard member, Thomas Flaherty, observed the lights before the photograph was taken. The existence of a second reported witness increased the perceived strength of the case among UFO researchers, although a [witness account]({{ 'witness-account-a3a003/' | relative_url }}) alone could not determine what caused the photographic image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>

The disagreement therefore was not simply between “explained” and “unexplained.” It involved different standards of evidence. A sceptical assessment could argue that the photograph lacked proof of physical objects because ordinary photographic effects were possible. A more cautious interpretation could argue that the available records did not conclusively demonstrate which specific photographic mechanism produced the image. Both positions can exist without reaching the same conclusion about what was photographed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>

## Why the case remained unresolved

The Salem photograph remained unresolved because Project Blue Book did not establish a final explanation that fully satisfied the available evidence. The Air Force’s classification of a case as “unidentified” meant that investigators had not identified the cause of the report; it did not represent an official conclusion that the event involved extraterrestrial technology or an unknown aircraft. The Air Force later stated that among the thousands of investigated reports, unidentified cases did not provide evidence of threats to national security or proof of technology beyond known scientific understanding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.secretsdeclassified.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/459832/project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretsdeclassified.af.mil">[Secrets Declassified]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretsdeclassified.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

For the Salem case specifically, the unresolved label reflected an evidence gap. The photograph existed, the photographer was known, and investigative documents survived, but the available material did not provide enough information to reconstruct the scene with certainty. Missing or ambiguous details — such as the exact relationship between the lights, the window, the camera position, and possible external light sources — limited the ability to prove one explanation conclusively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>

The case also demonstrates a broader limitation of early UFO photographic analysis. A photograph can be authentic while still being ambiguous. Authenticity answers the question of whether an image is a genuine photograph taken at a particular time; it does not automatically reveal what the image depicts. In Salem’s case, the debate continued because the image’s origin was relatively well documented, while its interpretation remained uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/disasters-and-phenomena/u2s-ufos-and-operation-blue-book.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command U-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandU-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue BookJanuary 24, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2024</span></span></span>

Ultimately, Project Blue Book did not solve the Salem photograph, but it did preserve the case as an example of the challenges involved in evaluating unusual aerial images. The investigation narrowed the field of possibilities by examining conventional explanations, yet it stopped short of a definitive finding. That combination — a credible historical record but incomplete explanatory evidence — is what allowed the Salem photograph to remain one of the better-known unresolved images from the Project Blue Book era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufo Phenomenon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo Phenomenonufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>
