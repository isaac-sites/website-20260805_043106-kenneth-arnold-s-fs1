## Introduction

The crew testimony from Japan Air Lines Flight 1628 is the central human evidence in one of the best-known commercial aviation UFO cases. On 17 November 1986, the three-person cockpit crew of a JAL Boeing 747 cargo aircraft reported seeing unusual lights and a much larger object while flying over Alaska. Their accounts became significant because the witnesses were experienced aviation professionals who made their reports during an active flight, communicated with air traffic controllers, and were later interviewed by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/01/02/ufo-sighting-confirmed-by-faa-air-force-radar/c186c4b7-54ed-459e-b94d-eeeff7b3322e/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Washington Post UFO SIGHTING CONFIRMED BY FAA, AIR FORCE RADAR</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Washington PostUFO SIGHTING CONFIRMED BY FAA, AIR FORCE RADAR - The Washington PostJanuary 1, 1987...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1987</span></span></span>

## The pilots' reported observations

The main witness was Captain Kenju Terauchi, commander of JAL Flight 1628. According to FAA interview notes, Terauchi first reported visual contact with unidentified air traffic while the Boeing 747 was cruising at approximately flight level 390. He described objects appearing ahead of the aircraft and later reported that the crew observed bright lights and a larger object accompanying the aircraft. The FAA records identify the other crew members interviewed with him as First Officer Takanori Tamefuji and Flight Engineer Yoshio Tsukuda.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/jal1628/733667-001-031.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[documents.theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This document is made available through the declassification efforts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This document is made available through the declassification efforts</span></span></span>

Terauchi’s account developed through several phases. The crew initially noticed bright lights that appeared to move near the aircraft. Later, Terauchi described a much larger object that he believed was considerably bigger than the 747 itself. Contemporary reporting quoted him as describing the object as enormous compared with his aircraft, while noting that the two smaller lights he had first observed were not seen on radar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/01/02/ufo-sighting-confirmed-by-faa-air-force-radar/c186c4b7-54ed-459e-b94d-eeeff7b3322e/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Washington Post UFO SIGHTING CONFIRMED BY FAA, AIR FORCE RADAR</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Washington PostUFO SIGHTING CONFIRMED BY FAA, AIR FORCE RADAR - The Washington PostJanuary 1, 1987...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1987</span></span></span>

A key feature of the testimony was the reported behaviour of the objects. The crew believed the lights were not simply distant stars or aircraft because they appeared to change position relative to the 747, approach the aircraft, and remain associated with its flight path. The crew contacted air traffic control while attempting to determine whether the objects could be identified as normal traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/jal1628/733667-001-031.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[documents.theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This document is made available through the declassification efforts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This document is made available through the declassification efforts</span></span></span>

The encounter also placed the crew in an unusual decision-making situation. Rather than simply observing an unexplained light, they were operating a large cargo aircraft in controlled airspace and had to consider whether the reported object represented a collision risk. FAA interview notes recorded that Terauchi was authorised to take action if necessary to avoid the reported traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/01/02/ufo-sighting-confirmed-by-faa-air-force-radar/c186c4b7-54ed-459e-b94d-eeeff7b3322e/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Washington Post UFO SIGHTING CONFIRMED BY FAA, AIR FORCE RADAR</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Washington PostUFO SIGHTING CONFIRMED BY FAA, AIR FORCE RADAR - The Washington PostJanuary 1, 1987...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1987</span></span></span>

## Crew credibility and aviation experience

The credibility of the witnesses is one of the reasons the case attracted lasting attention. The report did not come from a single casual observer on the ground, but from three crew members responsible for operating a commercial aircraft. FAA investigators interviewed the captain, first officer, and flight engineer shortly after the event, preserving their accounts while memories were still recent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/jal1628/733667-001-031.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[documents.theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This document is made available through the declassification efforts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This document is made available through the declassification efforts</span></span></span>

Supporters of the crew’s reliability point to several factors: the witnesses were trained to interpret aircraft environments, they reported the event through aviation channels rather than only after publicity developed, and they maintained that their observations involved structured movement rather than a brief unexplained flash. These characteristics led some researchers to treat the case as more significant than ordinary unidentified-light reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-vault-files-1986-alaska-jal-flight-1628/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the vault files 1986 alaska jal flight 1628</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultThe Vault Files: 1986 Alaska JAL Flight 1628 - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

However, witness expertise does not remove the possibility of misinterpretation. Aviation professionals are skilled observers of aircraft operations, but night-time aerial observation remains difficult because distance, lighting, atmospheric conditions, and lack of reference points can distort perceptions of size and movement. Skeptical analyses have argued that even experienced pilots can misidentify bright astronomical objects or unusual visual phenomena when judging objects from a moving aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-vault-files-1986-alaska-jal-flight-1628/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the vault files 1986 alaska jal flight 1628</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultThe Vault Files: 1986 Alaska JAL Flight 1628 - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

The role of the other two crew members is also debated. The strongest descriptions of the large object came from Captain Terauchi, while later summaries of the case often note that the other crew members’ observations were less detailed. Skeptics have used this difference to argue that the case relies heavily on the captain’s interpretation, whereas supporters argue that the overall cockpit observations still represent multiple trained witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-vault-files-1986-alaska-jal-flight-1628/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the vault files 1986 alaska jal flight 1628</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultThe Vault Files: 1986 Alaska JAL Flight 1628 - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

## How the testimony was interpreted

The crew’s account produced two competing interpretations. The first view treats the testimony as evidence of a genuinely unidentified aerial object. ## The lasting significance of the crew accounts


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The JAL 1628 testimony remains influential because it illustrates both the value and the limits of eyewitness evidence in aviation-related mysteries. The crew provided a detailed contemporaneous account from inside a commercial aircraft, but the observations were still shaped by the challenges of judging distant objects in the night sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/jal1628/733667-001-031.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[documents.theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This document is made available through the declassification efforts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This document is made available through the declassification efforts</span></span></span>
