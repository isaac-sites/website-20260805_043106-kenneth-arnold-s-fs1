## Introduction

The investigation into the Cedric Allingham encounter changed the nature of the 1954 case. What first appeared to be a report of a British man meeting a visitor from Mars became, decades later, an investigation into authorship, identity, and the methods used to test extraordinary claims. The central discovery was that Cedric Allingham was not a verifiable individual but a constructed persona behind *Flying Saucer from Mars*, with investigators linking the deception to astronomer Patrick Moore and his friend Peter Davies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Allingham" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cedric Allingham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cedric Allingham</span></span></span>

The case became significant because the alleged hoax was not exposed through a single dramatic contradiction. Instead, researchers reconstructed a chain of clues: the absence of a genuine witness trail, similarities between Allingham’s writing and Moore’s style, problems surrounding the book’s photographs, and links between the supposed author image, Peter Davies, and Moore’s own surroundings. The investigation demonstrated how UFO claims could be examined through ordinary historical methods: checking identities, comparing documents, and tracing evidence back to its origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://magoniamagazine.blogspot.com/2013/10/allingham.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: magoniamagazine.blogspot.com">[Magonia Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">magoniamagazine.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span></span></span>

## The investigation by Christopher Allan and Steuart Campbell

The breakthrough came in 1986, when Christopher Allan and Steuart Campbell published their investigation “Flying Saucer from Moore’s?” in the Fortean journal *Magonia*. Their work focused on a question that earlier critics had raised but had not fully resolved: if Cedric Allingham could not be found, who had actually created the story?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://magoniamagazine.blogspot.com/2013/10/allingham.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: magoniamagazine.blogspot.com">[Magonia Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">magoniamagazine.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span></span></span>

Earlier attempts to verify Allingham had already produced serious doubts. Researchers searching for the author found that he was unusually difficult to contact. His publisher gave explanations for his absence, including claims that he was receiving medical treatment in Switzerland and later that he had died, but independent confirmation of his existence remained absent. The supposed supporting witness, fisherman James Duncan, was similarly impossible to verify. These problems led some investigators, including Robert Chapman in the late 1960s, to question whether Allingham existed at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/allingham-cedric" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span></span></span>

Allan and Campbell approached the case as a reconstruction rather than simply a criticism of the UFO claim. They examined several surviving pieces of evidence:

* **The writing style of *Flying Saucer from Mars*.** They argued that the language, phrasing, and references showed notable similarities to the published work of Patrick Moore, the well-known British astronomer and broadcaster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/allingham-cedric" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span></span></span>
* **The author photograph.** The image presented as Cedric Allingham showed a man standing beside a telescope. Investigators compared the scene with photographs connected to Moore and argued that the telescope and garden setting matched Moore’s own equipment and surroundings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/allingham-cedric" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span></span></span>
* **The alleged UFO lecture.** Allingham was said to have appeared before a flying saucer group in Kent. The investigation later connected this appearance to Peter Davies, who reportedly admitted that he had impersonated Allingham at the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Allingham" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cedric Allingham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cedric Allingham</span></span></span>

The conclusion reached by Allan and Campbell was that the book was an elaborate construction involving Davies and Moore. Their investigation did not rely on a confession from Moore; instead, it assembled a pattern of documentary and circumstantial evidence that pointed towards his involvement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://magoniamagazine.blogspot.com/2013/10/allingham.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: magoniamagazine.blogspot.com">[Magonia Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">magoniamagazine.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span></span></span>

## Links between Allingham, Moore, and Davies

The suspected connection between Cedric Allingham, Patrick Moore, and Peter Davies rested on several overlapping clues rather than one definitive document naming the creators. Davies was identified as a key figure after correspondence sent through the publisher helped investigators trace him. According to accounts of the investigation, Davies acknowledged involvement with *Flying Saucer from Mars* but said that another person had written the main text, while he had helped disguise the author’s style. He also reportedly admitted that he had appeared publicly as Allingham.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/allingham-cedric" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span></span></span>

Patrick Moore’s connection was strengthened by the similarities investigators found between his writing and the Allingham text. Moore was already a prominent figure in British astronomy through his broadcasting work on *The Sky at Night*, and he had a reputation for scepticism towards claims of alien contact. This background made the alleged hoax particularly striking: the person suspected of helping create one of Britain’s best-known UFO deceptions was also known publicly as a critic of weak evidence in astronomy and UFO reporting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Moore" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Patrick Moore</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Patrick Moore</span></span></span>

The photograph evidence became one of the most memorable parts of the investigation. The image in *Flying Saucer from Mars* showed “Allingham” beside a reflecting telescope, supposedly establishing his identity as an amateur astronomer. Allan and Campbell argued that the telescope and garden background corresponded with Moore’s own equipment and home environment, suggesting that the photograph was staged rather than a genuine portrait of an independent witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/allingham-cedric" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span></span></span>

The case also reflected the culture of 1950s flying-saucer reporting. *Flying Saucer from Mars* appeared soon after George Adamski’s widely discussed contact claims, and investigators later suggested that Allingham’s story functioned partly as a parody of the contactee literature of the period. By creating a convincing but false account, the hoaxers appeared to be testing how readily UFO groups would accept an extraordinary story supported by weak verification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://magoniamagazine.blogspot.com/2013/10/allingham.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: magoniamagazine.blogspot.com">[Magonia Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">magoniamagazine.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span></span></span>

## The unresolved question of motive

Although the identity investigation produced a strong explanation of how the Allingham story was created, the exact motive remains less certain. The most common interpretation is that Moore and Davies intended the book as a practical joke and a demonstration of how easily uncritical UFO enthusiasts could be persuaded by an apparently detailed account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Allingham" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cedric Allingham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cedric Allingham</span></span></span>

The timing supports this interpretation. During the early 1950s, UFO books and contactee claims were attracting large audiences, often combining dramatic personal testimony with photographs, alleged witnesses, and claims of communication with extraterrestrial beings. The Allingham story copied many of these features closely enough to appear credible while including clues that investigators later interpreted as deliberate satire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://magoniamagazine.blogspot.com/2013/10/allingham.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: magoniamagazine.blogspot.com">[Magonia Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">magoniamagazine.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Magonia Magazine MAGONIA ARCHIVE: Flying Saucer from Moore&#x27;s?</span></span></span>

However, the absence of a full public admission from Moore left part of the story unresolved. Sources discussing the investigation note that Moore denied responsibility and did not publicly confirm his role, despite the evidence assembled by Allan and Campbell and the later acceptance among many researchers that he was central to the hoax.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/10959477/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en-academic.com">[en-academic.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en-academic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cedric Allingham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cedric Allingham</span></span></span>

This uncertainty is part of what has kept the Allingham case historically interesting. The mystery was not only whether a Martian had landed in Scotland, but how a fictional witness could acquire the appearance of reality through a book, photographs, a supposed lecture, and supporting testimony. The investigation into Patrick Moore’s connection transformed the case from a UFO claim into a study of authorship, evidence evaluation, and the vulnerabilities of investigative methods when faced with extraordinary stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/allingham-cedric" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Allingham, Cedric &#124; Encyclopedia.com</span></span></span>

      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
