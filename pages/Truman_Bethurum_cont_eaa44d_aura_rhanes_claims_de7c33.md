## Introduction

Aura Rhanes is the central figure in Truman Bethurum’s 1954 contact narrative: the alleged commander of a flying saucer crew from the planet Clarion. Bethurum’s account was unusual even within the early UFO contactee movement because it did not centre only on a mysterious craft or distant signal; it revolved around a named individual with a personality, authority, and a detailed description of another society. According to Bethurum, he met Rhanes during a series of encounters in the Nevada desert beginning in 1952, and she explained that her people had come from a peaceful world hidden from ordinary human observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

The Aura Rhanes story became the most memorable part of Bethurum’s wider claims because it combined a personal meeting, a proposed location for an unknown planet, and a moral message about humanity’s future. ## The commander of the Clarion craft

### Aura Rhanes as Bethurum’s central witness

Bethurum described Aura Rhanes as the captain of the spacecraft he called the “Admiral’s Scow”. In his account, she was not portrayed as a distant or frightening alien figure but as a calm, approachable leader who spoke with him directly about Earth, space travel, and the condition of human civilisation. Bethurum said that Rhanes was physically similar to a human being and described her as a woman with an olive complexion and dark hair, making her one of the more distinctive personalities in 1950s contactee literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

The reported meetings gave the case a narrative structure that differed from many UFO sightings of the period. Rather than a single observation of an unexplained object, Bethurum claimed repeated conversations with the same individual. He later stated that he had several meetings with the Clarion crew, with Rhanes serving as the main source of information about their home world and their purpose for visiting Earth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Bethurum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Bethurum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman Bethurum</span></span></span>

Rhanes’ role also reflected a wider pattern in 1950s contactee stories. Many accounts from that era presented extraterrestrial visitors as technologically superior but morally peaceful beings who warned humanity about war and nuclear weapons. Bethurum’s version placed Rhanes within this tradition: she represented a more advanced civilisation that had supposedly solved social problems that still troubled Earth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

### The conversations that shaped Clarion’s image

According to Bethurum, his discussions with Rhanes were not limited to spacecraft technology. He claimed that she described Clarion as a society without many of the conflicts found on Earth, presenting it as an example of what humanity could become. The themes included peace, cooperation, and the dangers of destructive technologies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

This emphasis on philosophy and social improvement was important to Bethurum’s later public identity. After publishing *Aboard a Flying Saucer* in 1954, he continued promoting messages associated with Clarion, including through later writings about the planet and its inhabitants. His claims helped transform Aura Rhanes from a character within a single encounter story into a recurring figure within contactee culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Bethurum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Bethurum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman Bethurum</span></span></span>

## Bethurum’s descriptions of encounters

### The Nevada meetings and the “Admiral’s Scow”

Bethurum said the first encounter occurred in July 1952 while he was working in the Nevada desert. He claimed that after being awakened by small humanoid beings, he was taken to a saucer-shaped craft where he met the crew and its commander, Aura Rhanes. He later presented this event as the beginning of a series of contacts rather than an isolated experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Bethurum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Bethurum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman Bethurum</span></span></span>

The craft itself became part of the identity of the story. Bethurum described the vehicle as a large flying saucer operated by the Clarion crew, and his descriptions contributed to the popular image of the “friendly spacecraft visitor” that became common in 1950s UFO culture. Unlike some contemporary reports focused on fear or military secrecy, Bethurum’s account presented the craft as a vehicle of communication and instruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

A significant feature of the story was the level of personal detail Bethurum provided. He described conversations, the appearance of the crew, and Rhanes’ manner of speaking. For supporters of contactee accounts, such detail suggested a consistent personal experience. For critics, however, the lack of independent witnesses or physical evidence meant that the details could not be externally verified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Bethurum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Bethurum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman Bethurum</span></span></span>

### From personal testimony to published contact narrative

Bethurum’s claims moved from private experience into public culture through UFO groups, lectures, and publications. A shortened version of his story appeared before his book, and *Aboard a Flying Saucer* brought the Aura Rhanes narrative to a wider audience in 1954. The book established Bethurum as one of the better-known contactees of the decade.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

The publication of the account also fixed many details of the Clarion story in UFO literature. Later discussions of Bethurum’s case generally focused on the same elements: the female commander, the peaceful planet, the hidden location of Clarion, and the absence of physical evidence that could confirm the encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Bethurum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Bethurum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman Bethurum</span></span></span>

## The hidden planet claim

### What Bethurum said about Clarion

The most controversial part of Aura Rhanes’ reported message was the existence of Clarion. Bethurum claimed that Clarion was a planet within the Solar System but remained unknown because it was hidden from Earth’s view. Some summaries of his account describe it as being positioned beyond or on the far side of the Moon from Earth’s perspective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

Bethurum portrayed Clarion as a highly advanced and peaceful world. ### Why Clarion became the main point of criticism

Astronomers and UFO critics regarded the Clarion claim as one of the weakest parts of Bethurum’s story. By the 1950s, observations of the Solar System had already identified the known planets and provided increasingly accurate methods for detecting additional bodies. ## Why Aura Rhanes remains significant in UFO history

Aura Rhanes occupies an unusual place in UFO history because she represents the shift from UFO reports about objects to narratives about relationships with alleged non-human visitors.     try { tz = String(Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone || ''); } catch (err) {}
    if (!tz) return '';
    for (var i = 0; i< timezoneRules.length; i += 1) {
      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};
      try {
        if (new RegExp(rule.pattern).test(tz)) return rule.market;
      } catch (err) {}
    }
    return '';
  }
  function inferMarket(available, options) {
    available = Array.isArray(available) ? available : [];
    options = options || {};
    var storageKey = options.storageKey || 'phoenix-affiliate-market';
    try {
      var saved = window.localStorage && localStorage.getItem(storageKey);
      if (saved && available.indexOf(saved) >= 0) return saved;
    } catch (err) {}
    var timezoneMarket = inferFromTimezone();
    if (timezoneMarket && available.indexOf(timezoneMarket) >= 0) return timezoneMarket;
    var localeMarkets = localeMatches();
    if (localeMarkets.exact && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.exact) >= 0) return localeMarkets.exact;
    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
    if (options.defaultMarket && available.indexOf(options.defaultMarket) >= 0) return options.defaultMarket;
    return available[0] || '';
  }

    inferMarket: inferMarket,
    inferFromLocale: inferFromLocale,
    inferFromTimezone: inferFromTimezone
