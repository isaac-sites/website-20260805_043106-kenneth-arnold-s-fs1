## Introduction

On 24 April 1964, Socorro police officer Lonnie Zamora reported one of the most closely documented close-range UFO encounters in the Project Blue Book files. What distinguishes his testimony is not simply the claim that he saw an unusual object, but that his account unfolded in a sequence of rapidly changing observations recorded within hours of the event. His description evolved through distinct phases: a pursuit interrupted by a loud roar and descending flame, the discovery of an apparently grounded object with two nearby figures, a powered lift-off accompanied by intense noise and flame, and finally a silent departure over the surrounding landscape. Because investigators interviewed Zamora immediately and compared his statements against the physical scene, the chronology itself became one of the strongest aspects of the Socorro case, regardless of how the object is ultimately interpreted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## The pursuit, roar, and descending flame

Late in the afternoon, Zamora was driving through Socorro while pursuing a speeding black Chevrolet. During the chase he heard a sudden, exceptionally loud roar from the south-west. At roughly the same time he noticed what he described as a bluish flame with orange colouring descending behind a ridge.

His immediate assumption was entirely conventional. A dynamite storage area lay in that direction, and he believed an explosion or industrial accident might have occurred. Rather than continuing the traffic pursuit, he diverted towards the source of the apparent emergency. This decision is significant because it places the beginning of the encounter within the normal duties of a police officer rather than an attempt to investigate something unusual. Investigators later regarded this as an important element in assessing the reliability of the sequence of events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

Reaching the area was not straightforward. Zamora drove along rough, sandy tracks and struggled to climb a steep embankment in his patrol car. Those difficulties explain why several minutes elapsed between the initial roar and his first clear view into the arroyo below.

## The grounded object and two figures

When Zamora finally gained a better vantage point, he did not immediately think he was looking at an aircraft or unknown craft. His first impression was that an automobile had overturned in the gully.

As he looked more carefully, he realised the object appeared smooth, white or aluminium-coloured, and unlike any vehicle he recognised. It was oval or egg-shaped, without visible wings, windows or obvious external equipment. Later interviews consistently describe it as resting on landing supports rather than wheels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

Near the object he briefly observed two figures dressed in white. His earliest statements are often more restrained than later popular retellings. He did not initially describe obviously alien beings. Instead, he said they appeared human in shape, perhaps small adults or large children wearing white coveralls.

One figure appeared to notice his patrol car and react with surprise. Zamora's observation of the figures lasted only a few seconds before his attention shifted to driving and radio communication.

### Why the figures remain controversial

The figures occupy a central place in later discussions because they introduce ambiguity into the encounter.

Several factors limit the certainty of this part of the testimony:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* They were viewed from a considerable distance.
* The observation lasted only moments.
* Zamora never reported seeing detailed facial features.
* He consistently avoided embellishing the description in later interviews.

</div>

This restraint has often been cited by both supporters and sceptics. Those who regard Zamora as a careful witness argue that he reported only what he believed he had actually seen. Critics counter that brief observations made under stress are especially vulnerable to misinterpretation.

## Approaching the object

Believing there might still be injured occupants, Zamora radioed dispatch before moving closer.

As he approached on foot, he reported hearing several sharp metallic sounds, comparing them to heavy doors being slammed shut. By the time he reached a closer position, the two figures had disappeared from view.

One additional observation became important during later investigation. Zamora reported a red marking on the side of the object, resembling a geometric emblem rather than ordinary lettering. Different sketches produced during the investigation vary slightly, but all depict a simple angular symbol rather than an identifiable logo or national marking. Investigators were unable to match it conclusively with known military or civilian aircraft insignia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## The powered ascent

The encounter changed dramatically only seconds later.

Without warning, Zamora heard the loud roar again. Looking towards the object, he saw a bright flame emerging beneath it. He described the flame as predominantly light blue with orange colouring near its lower portion.

At this point he feared an explosion and retreated behind his patrol car for cover. During this hurried movement he lost his glasses, a detail repeated consistently in official interviews.

Unlike many later UFO accounts involving silent hovering, Zamora consistently described the initial lift-off as noisy and apparently powered. The flame disturbed dust and loose material beneath the object, yet he specifically noted an absence of dense smoke. That combination—visible flame, dust disturbance and limited smoke—has featured prominently in both technical analyses and sceptical [explanations]({{ 'explanations/' | relative_url }}) ever since.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## The silent departure

After rising only a short distance above the ground, the character of the event appeared to change.

According to Zamora, the loud roar ceased completely. The visible flame disappeared, yet the object continued moving smoothly at low altitude.

He watched it travel south-west, passing beyond a nearby dynamite shack before gradually climbing away over higher ground.

This final phase is important because Zamora distinguished clearly between powered lift-off and later flight:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Initial ascent:** loud roar, visible blue-orange flame and disturbed dust.
* **Low-level flight:** no audible engine noise.
* **Departure:** smooth acceleration and continued movement without visible exhaust.

</div>

Investigators regarded this distinction as one of the more internally consistent aspects of his testimony because it remained stable across repeated interviews.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## How the encounter unfolded minute by minute

Although exact timings vary slightly between reconstructions, the encounter can be summarised as follows:

## What changed as Zamora's viewpoint changed

One notable feature of the testimony is that Zamora's description evolved naturally with distance and viewing angle rather than remaining fixed.

Initially he believed he had found an overturned vehicle. As he approached, the shape resolved into a smooth oval object. During departure he compared its distant appearance to a balloon, a comparison that referred to its silhouette rather than its identity.

These changing descriptions are often misunderstood. They do not necessarily indicate contradictory testimony. Instead, they illustrate how perception altered as the object occupied different positions relative to the witness and surrounding terrain.

Investigators generally regarded this progression as compatible with an observer attempting to describe an unfamiliar object under rapidly changing circumstances rather than repeating a rehearsed narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## Why Zamora's account remains influential

The strength of Zamora's eyewitness account lies less in any single extraordinary claim than in the combination of circumstances surrounding it.

His statement was recorded within hours of the event, before extensive media attention developed. The timeline was supported by contemporaneous radio traffic, the arrival of other officers shortly afterwards, and subsequent examination of the location where ground marks and scorched vegetation were documented. While those physical traces are examined separately within the wider Socorro case, they provided investigators with a chronology against which to test Zamora's recollections rather than relying solely on memory recorded long afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

The encounter nevertheless leaves important uncertainties. Only one person observed every stage of the event, the figures were seen briefly at distance, and no photographs or independent recordings exist. These limitations mean the account cannot conclusively establish what the object was. They also explain why competing interpretations—including an experimental aerospace vehicle, a hoax, misidentification or a genuinely unexplained phenomenon—continue to be debated.

Even so, the encounter timeline remains one of the most carefully documented eyewitness narratives in the history of Project Blue Book because its sequence was recorded promptly, internally consistent across multiple interviews, and closely examined by official investigators from the earliest stages of the investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>
