## Introduction

Claude Vorilhon’s account of the 1973 Puy de Lassolas encounter is the defining testimony behind Raëlism. According to Vorilhon, then a French motoring journalist, he met an extraterrestrial being from a civilisation called the Elohim on 13 December 1973 near the volcanic crater of Puy de Lassolas in central France. He later said the encounter was followed by a series of meetings in which the visitor revealed a message about humanity’s origins, science, religion and the future relationship between Earth and the Elohim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rael.ch/rael" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rael.ch">[RAEL.ORG]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rael.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Messenger of the Elohim - The Raelian Movement...</span></span></span>

The importance of the testimony lies not in independently verified evidence of an extraterrestrial visit—no physical object, scientific measurement or external witness has confirmed Vorilhon’s account—but in how the narrative developed into the foundation of a new religious movement. Scholars of new religious movements generally examine the episode as a case in which a personal encounter claim became a source of religious authority, doctrine and community formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/book/8388/chapter-abstract/154111792" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP Academic“Those who came from the sky”: Ancient Astronauts and Creationism in the Raëlian Religion &#124; Controversial New Religions &#124; Oxf...</span></span></span>

## What Vorilhon Said Happened at Puy de Lassolas

### The December 1973 encounter account

Vorilhon’s reported experience begins on 13 December 1973, when he said he was at Puy de Lassolas, an extinct volcanic site in the Auvergne region of France. In his later accounts, he described seeing a craft and meeting a humanoid figure who communicated with him in French. Vorilhon identified this being as a member of the Elohim, a technologically advanced civilisation that he said had created life on Earth through scientific means rather than supernatural processes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rael.ch/rael" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rael.ch">[RAEL.ORG]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rael.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Messenger of the Elohim - The Raelian Movement...</span></span></span>

According to Vorilhon’s testimony, the visitor did not present himself as a traditional religious figure. Instead, the being explained that many ancient religious ideas were misunderstandings of earlier contacts between humanity and the Elohim. In this interpretation, figures such as prophets and biblical characters were connected to encounters with an advanced extraterrestrial civilisation whose technology had been mistaken for divine power.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259732025_Extraterrestrial_Exegesis_The_Raelian_Movement_as_a_Biblical_Religion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The encounter became the turning point in Vorilhon’s life story. He later said that he moved away from his previous career and adopted the name Raël, presenting himself as the messenger responsible for communicating the Elohim’s message to humanity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rael.ch/rael" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rael.ch">[RAEL.ORG]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rael.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Messenger of the Elohim - The Raelian Movement...</span></span></span>

## The claimed Elohim meetings and message

Vorilhon’s account extends beyond a single meeting. He claimed that after the initial encounter he met the being repeatedly over a period of days, receiving further explanations about human origins, the history of religion and humanity’s future. The Raëlian Movement describes these as six consecutive meetings at the same location, during which Vorilhon said he was given a mission to prepare humanity for future contact with the Elohim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rael.ch/rael" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rael.ch">[RAEL.ORG]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rael.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Messenger of the Elohim - The Raelian Movement...</span></span></span>

The central elements of the claimed message included several ideas that later became core Raëlian teachings:

* **Humanity as a scientific creation:** Vorilhon said the Elohim had created life on Earth through advanced genetic engineering, presenting creation as a scientific process rather than a supernatural act.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/book/8388/chapter-abstract/154111792" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP Academic“Those who came from the sky”: Ancient Astronauts and Creationism in the Raëlian Religion &#124; Controversial New Religions &#124; Oxf...</span></span></span>
* **Ancient religions as misunderstood contact events:** He argued that references to gods, angels and miracles in religious traditions reflected encounters with technologically superior beings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259732025_Extraterrestrial_Exegesis_The_Raelian_Movement_as_a_Biblical_Religion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>
* **A future relationship with the Elohim:** The message presented humanity as needing to become sufficiently peaceful and scientifically advanced to welcome the return of its creators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://xps-venus.rael.org/rael/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: xps-venus.rael.org">[xps-venus.rael.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">xps-venus.rael.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Last Prophet, Rael</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Last Prophet, Rael</span></span></span>

Vorilhon published his first major account of these teachings in *Le Livre qui dit la vérité* (*The Book Which Tells the Truth*) in 1974. The book transformed a private testimony into a public religious claim and provided the doctrinal foundation for [the movement]({{ 'the-movement/' | relative_url }}) that followed. Scholars have noted that the encounter narrative gave Vorilhon a form of prophetic authority: his status as messenger depended directly on the claimed personal communication with the Elohim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259732025_Extraterrestrial_Exegesis_The_Raelian_Movement_as_a_Biblical_Religion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

## How the story developed over time

The interpretation of Vorilhon’s testimony changed as the Raëlian movement expanded. The original claim of a roadside or volcanic encounter developed into a broader worldview involving extraterrestrial creation, reinterpretations of biblical history and a vision of humanity’s technological future. Researchers studying Raëlism have described this process as an example of how contact narratives can evolve from individual experiences into organised belief systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/book/8388/chapter-abstract/154111792" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP Academic“Those who came from the sky”: Ancient Astronauts and Creationism in the Raëlian Religion &#124; Controversial New Religions &#124; Oxf...</span></span></span>

A significant feature of the story is the way Vorilhon combined UFO contact themes with religious language while also rejecting conventional supernatural explanations. The Elohim were presented not as gods but as advanced beings whose abilities appeared divine only because of the technological gap between them and ancient humans. This blending of extraterrestrial ideas and religious interpretation has led scholars to classify Raëlism among UFO-based new religious movements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/book/8388/chapter-abstract/154111792" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP Academic“Those who came from the sky”: Ancient Astronauts and Creationism in the Raëlian Religion &#124; Controversial New Religions &#124; Oxf...</span></span></span>

The claimed meetings also shaped Vorilhon’s public identity. His authority within the movement rests primarily on his role as the person who received and transmitted the message. From a historical perspective, the strongest evidence available concerns the existence and influence of the testimony itself: Vorilhon consistently promoted the account through books, interviews and organisational activities. The physical reality of the encounter remains a matter of belief rather than independently demonstrated fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mtlreviewofbooks.ca/reviews/aliens-adored-raels-ufo-religion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mtlreviewofbooks.ca">[Montreal Review of Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mtlreviewofbooks.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on mtlreviewofbooks.ca.</span></span></span>

## Assessing the testimony: belief, evidence and uncertainty

The Puy de Lassolas account occupies a distinctive place in UFO history because it is both a claimed close encounter and the origin story of a continuing religious movement. Supporters of Raëlism view Vorilhon’s experience as a genuine revelation and the foundation of a message about science and humanity’s future. The movement continues to commemorate the event as its first encounter with the Elohim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rael.org/events/raelian-celebration-of-the-first-encounter/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rael.org">[RAEL.ORG]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rael.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Celebration of The First Encounter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Celebration of The First Encounter</span></span></span>

From a critical historical perspective, however, the available evidence is limited to Vorilhon’s own testimony and later movement literature. There is no independent physical evidence demonstrating that an extraterrestrial visitor appeared at Puy de Lassolas, and no external witness has verified the claimed conversations. Scholars therefore tend to study the encounter less as a confirmed extraterrestrial event and more as a significant example of how personal testimony, cultural ideas about space exploration and religious interpretation can combine to create a lasting movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/book/8388/chapter-abstract/154111792" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP Academic“Those who came from the sky”: Ancient Astronauts and Creationism in the Raëlian Religion &#124; Controversial New Religions &#124; Oxf...</span></span></span>
