## Introduction

The U.S. Navy investigation into the loss of Flight 19 produced a detailed documentary record of aircraft movements, radio communications, witness testimony, weather conditions, and search operations. Its central finding was that the five TBM Avenger aircraft most likely became lost because of navigation errors, communication difficulties, and deteriorating conditions, rather than because of an unexplained event. However, the investigation also acknowledged an important limitation: no aircraft wreckage or definitive physical evidence from the Avengers was recovered, leaving the final sequence uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/news-and-events/news/2024/nhm-120324.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command Navy History Matters</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandNavy History MattersDecember 3, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 2024</span></span></span>

The surviving investigation record is therefore a mixture of strong evidence and unavoidable gaps. Radio logs and witness accounts provide a clear picture of increasing confusion during the flight, while the absence of wreckage prevents investigators from proving the exact moment or manner in which the aircraft were lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/l/loss-of-flight-19-official-accident-reports.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command Loss of Flight 19 Official Accident Reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandLoss of Flight 19 Official Accident ReportsOctober 7, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 7, 2019</span></span></span>

## Accident board evidence

### What investigators examined after the disappearance

The Navy convened a Board of Investigation at Naval Air Station Jacksonville on 7 December 1945 to examine the disappearance of five TBM Avengers from Naval Air Station Fort Lauderdale and the later loss of a PBM Mariner rescue aircraft. The board reviewed aircraft records, operational plans, weather information, radio transmissions, testimony from naval personnel, and search reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/rep/Flight19/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ibiblio.org">[Ibiblio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ibiblio.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HyperWar: Findings of the Board of Investigation Into the Loss of Flight 19 (Bermuda Triangle)December 7, 1945...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 1945</span></span></span>

A key part of the record was the original training plan, known as Navigation Problem No. 1. The Avengers were not on a mysterious patrol or experimental mission; they were following a standard over-water navigation exercise involving a route to the Bahamas area, a bombing practice point, and a planned return to Fort Lauderdale. The investigation confirmed that the flight departed as an authorised training mission at approximately 1410 local time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/rep/Flight19/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ibiblio.org">[Ibiblio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ibiblio.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HyperWar: Findings of the Board of Investigation Into the Loss of Flight 19 (Bermuda Triangle)December 7, 1945...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 1945</span></span></span>

The board also examined the condition of the aircraft before departure. The available records indicated that the Avengers had undergone normal pre-flight preparation, had fuel supplies sufficient for the planned mission, and carried required survival equipment. One unusual detail was that the aircraft lacked their 24-hour clocks, which were commonly removed as souvenirs or stolen items, but investigators did not treat this as a cause of the accident because pilots were expected to carry personal watches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-057/h-057-4.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span></span></span>

The evidence did not support a finding that the aircraft suffered from a widespread mechanical failure. Instead, investigators focused on how navigation decisions developed after the flight left the planned route. The official record increasingly centred on the belief that Flight 19’s leader, Lieutenant Charles C. Taylor, became uncertain of the aircraft’s position and continued making decisions based on an incorrect estimate of where the formation was located.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/news-and-events/news/2024/nhm-120324.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command Navy History Matters</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandNavy History MattersDecember 3, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 2024</span></span></span>

## Navigation and communication issues

### Radio transmissions became the central evidence trail

Because no wreckage was recovered, the most important surviving evidence came from radio communications intercepted by naval stations and other aircraft. These transmissions showed that the flight completed the early portion of the exercise but later became confused about its location. Another Navy pilot, Lieutenant Robert F. Cox, heard the transmissions and recognised that the group appeared to be in distress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/l/lost-patrol.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command Lost Patrol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command Lost Patrol</span></span></span>

The investigation concluded that Taylor apparently believed the flight was near the Florida Keys when it was actually in the Bahamas region. This incorrect assumption affected the decisions that followed. Instead of directing the formation toward the Florida mainland, the flight continued on a course that moved it farther away from a safe return route. The Naval History and Heritage Command later summarised the official assessment as a combination of faulty navigation and communication problems, including failure to change to the appropriate radio frequency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/news-and-events/news/2024/nhm-120324.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command Navy History Matters</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandNavy History MattersDecember 3, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 2024</span></span></span>

Communication difficulties were significant because rescue assistance depended on maintaining accurate contact. The Navy attempted to guide Flight 19 and obtain position information, but the situation became increasingly difficult as the aircraft remained uncertain about their location. The flight’s final transmissions suggested worsening confusion and declining confidence among the crew.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/disasters-and-phenomena/flight-19.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command The Loss of Flight 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command The Loss of Flight 19</span></span></span>

The investigation also considered the role of equipment limitations. In 1945, over-water navigation relied heavily on magnetic compasses, estimated position calculations, timing, fuel management, and visual observations. A mistaken identification of land features could create a chain of errors that became increasingly difficult to correct over open ocean. The Navy’s assessment reflected this reality: the loss was treated as a navigational accident amplified by environmental and communication factors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-057/h-057-4.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span></span></span>

### The question of compass failure

A recurring issue in later discussions of Flight 19 has been whether Taylor’s report that his compasses had failed was the primary cause of the disaster. The investigation record did consider possible instrument problems, but it did not establish that all navigation equipment had failed. Instead, investigators focused on the broader problem of position uncertainty and the decisions made after the flight became disoriented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-057/h-057-4.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because a compass problem alone would not necessarily have doomed the flight. The Navy’s analysis suggested that the critical failure was not simply a broken instrument but the inability to establish a reliable position after the pilots became unsure of their location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/news-and-events/news/2024/nhm-120324.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command Navy History Matters</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandNavy History MattersDecember 3, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 2024</span></span></span>

## Missing aircraft and unresolved details

### Why the final conclusion remained incomplete


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The greatest limitation in the Navy investigation was the absence of physical evidence. Despite an extensive search involving Navy, Coast Guard, and other aviation resources, no confirmed wreckage, bodies, or aircraft components from the five Avengers were recovered. The search covered a vast area of ocean and continued for days, but produced no conclusive discovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/disasters-and-phenomena/flight-19.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command The Loss of Flight 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command The Loss of Flight 19</span></span></span>

</div>

This lack of recovery prevented investigators from answering several important questions. The exact location where Flight 19 ran out of fuel, whether all five aircraft ditched together, and how long any survivors may have remained in the water could not be determined from physical evidence. The board could reconstruct the likely chain of events but not verify the final moments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-057/h-057-4.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span></span></span>

The rescue aircraft loss created a second investigation problem. A PBM Mariner sent to search for Flight 19 also disappeared. Unlike the Avengers, however, there was evidence suggesting a possible explanation: witnesses reported seeing a fireball and an oil slick in the area where the rescue aircraft was believed to have gone down. Later Navy analysis considered an in-flight fire, possibly related to fuel vapours, as the most likely explanation, although the exact cause was never conclusively established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-057/h-057-4.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span></span></span>

### What remained officially unresolved

The Navy’s findings were not a claim that every detail had been solved. The official record established the most probable causes but retained uncertainty because the main aircraft were never found. Later summaries from the Naval History and Heritage Command have emphasised that the case remains historically notable precisely because the documentary evidence points toward a conventional aviation accident while the missing wreckage leaves unanswered questions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-057/h-057-4.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command H-057-4: The Disappearance of Flight 19</span></span></span>

The evidence record therefore supports a balanced interpretation. The Navy investigation identified a credible chain involving navigation error, mistaken position assessment, communication difficulties, and worsening conditions. At the same time, the lack of physical recovery means the investigation could not provide a complete reconstruction of the final minutes of Flight 19.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/news-and-events/news/2024/nhm-120324.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command Navy History Matters</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandNavy History MattersDecember 3, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 2024</span></span></span>
