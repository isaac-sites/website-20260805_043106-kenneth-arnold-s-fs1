## Introduction

The soil trace left at the Delphos Ring incident in 1971 is the part of the case that transformed it from an ordinary UFO report into a long-running scientific dispute. The reported object itself was seen by [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-0a727c/' | relative_url }}), but the circular mark on the ground created something that investigators could photograph, sample, and analyse. Laboratory studies of the preserved soil found genuine differences between the ring material and nearby control samples, including strong water repellency, altered chemistry, and unusual organic compounds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/JUFOS/1991_NS_vol3_JUFOS.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesISSN: 0730-5748October 31, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2025</span></span></span>

Those findings remain important because they demonstrate a physical anomaly, but they do not by themselves establish what caused it. Supporters of an extraordinary explanation argue that the combination of a sharply defined ring, persistent dryness, and unusual chemistry is difficult to explain. Sceptical interpretations point to known biological processes, especially fungal or microbial “fairy rings”, which can also alter soil chemistry and create water-repellent patches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/JUFOS/1991_NS_vol3_JUFOS.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesISSN: 0730-5748October 31, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2025</span></span></span> The Delphos soil trace therefore remains a useful example of the difference between finding an unexplained physical effect and proving the origin of that effect.

## The circular ground formation

The physical trace was discovered shortly after Ronald Johnson reported seeing a glowing object hovering near his family’s farm outside Delphos, Kansas, on 2 November 1971. When the family returned to the location, they found a pale circular area of soil roughly eight feet in diameter. The ring was described as unusually bright and crust-like, with the affected area appearing different from the surrounding ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/analysis-soil-samples-related-delphos-kansas-november-2-1971/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Analysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultAnalysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas November 2, 1971 - The Black Vault Case Files...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 1971</span></span></span>

The feature attracted attention because it appeared to persist rather than disappear after the sighting. Investigator Ted Phillips, who specialised in collecting physical samples from UFO-related trace cases, visited the site several weeks later. He photographed the formation and collected samples from inside the ring along with comparison soil from outside the affected area. These control samples became important because later analyses relied on differences between the two soils rather than on observations of the ring alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/JUFOS/1991_NS_vol3_JUFOS.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesISSN: 0730-5748October 31, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2025</span></span></span>

One of the most frequently reported observations was the ring soil’s resistance to water. Investigators stated that water did not penetrate the affected soil in the same way as the surrounding ground. Later accounts described the ring remaining dry while nearby soil became wet, including observations after snow and rain. The behaviour suggested that the soil particles had acquired some kind of water-repellent coating or structural change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Delphos_Kansas_landing_ring.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Delphos, Kansas Landing Ring</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Delphos, Kansas Landing Ring...</span></span></span>

This feature made Delphos unusual among UFO reports. Many alleged close encounters leave only testimony or photographs of lights; Delphos produced a physical sample that could be stored and repeatedly examined. However, the existence of the trace and the interpretation of the trace are separate questions. Laboratory work could determine what was in the soil, but not automatically identify what process created it.

## Sample collection and testing

The Delphos samples underwent several rounds of examination by different laboratories. A later compilation published in the *Journal of UFO Studies* brought together analyses requested by J. Allen Hynek and Ted Phillips, noting that multiple laboratories had examined material connected with the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/JUFOS/1991_NS_vol3_JUFOS.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesISSN: 0730-5748October 31, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2025</span></span></span>

The reported findings from the analyses included several recurring observations:

* **Water repellency:** The ring soil resisted absorbing water compared with nearby control samples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://iccra.org/bystate/Kansas/ICCRA%20-%20KS%20-%20Delphos%2C%20Ottawa%20County%20%28November%202%2C%201971%29.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iccra.org">[iccra.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iccra.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kansas - Delphos, Ottawa County (November 2, 1971)August 10, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 1971</span></span></span>
* **Chemical differences:** Analysts reported higher levels of soluble salts and increased calcium compared with surrounding soil. Some summaries of the laboratory work reported calcium levels several times higher than control samples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://iccra.org/bystate/Kansas/ICCRA%20-%20KS%20-%20Delphos%2C%20Ottawa%20County%20%28November%202%2C%201971%29.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iccra.org">[iccra.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iccra.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kansas - Delphos, Ottawa County (November 2, 1971)August 10, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 1971</span></span></span>
* **Organic coatings:** Investigators identified organic material coating soil particles, which was considered a possible explanation for the hydrophobic behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/analysis-soil-samples-related-delphos-kansas-november-2-1971/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Analysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultAnalysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas November 2, 1971 - The Black Vault Case Files...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 1971</span></span></span>
* **No evidence of simple heat damage:** Later analysis did not find signs that the soil had been exposed to a high-temperature event such as intense burning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/analysis-soil-samples-related-delphos-kansas-november-2-1971/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Analysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultAnalysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas November 2, 1971 - The Black Vault Case Files...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 1971</span></span></span>

A major later study came from chemist Erol Faruk, who examined preserved Delphos samples and published his findings in the *Journal of UFO Studies*. Faruk reported that the soil contained a water-soluble organic compound associated with the unusual hydrophobic behaviour. His analysis suggested that the material behaved like a metal salt of an organic acid and could explain some of the physical properties observed in the ring soil.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/JUFOS/1989_NS_vol1_JUFOS.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies JOURNAL OF UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO Studies JOURNAL OF UFO</span></span></span>

The importance of Faruk’s work was not that it identified an extraterrestrial substance, but that it proposed a chemical mechanism for how ordinary-looking soil could become unusually water-resistant. His interpretation focused on the interaction between organic material and mineral particles rather than on simply labelling the trace as unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/JUFOS/1989_NS_vol1_JUFOS.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies JOURNAL OF UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO Studies JOURNAL OF UFO</span></span></span>

## Unusual soil properties and limits

The most debated aspect of the Delphos samples is that the same evidence can support very different interpretations.

### Why the soil findings attracted attention

For supporters of the UFO interpretation, the strongest point is that the ring was not merely a visual impression. The soil displayed measurable differences from nearby ground, and investigators preserved samples long after the event. ### Why natural explanations remain possible

The main conventional explanation involves biological soil processes, especially fairy rings produced by fungi or related organisms. These formations are well documented in nature: fungal growth can spread in circular patterns, alter vegetation, change soil chemistry, and create compounds that affect water movement through soil.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/JUFOS/1991_NS_vol3_JUFOS.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesISSN: 0730-5748October 31, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2025</span></span></span>

## What the Delphos soil trace actually established

The laboratory findings changed the status of the Delphos incident by showing that investigators were dealing with a real physical feature rather than only a story about a sighting. The ring soil was measurably different from nearby soil, and decades of discussion have focused on understanding those differences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/JUFOS/1991_NS_vol3_JUFOS.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesISSN: 0730-5748October 31, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2025</span></span></span>

What remains unresolved is the cause. The soil analyses demonstrate alteration, but they do not uniquely identify the process responsible. The evidence is consistent with several possibilities, ranging from unusual biological activity to an unknown external influence. The strongest conclusion supported by the laboratory record is therefore narrower than the most famous claim: Delphos left behind an unusual and studied soil trace, but the trace alone does not prove what created it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/analysis-soil-samples-related-delphos-kansas-november-2-1971/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Analysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultAnalysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas November 2, 1971 - The Black Vault Case Files...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 1971</span></span></span>
