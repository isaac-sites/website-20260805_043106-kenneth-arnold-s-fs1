## Introduction

The physical traces at the Socorro landing site are among the strongest reasons the 24 April 1964 Lonnie Zamora case remains significant. Unlike many UFO reports that rely entirely on witness testimony, investigators reached the location within minutes of Zamora's radio call and documented fresh ground disturbances and scorched vegetation before the site had been substantially altered. Those observations provided independent support for parts of Zamora's account that an object had rested on the ground and departed with an intense flame. At the same time, the traces did not identify what produced them. No recovered material, measurable residue or diagnostic engineering evidence linked the marks to any known vehicle—or to anything extraordinary. As a result, the physical evidence occupies an unusual middle ground: stronger than eyewitness testimony alone, but far short of proving the nature of the object that allegedly created it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## Four depressions and circular marks

The most frequently discussed physical evidence consisted of four fresh ground depressions arranged in a roughly symmetrical pattern. Investigators from local law enforcement, the FBI and Project Blue Book all documented these impressions shortly after the event. According to the FBI report, each depression was approximately 16 by 6 inches (about 40 by 15 cm), around 2 inches deep, and appeared to have been made by an object entering the soil at an angle rather than straight down. Earth had been displaced consistently to one side of each mark, suggesting a substantial downward load.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

The spacing and arrangement of the four depressions broadly matched Zamora's description of an object standing on multiple supports. Investigators also noted three smaller circular impressions, each roughly 4 inches (10 cm) across and only lightly pressed into the sandy ground. Their relationship to the larger depressions has never been established with certainty. They were documented but not confidently interpreted as landing gear, footprints or unrelated surface features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

One important aspect of the case is chronology. These marks were not discovered days later after rumours had spread. Officers were directed to them by Zamora almost immediately after the reported departure of the object, reducing—but not eliminating—the possibility that they resulted from later contamination or deliberate fabrication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## Burned grass and disturbed soil

The second major category of evidence involved vegetation rather than soil compression.

Investigators found several patches of burned grass within the area enclosed by the depressions, along with at least one additional burned patch outside that pattern. Significantly, surrounding vegetation remained largely unaffected. Instead of a broad brush fire, the damage appeared localised to individual clumps, suggesting a brief and concentrated heat source rather than a spreading ground fire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

This pattern was broadly consistent with Zamora's report that the object departed using a loud blue-orange flame directed downward for only a short period. However, the observations were qualitative rather than instrumental. Investigators did not recover measurable combustion products that uniquely identified rocket exhaust, chemical propellants or any unconventional energy source. The burned vegetation therefore corroborated that heating had occurred but did not establish its origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

The disturbed soil also attracted attention because it appeared concentrated around the depressions rather than scattered randomly. Nevertheless, no laboratory analysis demonstrated unusual mineral changes or exotic residues that could distinguish the site from damage caused by more ordinary heat-producing equipment. Claims made in later popular accounts about extraordinary laboratory findings have not been supported by the primary investigative documents. The contemporary reports remain notably restrained in their conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## Why investigators regarded the traces as significant

The physical traces mattered for several reasons beyond their appearance.

First, they provided evidence independent of Zamora's memory. Even if every aspect of his visual observation were questioned, investigators could still verify that something had recently disturbed the ground and scorched vegetation at the location he identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

Second, the evidence was examined unusually quickly. FBI Special Agent D. Arthur Byrnes Jr. happened to be in Socorro on unrelated business and reached the area shortly after local officers, allowing federal documentation before extensive public access or prolonged environmental exposure. This rapid response distinguishes Socorro from many historical UFO reports where physical evidence was recorded only after considerable delay.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

Third, multiple investigators from different agencies observed substantially the same features. While agreement among observers does not establish the cause of the traces, it increases confidence that the depressions and burned vegetation themselves were genuine physical conditions rather than later embellishments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

## Skeptical interpretations of the ground evidence

The physical traces have also been central to sceptical analyses because they demonstrate that real evidence existed while leaving open conventional [explanations]({{ 'explanations/' | relative_url }}).

Several hypotheses have been proposed over the decades:

* **Aircraft or experimental equipment:** Some writers suggested that an experimental vehicle from nearby military facilities might have produced landing marks and exhaust damage. However, no documentary evidence has confirmed that such a vehicle was operating at the location and time in question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://decur.org/documents/socorro-blue-book-investigation-1964" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decur.org">[DECUR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Socorro / Zamora Blue Book Investigation Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Socorro / Zamora Blue Book Investigation Report - DECUR...</span></span></span>
* **Heavy machinery or terrestrial vehicles:** Others have argued that construction or utility equipment could account for ground depressions. This explanation struggles to account for Zamora's immediate observations and the reported sequence of events but remains physically possible in principle if unrelated activities had occurred at the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://decur.org/documents/socorro-blue-book-investigation-1964" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decur.org">[DECUR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Socorro / Zamora Blue Book Investigation Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Socorro / Zamora Blue Book Investigation Report - DECUR...</span></span></span>
* **Student prank theories:** Later proposals involving a hoax by students from the nearby New Mexico Institute of Mining and Technology attempt to explain both the visual sighting and the physical traces together. These theories remain speculative because no contemporary evidence demonstrated that such a prank occurred, and no participant conclusively established responsibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://decur.org/documents/socorro-blue-book-investigation-1964" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decur.org">[DECUR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Socorro / Zamora Blue Book Investigation Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Socorro / Zamora Blue Book Investigation Report - DECUR...</span></span></span>

Importantly, sceptical interpretations generally do not deny that the depressions and burned grass existed. Instead, they argue that these traces are compatible with ordinary causes even if the specific cause has never been identified.

## What the traces can and cannot prove

The Socorro landing site illustrates both the strengths and limitations of physical evidence in an anomalous event investigation.

The traces can reasonably support several conclusions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Something disturbed the ground before investigators arrived.
* Localised burning affected vegetation in the reported landing area.
* The physical scene broadly matched important elements of Zamora's immediate description.
* Multiple investigators independently documented these conditions shortly after the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

</div>

However, the evidence cannot establish several much stronger claims:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It does not identify the object responsible for the marks.
* It does not demonstrate non-human technology.
* It does not exclude all conventional explanations.
* It provides no recoverable manufactured material, propulsion residue or engineering signature that could be independently analysed decades later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>

</div>

This distinction explains why the Socorro case continues to receive serious attention from both supporters and sceptics. The physical evidence is stronger than in most classic UFO reports because it exists independently of the witness's testimony, yet it remains insufficient to determine what actually produced the observed traces. Within the broader Socorro dossier, the landing marks therefore serve primarily as corroborative evidence: they strengthen the claim that an unusual event occurred at the location, while leaving the identity and nature of its cause unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/62-HQ-83894/serial/438" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI Case 62-HQ-83894 Serial 438: Socorro, New Mexico UFO Sighting, Officer Lonnie Zamora, April 24, 1964 &#124; BlueBookApril 24, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 1964</span></span></span>
