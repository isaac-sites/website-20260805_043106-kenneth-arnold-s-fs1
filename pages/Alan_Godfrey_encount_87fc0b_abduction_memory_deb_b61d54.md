## Introduction

The most controversial part of the Alan Godfrey encounter is not the reported sighting itself, but what happened afterwards: hypnosis sessions transformed a missing-time incident into an alleged abduction narrative. Before regression, Godfrey described seeing an unusual object near Todmorden in 1980 and experiencing a gap in his memory. During later hypnotic sessions, he recalled a much more detailed sequence involving being taken aboard an object, encountering strange figures, and undergoing an examination. These memories became the foundation of the “abduction” interpretation of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/police-constable-alan-godfreys-abduction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">police constable alan godfreys abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1980: Police constable Alan Godfrey&#x27;s abductionApril 8, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that the abduction account depends almost entirely on hypnotically recovered memories rather than independently verifiable evidence. Supporters argue that hypnosis helped reveal details hidden by a traumatic experience; sceptics argue that hypnosis is not a reliable method for recovering forgotten events and can increase confidence in memories that are inaccurate. The Godfrey case therefore sits at the centre of a wider debate about whether recovered memories can uncover lost experiences or unintentionally create convincing narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40574575/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Does Hypnosis Aid Memory Retrieval?: A Review of Steven Jay Lynn&#x27;s Research - PubMedJune 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 18, 2025</span></span></span>

## Did hypnosis change the Godfrey story?

### Regression sessions and reported memories

Godfrey’s initial account involved a strange encounter, a period of missing time, and uncertainty about what occurred during the unexplained gap. According to accounts of the investigation, he later agreed to undergo hypnotic regression after investigators had recorded his conscious recollections. The first session took place several months after the incident, with later sessions conducted with other investigators and recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/police-constable-alan-godfreys-abduction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">police constable alan godfreys abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1980: Police constable Alan Godfrey&#x27;s abductionApril 8, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

During these sessions, Godfrey reportedly described events that went far beyond his original memory. The recovered narrative included being affected by a bright light, losing control of the situation, finding himself inside a strange environment, and encountering non-human or unusual beings. These details changed the nature of the case: what had initially been a report of an unexplained aerial encounter became classified by many UFO researchers as an alleged close encounter involving abduction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tvi.show/close-encounters/the-1980-todmorden-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tvi.show">[Things Visible &amp; Invisible]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tvi.show</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the 1980 todmorden incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Things Visible &amp; InvisibleThe 1980 Todmorden UFO Encounter: Policeman Alan Godfrey’s Close Encounter That Shook West Yorkshire...</span></span></span>

A key feature of the case is the difference between the two stages of testimony:

* **Contemporary account:** Godfrey reported an unusual object, a disruption in his experience, and missing time.
* **Post-hypnosis account:** The missing period was filled with a detailed abduction scenario.

This distinction matters because the later narrative is not simply a fuller version of the original report. It represents a major expansion of the remembered event, produced through a technique whose ability to recover accurate memories is disputed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40574575/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Does Hypnosis Aid Memory Retrieval?: A Review of Steven Jay Lynn&#x27;s Research - PubMedJune 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 18, 2025</span></span></span>

The recorded sessions became important to supporters because they provided a documented record of Godfrey describing the experience. They argue that the consistency of some details across sessions suggests that the memories represented a genuine underlying experience rather than a simple invention. However, critics note that consistency alone does not prove accuracy: once a person has formed a particular interpretation of an event, later recollections can remain stable while still being mistaken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40574575/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Does Hypnosis Aid Memory Retrieval?: A Review of Steven Jay Lynn&#x27;s Research - PubMedJune 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 18, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why recovered memories became the main point of dispute

### Problems with hypnosis and memory recovery

The Godfrey case emerged during a period when hypnotic regression was widely used by some UFO investigators and therapists as a way to explore missing-time experiences. The underlying assumption was that the mind might retain a complete record of an event that conscious memory had blocked or forgotten. Modern memory research has challenged that assumption.

Scientific reviews have found that hypnosis does not reliably improve accurate recall. Instead, it can encourage additional details, increase confidence, and make it harder to separate genuine memory from imagination or suggestion. The American Medical Association’s review of hypnotically refreshed recollection concluded that hypnosis could produce confabulations — invented or distorted details that fill gaps in memory — and could make recollections less reliable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3974082/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scientific status of refreshing recollection by the use of hypnosis. Council on Scientific Affairs - PubMed...</span></span></span>

This does not mean that every memory recalled under hypnosis is false. The problem is that there is no dependable way to identify which details are accurate and which may have been constructed during the process. Research on hypnosis and false memories has repeatedly shown that people can become highly confident in memories that are not supported by external evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1433762/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FrontiersFrontiers &#124; Remembering what did not happen: the role of hypnosis in memory recall and false memories formationFebruary 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2025</span></span></span>

For the Godfrey case, this creates a specific evidential problem. The alleged abduction occurred during the period for which there was no independent witness or physical record. The more extraordinary claims — the occupants, the interior of the craft, and the examination sequence — are also the claims most dependent on hypnotic recall. As a result, the strongest part of the abduction story is also the part most vulnerable to questions about memory reliability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/police-constable-alan-godfreys-abduction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">police constable alan godfreys abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1980: Police constable Alan Godfrey&#x27;s abductionApril 8, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

### The role of expectation and interpretation

Another issue is that memory does not operate like a recording device. People reconstruct past events using fragments of perception, emotion, later information, and existing beliefs. In unusual situations, especially those involving fear, confusion, or missing information, the mind may attempt to create a coherent explanation from incomplete material.

In the Godfrey investigation, the UFO-abduction framework was already part of the surrounding discussion before the regression accounts developed. Sceptics argue that this context may have influenced how ambiguous memories were interpreted. Supporters counter that Godfrey’s account contained unusual details that he did not appear to expect, and that his own uncertainty about whether the experience was physical, psychological, or something else should be considered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufocasebook.com/godfreyabduction.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufocasebook.com">[UFO Casebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufocasebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Casebook The Alan Godfrey Abduction,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO CasebookThe Alan Godfrey Abduction, November 28, 1980...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 28, 1980</span></span></span>

The controversy therefore is not simply whether Godfrey was truthful. Many commentators, including some who question the abduction interpretation, have regarded him as a sincere witness. The central disagreement is whether sincere recollection obtained through hypnosis can establish that an event actually happened as remembered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40574575/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Does Hypnosis Aid Memory Retrieval?: A Review of Steven Jay Lynn&#x27;s Research - PubMedJune 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 18, 2025</span></span></span>

## How supporters and sceptics interpret the same evidence

### The supportive interpretation

Those who accept the abduction account generally point to several features:

* Godfrey was a serving police officer whose initial report was made as part of his professional duties.
* He did not begin with a fully developed abduction story; the missing-time element came first and the later memories emerged during investigation.
* The hypnosis sessions produced a detailed narrative that, supporters argue, would be difficult to fabricate deliberately.
* Some supporters view the physical details associated with the encounter, such as reported effects at the site and changes in Godfrey’s recollection, as signs that something unusual occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/police-constable-alan-godfreys-abduction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">police constable alan godfreys abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1980: Police constable Alan Godfrey&#x27;s abductionApril 8, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

From this perspective, hypnosis did not create the experience but allowed Godfrey to access memories that had been inaccessible. The case remains significant because it combines a credible witness with a later, unusually detailed account.

### The sceptical interpretation

Sceptical analyses focus on the limits of the evidence. The main objections are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The abduction narrative appeared only after hypnosis.
* There is no independent confirmation of the alleged interior of the craft or the beings described.
* Hypnosis is known to be vulnerable to suggestion and memory distortion.
* Confidence and detail do not necessarily indicate historical accuracy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40574575/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Does Hypnosis Aid Memory Retrieval?: A Review of Steven Jay Lynn&#x27;s Research - PubMedJune 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 18, 2025</span></span></span>

</div>

From this viewpoint, the Godfrey case demonstrates the difference between a genuine unexplained experience and a proven extraordinary event. A person can sincerely report an experience while the explanation attached to that experience remains uncertain.

## What the controversy reveals about the case

The hypnosis debate is what separates the Alan Godfrey encounter from many ordinary UFO reports. The case is not disputed only because of the object allegedly seen over Todmorden; it is disputed because a limited initial memory was later expanded into a far more dramatic account through a method now regarded with caution by many memory researchers.

The most defensible assessment is therefore a divided one. The evidence supports that Godfrey reported an unusual event and that he later described an abduction experience during regression sessions. It does not independently establish that the recovered memories represent an actual physical abduction. The unresolved question is not whether Godfrey remembered the story, but whether the memory produced by hypnosis should be treated as a record of an event or as a reconstruction shaped by the uncertainties surrounding the original encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40574575/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Does Hypnosis Aid Memory Retrieval?: A Review of Steven Jay Lynn&#x27;s Research - PubMedJune 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 18, 2025</span></span></span>
