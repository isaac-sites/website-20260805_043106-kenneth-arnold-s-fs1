## Introduction

The most debated claim in the Loch Raven Dam incident of 1958 is not simply that two witnesses saw an unusual object, but that the object appeared to interact with their car. Philip Small and Alvin Cohen reported that their vehicle stopped as they approached the object, then restarted after the object departed. They also described heat, a bright flash, and skin irritation after the encounter. These details made the case one of the better-known alleged “vehicle interference” incidents in the Project [Blue Book files]({{ 'blue-book-file/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://isaackoi.com/ufo-history/ufo/19581026-loch-raven-dam-incident/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: isaackoi.com">[Isaac Koi Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">isaackoi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">19581026 loch raven dam incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Isaac Koi Archive1958.1026 Loch Raven Dam incident &#124; Isaac Koi Archive...</span></span></span>

The vehicle effects remain unexplained in the limited historical record, but they are not independently verified physical measurements. There is no surviving mechanical inspection, electrical recording, photographic evidence, electromagnetic measurement, or other instrument data showing that an external force affected the car. The central question is therefore not whether the witnesses reported a car failure — they did — but whether the failure can be linked to the reported object or whether ordinary causes remain possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/581026lochravendam_report2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report...</span></span></span>

## Did the UFO Affect the Car?

### The reported electrical failure

According to the Project Blue Book-linked summaries of the case, Small and Cohen were driving near Loch Raven Dam on 26 October 1958 when they saw a flattened, egg-shaped luminous object above a bridge. As they approached, they reported that their car suddenly stopped functioning. After watching the object rise and disappear, they said the vehicle restarted and they drove away to report the sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/1958-the-loch-raven-dam-incident/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1958 the loch raven dam incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1958: The Loch Raven Dam IncidentApril 8, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

The timing of the failure is the main reason the incident became associated with alleged electromagnetic effects. The witnesses described a sequence in which the vehicle problem occurred only when they were near the object and ended after it left. In UFO literature, this pattern has often been interpreted as a possible interaction between the object and the vehicle’s electrical system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nicap.org/papers/effectsposition.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effects of position &amp; distance in UFO ignition interference cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects of position &amp; distance in UFO ignition interference cases...</span></span></span>

However, the available evidence does not establish the mechanism. A car stopping briefly does not by itself demonstrate an external influence. A vehicle can stall because of ignition faults, fuel delivery problems, battery or wiring issues, or other temporary failures that may disappear without repair. Because the incident was not recorded with instruments and the vehicle was not examined afterwards, the reported correlation remains the strongest evidence available — and also its main limitation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/s3chap04.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report Section III, Chapter 4: Indirect Physical Evidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report Section III, Chapter 4: Indirect Physical Evidence...</span></span></span>

The same problem applies to the reported headlights or electrical symptoms associated with other vehicle-interference cases. Researchers studying such reports have noted that many alleged effects involve engine stoppage or electrical failure, but connecting those failures to an external cause requires evidence beyond witness timing and perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nicap.org/papers/effectsposition.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effects of position &amp; distance in UFO ignition interference cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects of position &amp; distance in UFO ignition interference cases...</span></span></span>

## Possible ordinary explanations

The Loch Raven account has often been discussed through the idea of electromagnetic interference, because older vehicles relied heavily on electrical ignition systems. In theory, a sufficiently strong electromagnetic disturbance could interfere with vehicle electronics, although the Loch Raven case provides no direct measurement showing that such a disturbance occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/s3chap04.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report Section III, Chapter 4: Indirect Physical Evidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report Section III, Chapter 4: Indirect Physical Evidence...</span></span></span>

### Mechanical and electrical faults

The most straightforward alternative explanation is that the vehicle experienced an ordinary temporary failure that coincided with the sighting. A brief engine stall can occur without leaving obvious evidence later, particularly in older vehicles. The fact that the car restarted after a short period is consistent with several mundane possibilities, including intermittent electrical contact problems or ignition issues.

The difficulty for investigators is that the incident was reported after the fact. There was no controlled attempt to reproduce the failure, no examination of the car’s components, and no independent witness who observed the vehicle malfunction. This leaves a gap between the reported event and any proposed cause.

### The electromagnetic interpretation

The electromagnetic explanation became popular because vehicle interference was a recurring feature in some close-encounter reports from the 1950s and 1960s. Some researchers attempted to identify patterns in these accounts, including relationships between reported object position, distance, and severity of vehicle effects. One analysis of collected reports argued that complete engine stoppages were more frequently reported when objects were described as directly in front of or above vehicles, although such studies relied largely on witness reports rather than controlled measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nicap.org/papers/effectsposition.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effects of position &amp; distance in UFO ignition interference cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects of position &amp; distance in UFO ignition interference cases...</span></span></span>

The Loch Raven case fits that general pattern: the witnesses described the object as close enough to appear physically connected to the vehicle’s failure. But fitting a pattern does not prove a cause. A collection of similar reports may indicate a recurring experience worth studying, while still leaving open whether the cause is extraordinary, misidentified, or a combination of human perception and ordinary events.

## The missing physical evidence

The strongest limitation in the Loch Raven vehicle claim is the absence of measurable evidence. The case contains testimony, police involvement, and Air Force documentation, but no recovered material, damaged equipment, electromagnetic readings, engine inspection report, or other physical trace that demonstrates an interaction between the object and the car.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/581026lochravendam_report2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report...</span></span></span>

The reported heat and skin effects have a similar evidential problem. The witnesses said they felt heat and later experienced a burning sensation or reddening of the skin, but the surviving accounts do not provide medical findings that establish the cause. Such sensations could result from several factors, including exposure to a bright light source, environmental conditions, stress responses, or another unidentified factor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/581026lochravendam_report2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Report...</span></span></span>

This distinction explains why the case remained interesting without becoming conclusive. Project Blue Book classified Loch Raven as unidentified because investigators did not find a satisfactory explanation from the information available, not because the investigation confirmed a non-human technology or a proven physical mechanism. The surviving record supports the claim that two witnesses reported a vehicle failure during an unusual sighting; it does not provide the independent measurements needed to determine why the failure occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/bluebook/unknowns.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Project Bluebook &quot;Unknowns</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Project Bluebook &quot;Unknowns&quot;...</span></span></span>

## Why the car claim remains central to the debate

The vehicle effect is the feature that separates Loch Raven from many simple observation reports. A distant light in the sky can often be debated as an aircraft, astronomical object, or misperception. A claimed interaction with a functioning vehicle raises a different question: did something external physically affect the witnesses’ environment?

In Loch Raven’s case, the answer remains unresolved because the evidence stops at testimony. The reported sequence — object appears, car fails, object departs, car restarts — is unusual and explains why the incident has remained notable among close-encounter reports. Yet the lack of technical records means the case cannot distinguish between an unexplained external effect and an ordinary vehicle problem occurring at the same time as the sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://isaackoi.com/ufo-history/ufo/19581026-loch-raven-dam-incident/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: isaackoi.com">[Isaac Koi Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">isaackoi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">19581026 loch raven dam incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Isaac Koi Archive1958.1026 Loch Raven Dam incident &#124; Isaac Koi Archive...</span></span></span>

The lasting importance of the incident is therefore methodological as much as historical. It illustrates the difference between an unexplained report and a demonstrated physical anomaly: the first depends on the credibility and consistency of witnesses, while the second requires measurements, records, or evidence that can be independently tested. Loch Raven provides the former, but not the latter.
