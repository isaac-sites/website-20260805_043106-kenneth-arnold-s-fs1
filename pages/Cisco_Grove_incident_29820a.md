## Introduction

The case is often discussed as a “close encounter of the third kind” — a UFO report involving claimed observation of beings associated with an unusual object. However, the evidential status of Cisco Grove remains contested. The strongest documented elements are that Shrum reported an encounter, that at least one other person reportedly saw an unusual light, and that civilian UFO groups and Air Force investigators took interest. ## The Night in the California Forest

Shrum was hunting with friends near Cisco Grove in Placer County, California, when he became separated from the group after dark. According to later accounts preserved by UFO investigators, he decided to spend the night in a tree rather than risk travelling through unfamiliar terrain in darkness. While there, he saw a moving white light at low altitude. Initially believing it might be a helicopter or a rescue attempt, he descended and attempted to signal it. The light then appeared to move towards him and stop nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/640904ciscogrove_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportSeptember 4, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 4, 1964</span></span></span>

Shrum later described the object as unlike any aircraft he recognised. He reported that three figures approached: two humanoid beings and a third figure with a more mechanical or “robot-like” appearance. His description included unusual details such as metallic features, glowing reddish-orange eyes on the robot-like figure, and a white vapour that he said caused him to lose consciousness temporarily.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/cisco-grove-1964/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Cisco Grove · California · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Cisco Grove · California · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

According to his account, the beings repeatedly returned to the tree until the situation ended near dawn, when he found himself still alive and was eventually reunited with his companions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufocasebook.com/ciscocounty1964.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufocasebook.com">[UFO Casebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufocasebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO CasebookCisco Grove, California Alien Encounter, 1964...</span></span></span>

## What Was Corroborated and What Was Not

The most important corroborating detail in the Cisco Grove case is not the alleged confrontation itself but the reported sighting of the light. One of Shrum’s companions, Vincent Alvarez, was also separated from the group and reportedly saw the unusual object or light. This provided some support that an unexplained aerial phenomenon may have been present, although it did not independently confirm Shrum’s account of beings approaching the tree.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/cisco-grove-1964/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Cisco Grove · California · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Cisco Grove · California · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

The central difficulty for investigators is that the most extraordinary parts of the story depended almost entirely on one person’s testimony. There are no publicly established photographs, biological samples, instrument readings, or independently examined physical traces that confirm the presence of unknown entities. Later summaries of the case generally treat it as a single-witness encounter with limited supporting evidence rather than a physically demonstrated event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/640904ciscogrove_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportSeptember 4, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 4, 1964</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. A witness can be sincere and still misinterpret an experience, particularly in conditions involving darkness, isolation, fear, exhaustion, and unfamiliar surroundings. Conversely, the absence of physical evidence does not by itself prove that nothing unusual occurred; it simply limits what can be concluded from the available record.

## The Air Force and Project Blue Book Investigation

Cisco Grove appeared during the period when Project Blue Book was the U.S. Air Force’s official programme for collecting and evaluating UFO reports. Project Blue Book investigated thousands of cases between 1952 and 1969 before closing, with its files later transferred to the National Archives for public research. The Air Force ultimately stated that its investigations had not found evidence that UFO reports represented extraterrestrial vehicles or technology beyond known science.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Civilian UFO researchers have long cited Cisco Grove as an example of a case that received serious attention because of the apparent strangeness of the testimony. Accounts associated with NICAP and later researchers describe investigators examining the report and considering it unusual because of the claimed interaction between the witness and the entities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/640904ciscogrove_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportSeptember 4, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 4, 1964</span></span></span>

## Explanations and Skeptical Assessments

Skeptical interpretations generally focus on ordinary causes that could produce an unusual experience. Possible factors include mistaken identification of lights or people in a forest environment, the effects of extreme stress and isolation, and memory changes over time as a dramatic event is retold. These explanations do not necessarily require deliberate fabrication; a frightened person in a difficult situation may genuinely experience and remember events differently from an outside observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/cisco-grove-1964/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Cisco Grove · California · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Cisco Grove · California · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

Other researchers have pointed out that the case contains features common in many historical UFO reports: a remote location, a single primary witness, unusual entities, and limited physical evidence. These characteristics make the case difficult to test scientifically. The report can be studied as a historical account of belief, testimony, and investigation, but it does not provide the type of evidence normally required to establish a new physical phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/cisco-grove-1964/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Cisco Grove · California · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Cisco Grove · California · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

Supporters of the case argue that Shrum’s detailed and consistent description, the reported sighting of the object by another person, and the fact that investigators recorded the claim make it worthy of attention. Critics respond that documentation of a claim is not the same as confirmation of the claim. The disagreement largely centres on how much weight should be given to eyewitness testimony when independent evidence is absent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/640904ciscogrove_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportSeptember 4, 1964...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 4, 1964</span></span></span>

## Why Cisco Grove Remains a Famous UFO Case

Cisco Grove remains memorable because it differs from many classic UFO reports. It was not simply a distant light in the sky; it was a claimed physical encounter in which the witness described an extended interaction with strange beings.
