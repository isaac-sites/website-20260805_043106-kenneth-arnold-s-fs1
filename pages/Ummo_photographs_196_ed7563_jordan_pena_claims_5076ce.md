## Introduction

José Luis Jordán Peña became the central figure in the modern explanation of the [Ummo photographs]({{ 'ummo-photographs-1967/' | relative_url }}) of 1967 because he later claimed responsibility for creating the wider Ummo phenomenon and for staging the San José de Valderas images. His statements transformed the case from a disputed UFO photograph investigation into a major study of how a complex belief system could be constructed, circulated, and defended. According to Peña’s own accounts in the 1990s, the Ummo affair was a deliberate human creation intended as an experiment in belief and credulity, although some supporters of the extraterrestrial interpretation continued to dispute how much responsibility he could genuinely claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The importance of Peña’s role lies not only in the confession itself but in how his claims connected previously separate elements of the Ummo story: the anonymous letters, the alleged predictions of a spacecraft appearance, the San José de Valderas photographs, and the network of researchers and witnesses who promoted the case. His later statements became one of the strongest reasons many investigators concluded that the 1967 photographs were part of a planned hoax rather than evidence of an unknown aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Peña's role in the Ummo story

### From investigator to suspected architect

José Luis Jordán Peña was not originally viewed by many UFO enthusiasts as an obvious suspect. During the height of the Ummo controversy, he participated in investigations and discussions surrounding the phenomenon, gaining credibility among parts of the Spanish UFO community. His later identification as a possible creator of the deception was therefore significant because it placed a recognised participant inside the story rather than an outside critic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The Ummo affair developed around a series of anonymous communications allegedly written by beings from a planet called Ummo. These documents contained elaborate scientific, philosophical, and social ideas and were distributed among selected recipients. The San José de Valderas photographs of 1967 gained additional importance because they appeared to confirm earlier Ummo-related claims about a possible appearance of a spacecraft near Madrid. Critics later argued that this connection was not independent evidence but part of a constructed narrative in which earlier claims prepared an audience to interpret the photographs as extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Peña’s later explanation was that the phenomenon began as an experiment designed to test how easily people could accept an elaborate paranormal claim. In accounts attributed to him, he described Ummo as something that expanded beyond his original intentions, becoming a much larger movement involving many participants and interpretations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elpais.com/elpais/2019/08/29/icon/1567087063_903049.html?outputType=amp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elpais.com">[El País]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elpais.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elpais.com.</span></span></span>

### The San José de Valderas photographs and Peña's connection

The 1967 photographs became one of the most important pieces of evidence associated with Peña because later investigators linked him to the creation of the images. According to accounts published by researchers who interviewed him, Peña described the photographs as having been produced using a model rather than a real unknown object. Some investigators reported that the object was recreated using a small model suspended by a line, demonstrating that a similar photographic effect could be achieved by ordinary means.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elojocritico.info/ummo-la-teoria-de-la-conspiracion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elojocritico.info">[elojocritico.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elojocritico.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UMM O: LA TEORíA DE LA CONSPIRACIÓN – El Ojo Critico</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UMMO: LA TEORíA DE LA CONSPIRACIÓN – El Ojo CriticoSeptember 5, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2020</span></span></span>

This explanation matched earlier technical criticisms of the photographs. Independent analyses in the late 1970s had already raised doubts about the images, with some investigators concluding that the object resembled a model photographed at close range rather than a distant craft. Later reproduction attempts strengthened the argument that the images did not require an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falsificazioni_in_ufologia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Falsificazioni in ufologia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Falsificazioni in ufologia</span></span></span>

The significance of Peña’s involvement was therefore not simply that he offered a confession. His claims provided a possible mechanism for the entire sequence: a creator familiar with UFO expectations, an existing community ready to interpret ambiguous evidence, and a photograph capable of becoming a visual symbol of the wider story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elpais.com/elpais/2019/08/29/icon/1567087063_903049.html?outputType=amp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elpais.com">[El País]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elpais.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elpais.com.</span></span></span>

## Confession and disputed details

### The 1990s admissions

Peña’s most important public admissions came during the early 1990s. In correspondence with Rafael Farriols, a major figure in the Ummo community, Peña claimed responsibility for creating the Ummo deception. Letters dated April and May 1993 contain his assertions that he had constructed the affair and that the supposed extraterrestrial communications were not genuine messages from another civilisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummo-sciences.org/fr/data-E/E50.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummo-sciences.org">[UMMO Sciences]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummo-sciences.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UMMO SciencesE50April 11, 1993...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 11, 1993</span></span></span>

In one of these letters, Peña presented his explanation as his own account of events, stating that he was the author of the deception. Other letters discussed the consequences of the phenomenon becoming too large and difficult to control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummo-sciences.org/fr/data-E/E50.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummo-sciences.org">[UMMO Sciences]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummo-sciences.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UMMO SciencesE50April 11, 1993...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 11, 1993</span></span></span>

His statements were later reinforced through interviews with researchers, including Manuel Carballal, who reported that Peña acknowledged creating Ummo as an experiment into human belief and described his involvement in the production of the famous photographs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elpais.com/elpais/2019/08/29/icon/1567087063_903049.html?outputType=amp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elpais.com">[El País]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elpais.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elpais.com.</span></span></span>

### Why some researchers rejected Peña's account

Although Peña’s claims influenced many investigators, they did not end all debate. Some supporters of Ummo argued that his confession contained contradictions and that a single person could not easily account for the enormous volume and technical complexity of the Ummo documents. The argument was that the material appeared too extensive, too detailed, or too internally developed to have been produced by one individual alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummowiki.fr/index.php/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Jord%C3%A1n_Pe%C3%B1a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummowiki.fr">[Ummo Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummowiki.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ummo Wiki José Luis Jordán Peña — UMMO WIKI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ummo Wiki José Luis Jordán Peña — UMMO WIKI</span></span></span>

Another dispute concerned the later Ummo communications. Critics of Peña’s confession pointed out that some material continued to appear after periods when his health problems would have made writing or organising such material more difficult. Supporters of the extraterrestrial interpretation used this as an argument that Peña could not have been responsible for every aspect of the phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Jord%C3%A1n_Pe%C3%B1a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">José Luis Jordán Peña</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">José Luis Jordán Peña</span></span></span>

However, many sceptical investigators interpreted these problems differently. They argued that a large social phenomenon does not require a single author for every later development. ## Impact on later investigations

### A turning point in the assessment of the photographs

Peña’s claims changed the status of the Ummo photographs. Before the 1990s, the images were debated mainly as a technical question: what was the object, and could the photographs prove an unknown craft? After Peña’s statements, the central question shifted towards provenance: who created the images, how were they introduced into the UFO community, and why did they become persuasive?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elpais.com/elpais/2019/08/29/icon/1567087063_903049.html?outputType=amp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elpais.com">[El País]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elpais.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elpais.com.</span></span></span>

This shift was important because photographic evidence depends heavily on its history. A photograph may be authentic as a physical object while still being misleading about what it depicts. In the Ummo case, investigators increasingly focused not only on image analysis but also on the chain of events surrounding the photographs: prior expectations, witness networks, publication history, and the involvement of people connected to the wider Ummo story.

### The legacy of Peña's claims

The lasting impact of Peña’s admissions is that they provided a human explanation for one of Spain’s most famous UFO cases. They did not convince every believer, and debates over the full authorship of all Ummo material continued, but they strongly influenced the prevailing interpretation among sceptical researchers: that the Ummo photographs were part of an elaborate hoax connected to a broader mythology rather than documentation of an extraterrestrial visit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

For the history of the 1967 photographs, Peña’s role remains the key disputed link between image, narrative, and deception.
