## Introduction

The Oloron “[Angel Hair]({{ 'angel-hair/' | relative_url }})” incident of 1952 is one of the most famous French UFO reports because it combined an unusual aerial sighting with a strange physical claim: a white, web-like substance reportedly fell from the sky and disappeared when people tried to collect it. The event took place over Oloron-Sainte-Marie in south-west France on 17 October 1952, during a period of intense European interest in “flying saucer” reports. Witnesses described a large tilted cylindrical object, smaller red spherical objects, and strands of material later known as “angel hair”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.howstuffworks.com/space/aliens-ufos/angel-hair-ufo.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.howstuffworks.com">[HowStuffWorks Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.howstuffworks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How Stuff Works Science Angel Hair UFOs in Oloron, France &#124; How Stuff Works</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Stuff Works Science Angel Hair UFOs in Oloron, France &#124; How Stuff Works</span></span></span>

## What witnesses reported over Oloron in 1952

### The formation in the sky

On the afternoon of 17 October 1952, residents of Oloron-Sainte-Marie reported seeing an unusual formation moving across the sky. The best-known account came from Jean-Yves Prigent, a local school superintendent, who described a strange cloud-like appearance with a long cylindrical object apparently tilted at about 45 degrees. According to later accounts of his testimony, the object appeared to move in a straight path and seemed to emit a white trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.howstuffworks.com/space/aliens-ufos/angel-hair-ufo.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.howstuffworks.com">[HowStuffWorks Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.howstuffworks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How Stuff Works Science Angel Hair UFOs in Oloron, France &#124; How Stuff Works</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Stuff Works Science Angel Hair UFOs in Oloron, France &#124; How Stuff Works</span></span></span>

Ahead of the cylinder, witnesses reported approximately 30 smaller objects. Through optical aids, Prigent described them as red spheres surrounded by yellow rings. They were said to move in pairs and follow irregular paths, details that later became central to UFO interpretations of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.howstuffworks.com/space/aliens-ufos/angel-hair-ufo.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.howstuffworks.com">[HowStuffWorks Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.howstuffworks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How Stuff Works Science Angel Hair UFOs in Oloron, France &#124; How Stuff Works</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Stuff Works Science Angel Hair UFOs in Oloron, France &#124; How Stuff Works</span></span></span>

The most memorable feature was not the objects themselves but the material that appeared to fall from the sky. Witnesses reported white filaments landing on telephone wires, roofs and trees. When handled, the substance reportedly became soft or gelatinous and then vanished, leaving no sample available for later laboratory examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/oloron-gaillac-1952/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Oloron / Gaillac · Angel Hair · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Oloron / Gaillac · Angel Hair · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

## Why the “angel hair” became the centre of the case

The term “angel hair” refers to a fragile, fibrous substance reported in connection with some UFO sightings and other unusual sky events. In the Oloron case, the material became the strongest element for those arguing that something physical had been released by the objects. The lack of a surviving sample, however, created a major evidential problem: the most unusual part of the event could not be independently tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/oloron-gaillac-1952/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Oloron / Gaillac · Angel Hair · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Oloron / Gaillac · Angel Hair · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

## The Gaillac recurrence ten days later

A major reason the Oloron event gained attention is that a similar report followed soon afterwards. On 27 October 1952, residents of Gaillac, also in France, described another aerial event involving a cylindrical object, smaller accompanying objects and falling white filaments. The similarity between the two reports helped establish the combined “Oloron-Gaillac” case in UFO literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/oloron-gaillac-1952/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Oloron / Gaillac · Angel Hair · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Oloron / Gaillac · Angel Hair · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

## The main explanation: spider silk and atmospheric effects

The leading conventional explanation for the “angel hair” component is spider silk produced during a behaviour known as ballooning. Some spiders release extremely fine silk threads that allow them to be carried by air currents and, under certain conditions, atmospheric electric fields. Large amounts of this gossamer-like material can appear suddenly across fields, trees and structures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballooning_%28spider%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ballooning (spider</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ballooning (spider</span></span></span>

## What remains uncertain about the sighting

The spider-silk explanation addresses the “angel hair” more convincingly than the entire sighting. The reported cylinder, red spheres and unusual movements are harder to evaluate because there are no photographs, radar records or surviving physical traces from the event. The available evidence depends mainly on witness descriptions recorded after the observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/oloron-gaillac-1952/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Oloron / Gaillac · Angel Hair · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Oloron / Gaillac · Angel Hair · UAP Codex</span></span></span>
