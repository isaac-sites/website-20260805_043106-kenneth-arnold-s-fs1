## Introduction

Budd Hopkins’s investigation transformed the Linda Cortile abduction report from a private account into one of the most discussed cases in UFO literature. His work centred on two methods: interviewing Cortile and other alleged [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-8232d7/' | relative_url }}), and using hypnosis to explore memories that he believed contained hidden details of the 1989 event. Hopkins argued that the case was exceptional because memories recovered during his sessions appeared to connect with testimony from people who claimed to have observed the incident from outside her Manhattan apartment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Witnessed.html?id=OxtLPgAACAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books Witnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksWitnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions - Budd Hopkins - Google Books...</span></span></span>

The same methods also created the main controversy surrounding the case. Supporters viewed Hopkins’s investigation as an attempt to uncover suppressed information and assemble independent testimony. Critics argued that hypnotic memory recovery is vulnerable to suggestion and that the investigator’s expectations could influence the narratives produced. The Cortile case therefore became not only a dispute about an alleged abduction, but also a wider debate about how investigators should handle uncertain memories, witness testimony, and extraordinary claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dictionary.apa.org/recovered-memory-therapy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dictionary.apa.org">[APA Dictionary]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dictionary.apa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dictionary APA Dictionary of Psychology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APA DictionaryAPA Dictionary of PsychologyApril 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 19, 2018</span></span></span>

## Hopkins and abduction research methods

Budd Hopkins was one of the most influential figures in late twentieth-century alien-abduction research. Before investigating Linda Cortile, he had developed a research style based around collecting personal accounts, identifying recurring patterns among reports, and conducting hypnotic regression sessions to explore experiences that witnesses said they could not fully remember. His earlier work, especially *Intruders* (1987), helped popularise the idea that some alleged abduction experiences involved missing time, medical examinations, and memories that only emerged later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Witnessed.html?id=OxtLPgAACAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books Witnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksWitnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions - Budd Hopkins - Google Books...</span></span></span>

When Cortile contacted Hopkins, she was already familiar with his ideas and had become involved in a community of people who believed they had experienced similar events. Hopkins treated her initial account as part of a broader abduction pattern. According to his later presentation of the case, hypnosis sessions revealed additional details beyond her first recollections, including descriptions of being taken through the apartment window, encounters with non-human figures, and links to other alleged experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinsight.com/aliens/abductions/alien-abduction-linda-cortile" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinsight.com">[UFO Insight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinsight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Insight The Controversial Alien Abduction Case Of Linda Cortile</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO InsightThe Controversial Alien Abduction Case Of Linda Cortile - UFO InsightMay 14, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 14, 2018</span></span></span>

The investigation changed direction when Hopkins received accounts from people who claimed they had witnessed the event externally. He considered these reports important because they appeared to provide independent confirmation of details that Cortile had described. In *Witnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions* (1996), Hopkins presented the case as a rare example of an alleged abduction supported by multiple observers rather than only the memory of a single experiencer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Witnessed.html?id=OxtLPgAACAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books Witnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksWitnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions - Budd Hopkins - Google Books...</span></span></span>

However, the strength of this approach depended heavily on the reliability of testimony. Much of the case record consisted of interviews, letters, recollections, and Hopkins’s interpretation of conversations with witnesses. Because many individuals were identified by pseudonyms, outside researchers found it difficult to independently verify important parts of the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780671569150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publishersweekly.com">[PublishersWeekly.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publishersweekly.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Witnessed: The Ture Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions by Budd Hopkins, Carol RaineyMarch 29, 1999...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 29, 1999</span></span></span>

## Hypnosis and memory questions

The role of hypnosis is central to understanding Hopkins’s investigation. In abduction research during this period, hypnotic regression was often used as a way to access memories that witnesses believed had been blocked or hidden. Hopkins regarded these sessions as a tool for recovering information rather than creating new narratives. He argued that repeated similarities between different abductee accounts suggested a genuine underlying phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budd_Hopkins" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Budd Hopkins</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Budd Hopkins</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that modern memory research does not treat hypnosis as a reliable method for recovering historically accurate memories. Psychological organisations have noted that recovered memories are controversial because the process of attempting to retrieve forgotten events can involve suggestion, interpretation, and uncertainty about whether a recalled event actually occurred as remembered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dictionary.apa.org/recovered-memory-therapy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dictionary.apa.org">[APA Dictionary]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dictionary.apa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dictionary APA Dictionary of Psychology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APA DictionaryAPA Dictionary of PsychologyApril 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 19, 2018</span></span></span>

This issue became especially important in the Cortile case because the hypnotic sessions occurred after Hopkins had already developed a strong interest in alien-abduction explanations. Critics argued that an investigator who believes a particular explanation is possible may unintentionally guide a witness towards details that fit that framework. This does not require deliberate deception; memory itself is reconstructive and can be affected by questioning style, expectations, and repeated discussion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dictionary.apa.org/recovered-memory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dictionary.apa.org">[APA Dictionary]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dictionary.apa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dictionary APA Dictionary of Psychology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APA DictionaryAPA Dictionary of Psychology...</span></span></span>

Supporters of Hopkins responded that the Cortile case contained details that seemed difficult to dismiss, particularly the alleged overlap between her hypnotically recalled experiences and the accounts of people who claimed to have seen the event. From their perspective, the apparent convergence of separate narratives was evidence that the memories were revealing something real rather than simply being shaped by suggestion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.exopaedia.org/Cortile%2C%2BLinda" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: exopaedia.org">[Exopaedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">exopaedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cortile, Linda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Exopaedia:: Cortile, Linda...</span></span></span>

The dispute therefore turned on a fundamental question: whether agreement between accounts should be interpreted primarily as corroboration, or whether shared expectations and prior exposure to similar ideas could also produce similarities.

## The *Witnessed* book controversy

Hopkins’s 1996 book *Witnessed* became the main public presentation of the Cortile investigation. The book combined Cortile’s account, the alleged witness testimonies, Hopkins’s analysis, and material from his investigation into a single narrative. It argued that the Brooklyn Bridge abduction was among the strongest cases in UFO history because it appeared to involve both an experiencer and outside observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Witnessed.html?id=OxtLPgAACAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books Witnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksWitnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions - Budd Hopkins - Google Books...</span></span></span>

The book also intensified criticism because of the way evidence was presented. Several important witnesses were protected through pseudonyms, including the two men who claimed to have been security personnel connected with a United Nations official. This protected privacy but also limited independent verification. Critics argued that extraordinary claims required publicly testable evidence, while Hopkins maintained that revealing identities could harm witnesses who feared ridicule or professional consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780671569150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publishersweekly.com">[PublishersWeekly.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publishersweekly.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Witnessed: The Ture Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions by Budd Hopkins, Carol RaineyMarch 29, 1999...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 29, 1999</span></span></span>

A further challenge came from later criticism by Carol Rainey, a documentary filmmaker who was married to Hopkins and had worked closely with his research. Rainey later questioned aspects of the Cortile investigation, including the reliability of hypnotic sessions and the way Hopkins handled conflicting information. She argued that some of his most famous cases received stronger promotion than their evidence warranted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://johnemackinstitute.org/2011/01/the-priests-of-high-strangeness-a-warning-about-expectations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: johnemackinstitute.org">[John Mack Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">johnemackinstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John Mack InstituteJanuary 15, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 15, 2011</span></span></span>

The controversy did not eliminate the case’s importance within UFO history. Instead, it shifted the focus from simply asking whether the alleged abduction occurred to asking how investigators should evaluate testimony when physical evidence is limited. The Cortile investigation became an example of the difficulties involved when a researcher is both collecting evidence and interpreting experiences that already fit a particular theory.

## What Hopkins’s investigation changed in the Cortile case

Hopkins’s greatest influence on the Linda Cortile case was not the discovery of physical proof, but the construction of a detailed investigative narrative. Through interviews, hypnosis, and publication, he turned a personal report into a complex case involving alleged witnesses, hidden memories, and disputed documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Witnessed.html?id=OxtLPgAACAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books Witnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksWitnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions - Budd Hopkins - Google Books...</span></span></span>

For believers, this process demonstrated how a seemingly isolated experience could reveal a larger event with multiple layers of testimony. For critics, it showed the risks of relying on memory-based evidence when the investigator’s assumptions may shape the interpretation of events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dictionary.apa.org/recovered-memory-therapy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dictionary.apa.org">[APA Dictionary]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dictionary.apa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dictionary APA Dictionary of Psychology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APA DictionaryAPA Dictionary of PsychologyApril 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 19, 2018</span></span></span>

The lasting significance of Hopkins’s work on the Cortile case lies in this unresolved tension. His investigation remains one of the most detailed attempts to document an alleged alien-abduction experience, but it also illustrates why recovered memories and witness testimony require careful evaluation when independent physical evidence is unavailable.
