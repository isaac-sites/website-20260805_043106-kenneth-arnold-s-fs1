## Introduction

[Herbert Schirmer]({{ 'herbert-schirmer-abduction-1967/' | relative_url }})’s night encounter on 3 December 1967 is remembered because the earliest part of the case contains a relatively simple witness report: a Nebraska police officer on patrol saw an unusual object near a rural junction, returned to his station, and recorded the event in the police log. The more elaborate abduction narrative came later through hypnotic sessions. This separation between the immediate report and later recollection is central to understanding the case: the first account is a documented sighting by a single witness, while the most extraordinary details depend on memories recovered after hypnosis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoevidence.org/cases/case659.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoevidence.org">[UFO Evidence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoevidence.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Evidence Police Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO EvidencePolice Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction - Ashland, Nebraska, United States - December 3, 1967 - UFO EvidenceDecember 3, 1967...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 1967</span></span></span>

The timeline of Schirmer’s account therefore has two distinct phases. The first is the roadside encounter itself, including the object he reported seeing, the police log entry, and his claim of missing time. The second is the later expansion of the story, when he described contact with occupants of the craft during hypnosis. Supporters view the early report as an important contemporaneous record; sceptics argue that the later material cannot be separated from the limitations of hypnotically recovered memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/304669" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en-academic.com">[Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en-academic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Schirmer Abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Schirmer Abduction</span></span></span>

## The Patrol Encounter and Initial Report

### A rural stop becomes a UFO sighting

During the early hours of 3 December 1967, 22-year-old Ashland, Nebraska patrolman Herbert Schirmer was driving his police vehicle near the junction of U.S. Highway 6 and Nebraska Highway 63. He reported seeing what he first interpreted as a possible disabled vehicle or truck because of flashing red lights. As he approached, he said the object appeared unlike a normal road vehicle: he described a hovering, metallic-looking craft with red lights and a disc-like shape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoevidence.org/cases/case659.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoevidence.org">[UFO Evidence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoevidence.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Evidence Police Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO EvidencePolice Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction - Ashland, Nebraska, United States - December 3, 1967 - UFO EvidenceDecember 3, 1967...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 1967</span></span></span>

According to later accounts of his statement, Schirmer watched the object rise from its position near the road. He described sounds from the object and a rapid departure, with the craft moving upward and disappearing from view. The sighting itself lasted only a short period by his own estimate, but the sequence became the foundation for the later missing-time claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoevidence.org/cases/case659.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoevidence.org">[UFO Evidence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoevidence.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Evidence Police Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO EvidencePolice Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction - Ashland, Nebraska, United States - December 3, 1967 - UFO EvidenceDecember 3, 1967...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 1967</span></span></span>

The importance of this first stage is that it did not initially involve an abduction claim. Schirmer’s immediate report was a sighting of an unidentified object during a patrol shift. The later description of being taken aboard, encountering beings, and receiving messages was added during subsequent investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/304669" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en-academic.com">[Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en-academic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Schirmer Abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Schirmer Abduction</span></span></span>

## The Police Log and Missing Time Claim

After leaving the area, Schirmer returned to the police station and made a brief written entry in the department log. The entry is often cited by UFO researchers because it was made close to the reported event rather than years later. It recorded that he had seen a “flying saucer” at the highway junction and included the unusual phrase “Believe it or not!”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoevidence.org/cases/case659.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoevidence.org">[UFO Evidence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoevidence.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Evidence Police Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO EvidencePolice Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction - Ashland, Nebraska, United States - December 3, 1967 - UFO EvidenceDecember 3, 1967...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 3, 1967</span></span></span>

The timing of his return became one of the most discussed elements of the case. Schirmer later said that he believed only a short period had passed during the encounter, but when he reached the station the clock indicated that approximately 20 minutes were unaccounted for. This reported gap became the basis for describing the case as involving “missing time”, a feature that later became common in many alleged abduction reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/police-officer-herbert-schirmer-abduction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">police officer herbert schirmer abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1967: Police Officer Herbert Schirmer Abduction...</span></span></span>

The missing-time claim is also where interpretations begin to divide. For supporters, the combination of a police log entry, a trained observer, and a reported unexplained time gap makes the event unusual. For critics, the absence of independent witnesses, recordings, or physical measurements means the missing interval cannot establish what happened during that period. The strongest documented fact is that Schirmer made a report; the cause of the missing time remains unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufoevidence.com/case/herbert-schirmer-abduction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoevidence.com">[UFO Evidence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoevidence.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Evidence Herbert Schirmer Abduction – UFOevidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Evidence Herbert Schirmer Abduction – UFOevidence</span></span></span>

## Witness Details After the Encounter

Following the reported sighting, Schirmer said he experienced physical effects including a headache, illness, and a red mark or welt on his neck. These details became part of the later case file because investigators considered them possible indications that something unusual had occurred. However, no independent evidence established that the mark or symptoms were caused by an extraordinary event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theunexplainedmysteries.com/Schirmer-Abduction.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theunexplainedmysteries.com">[Unexplained Mysteries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theunexplainedmysteries.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Schirmer Abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unexplained Mysteries1967-The Herbert Schirmer Abduction...</span></span></span>

The physical symptoms also illustrate the difference between observation and interpretation in the Schirmer case. The existence of a reported headache or mark is a record of what Schirmer experienced, but the explanation for those symptoms depends on which interpretation is accepted. UFO investigators treated them as possible supporting details, while sceptical analyses have noted that ordinary causes cannot be ruled out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/304669" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en-academic.com">[Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en-academic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Schirmer Abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Schirmer Abduction</span></span></span>

## How the Story Expanded Under Hypnosis

The most detailed parts of Schirmer’s account emerged months after the original sighting. In February 1968, psychologist R. Leo Sprinkle conducted a hypnotic session connected with the investigations surrounding the case.
