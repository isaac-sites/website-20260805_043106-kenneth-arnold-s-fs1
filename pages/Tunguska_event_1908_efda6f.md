## Introduction

The event became famous because it combined overwhelming destruction with an apparent lack of a visible impactor. Millions of trees were flattened, shock waves travelled across continents, and eyewitnesses described a brilliant fireball and intense heat, yet no large meteorite fragment or crater was found. That gap between dramatic effects and missing physical remains created decades of debate, ranging from serious scientific disagreements over the object’s nature to unsupported claims involving exotic causes. Modern planetary science considers the airburst explanation the strongest fit for the evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/tunguska-revisited-111-year-old-mystery-impact-inspires-new-more-optimistic-asteroid-predictions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tunguska Revisited: 111-Year-Old Mystery Impact Inspires New, More Optimistic Asteroid Predictions - NASAJune 26, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2019</span></span></span>

## What happened above Siberia on 30 June 1908?

At around 7:00 in the morning local time, residents and Indigenous Evenki witnesses in the sparsely populated Siberian region reported seeing a bright object crossing the sky. Accounts collected later described a glowing trail, a flash brighter than the Sun, intense heat, and a violent blast that threw people from their feet and damaged structures. Reports also came from hundreds of kilometres away, where people observed unusual skies and felt shock waves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/history/115-years-ago-the-tunguska-asteroid-impact-event/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event - NASAJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

The explosion occurred over a remote part of the Russian Empire, which greatly reduced the human consequences. Although there were injuries and later studies have examined possible deaths among people in the wider area, there was no mass casualty event because the blast happened over largely uninhabited forest rather than a city.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0019103518305104" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ScienceDirect Tunguska eyewitness accounts, injuries, and casualties</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tunguska eyewitness accounts, injuries, and casualties - ScienceDirectJuly 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2019</span></span></span>

The physical effects were enormous. Later surveys mapped a flattened forest area covering roughly 2,000 square kilometres, with trees knocked down in a distinctive pattern spreading outward from the explosion point. Near the centre, some trees remained standing but were stripped and damaged, a pattern consistent with an explosion high above the ground rather than a direct strike.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/history/115-years-ago-the-tunguska-asteroid-impact-event/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event - NASAJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why scientists think it was an airburst, not a ground impact

The missing crater is one of the most misunderstood aspects of Tunguska. A large object striking the ground normally leaves a crater and surviving fragments, but Tunguska’s evidence points to the object breaking apart in the atmosphere.

An airburst occurs when a space rock enters the atmosphere at extremely high speed and experiences enormous pressure and heating. If the object is weak or fragments under stress, much of its energy can be released before reaching the surface. The resulting blast wave can flatten forests over a huge area even though the object itself leaves little or no visible trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/facts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Meteors and Meteorites: Facts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceMeteors and Meteorites: Facts - NASA ScienceFebruary 14, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 14, 2025</span></span></span>

Scientists estimate that the Tunguska object was probably around 50–60 metres across, although estimates vary depending on assumptions about its composition, speed, and angle of entry. The explosion energy is generally estimated in the range of several megatons of TNT, making it hundreds of times more powerful than the atomic bomb used at Hiroshima.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/facts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Meteors and Meteorites: Facts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceMeteors and Meteorites: Facts - NASA ScienceFebruary 14, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 14, 2025</span></span></span>

The event is often compared with the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor, another atmospheric explosion over Russia. Chelyabinsk involved a much smaller object, but it demonstrated the same basic mechanism: a space rock entering the atmosphere, fragmenting, and producing a powerful shock wave without a traditional crater.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/facts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Meteors and Meteorites: Facts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceMeteors and Meteorites: Facts - NASA ScienceFebruary 14, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 14, 2025</span></span></span>

## How the investigation unfolded after the explosion

The remoteness of the Tunguska region delayed scientific investigation. The first major expedition was led by Russian mineralogist Leonid Kulik in 1927, almost two decades after the event. Kulik expected to find a large meteorite crater, but instead found the enormous area of flattened forest and evidence of an aerial explosion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/history/115-years-ago-the-tunguska-asteroid-impact-event/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event - NASAJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

Kulik’s work transformed Tunguska from a mysterious local disaster into a major scientific case study. Later expeditions mapped the tree-fall pattern, studied soil and vegetation, and searched for fragments of the incoming body. Researchers found microscopic material consistent with an extraterrestrial source, but no large surviving piece of the object was recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/history/115-years-ago-the-tunguska-asteroid-impact-event/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event - NASAJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

The lack of a recovered meteorite remains one reason the event continues to attract debate. However, modern impact modelling, comparisons with other airbursts, and analysis of the destruction pattern strongly support an atmospheric explosion caused by a cosmic object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2018/06/tunguska-event" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aps.org">[American Physical Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 30, 1908: The Tunguska Event &#124; American Physical Society...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 1908</span></span></span>

## Asteroid or comet: what caused Tunguska?

The main scientific debate has been about the type of object involved rather than whether something from space caused the explosion.

### The asteroid explanation

The current mainstream view favours a stony asteroid or asteroid-like body. Orbital modelling, chemical evidence from collected samples, and comparisons with other meteor events have supported this interpretation. A rocky object would explain why it could produce a major blast while leaving little surviving material after fragmentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2018/06/tunguska-event" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aps.org">[American Physical Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 30, 1908: The Tunguska Event &#124; American Physical Society...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 1908</span></span></span>

The asteroid explanation also fits later discoveries about near-Earth objects: relatively small asteroids can still carry enormous energy because they travel at many kilometres per second. Tunguska demonstrated that objects far smaller than the asteroid that caused the dinosaur extinction can still create regional devastation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/history/115-years-ago-the-tunguska-asteroid-impact-event/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event - NASAJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

### The comet explanation

Some researchers have proposed that Tunguska was caused by a comet fragment. Comets contain large amounts of volatile material that can break apart and vaporise during atmospheric entry, which could help explain the absence of a crater and the unusual atmospheric effects reported after the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/space-astronomy/tunguska-event" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rmg.co.uk">[Royal Museums Greenwich]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rmg.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rmg.co.uk.</span></span></span>

The comet idea was historically influential, but it is less favoured today than the asteroid explanation. The available evidence does not provide a complete chemical fingerprint of the original body, so the exact composition remains uncertain even though the general airburst model is widely accepted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/tunguska-revisited-111-year-old-mystery-impact-inspires-new-more-optimistic-asteroid-predictions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tunguska Revisited: 111-Year-Old Mystery Impact Inspires New, More Optimistic Asteroid Predictions - NASAJune 26, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2019</span></span></span>

## Theories that do not fit the evidence

Because Tunguska lacked a crater and a recovered object, many alternative explanations have been suggested. These have included volcanic activity, a miniature black hole, antimatter, human-made weapons, and fictional spacecraft scenarios.

Scientific investigations have not found evidence supporting these ideas. The observed fireball, atmospheric shock wave, tree-fall pattern, and extraterrestrial traces are all consistent with a natural cosmic airburst. The absence of a crater is not evidence against an [asteroid or comet]({{ 'asteroid-or-comet/' | relative_url }}); in this case, it is one of the strongest clues that the object exploded above the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/tunguska-revisited-111-year-old-mystery-impact-inspires-new-more-optimistic-asteroid-predictions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tunguska Revisited: 111-Year-Old Mystery Impact Inspires New, More Optimistic Asteroid Predictions - NASAJune 26, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2019</span></span></span>

## What Tunguska taught scientists about asteroid risks

The importance of Tunguska extends beyond the historical mystery. It became a key example of the risks posed by near-Earth objects: bodies that may be too small to destroy a planet but still large enough to devastate a region.

The event also influenced modern planetary defence efforts. Space agencies now track potentially hazardous asteroids and study ways to detect or respond to future threats. The United Nations’ International Asteroid Day is held on 30 June each year, the anniversary of Tunguska, to raise awareness of asteroid impacts and planetary defence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/history/115-years-ago-the-tunguska-asteroid-impact-event/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">115 Years Ago: The Tunguska Asteroid Impact Event - NASAJune 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2023</span></span></span>

Tunguska remains scientifically important because it represents a rare middle ground between ordinary meteor activity and global-scale impacts. It showed that Earth can experience powerful cosmic explosions without a visible crater, and it provided a real-world example of why detecting smaller near-Earth objects matters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/facts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Meteors and Meteorites: Facts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceMeteors and Meteorites: Facts - NASA ScienceFebruary 14, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 14, 2025</span></span></span>

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
