## Introduction

The Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF) investigation into the 1966 Tully “saucer nest” incident became one of Australia’s most detailed official examinations of a reported UFO landing trace. Rather than confirming that an unknown craft had landed, the RAAF investigation treated the event as an unresolved report in which ordinary environmental explanations remained possible. Its work focused on the physical marks in the lagoon, the condition of the vegetation, witness accounts, and laboratory testing of samples. The official conclusion was that the damaged reeds could be explained by natural causes, particularly submersion, while later researchers continued to debate whether that explanation fully accounted for the observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/tully-saucer-nests-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

## Police and RAAF response in 1966

### From local report to military investigation

After banana farmer George Pedley reported seeing a disc-shaped object rise from Horseshoe Lagoon near Tully, Queensland, and finding a circular disturbance among the reeds, the matter was brought to local police attention. The report quickly moved beyond a routine witness statement because it involved an alleged physical trace rather than only an observation in the sky. Contemporary reporting recorded that police involvement led to requests for further examination and that the RAAF was asked to collect vegetation samples for testing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/queenslandstatearchives/50704411782" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentifiable Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentifiable Flying Objects - Page 29 &#124; Transcription: SUN… &#124; FlickrJanuary 23, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 23, 1966</span></span></span>

The RAAF was already the Australian government body responsible for recording and assessing many UFO reports during this period. National Archives material shows that the service maintained records of public sightings, reflecting a policy of examining reports of possible unidentified objects even when many cases were later attributed to conventional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.naa.gov.au/blog/flying-saucers-fact-or-fiction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naa.gov.au">[naa.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flying saucers – fact or fiction? &#124; naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flying saucers – fact or fiction? &#124; naa.gov.au</span></span></span>

In the Tully case, the investigation examined several questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Was the flattened vegetation evidence of a physical landing?
* Did the plants show signs of heat, radiation, chemical effects, or unusual damage?
* Could a known environmental process explain the pattern?

</div>

The investigation therefore shifted attention away from the dramatic description of a “flying saucer” and towards measurable evidence at the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/tully-saucer-nests-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

## Samples, interviews, and official records

### What the investigators tested

A key feature of the RAAF inquiry was the collection and examination of vegetation samples from the reported “nest”. The samples were sent for specialist analysis, including examination by Queensland scientific departments. Tests considered whether the plants showed evidence of unusual effects such as burning, radiation, or other unexplained damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thinkaboutitdocs.com/the-1966-tully-saucer-nest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the 1966 tully saucer nest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1966: The Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

The reported laboratory findings did not identify evidence of an extraordinary event. Radioactivity results were described as insignificant, with only a small variation in one sample that was considered within normal background levels. Botanical examination concluded that the vegetation appeared consistent with death caused by submersion in swamp water rather than a physical force associated with a spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thinkaboutitdocs.com/the-1966-tully-saucer-nest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the 1966 tully saucer nest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1966: The Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

This finding became the central point of the official explanation: the reeds were not considered to have been burned, chemically altered, or otherwise affected by an unknown mechanism. Instead, the RAAF assessment accepted that natural processes could account for the appearance of the flattened area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/tully-saucer-nests-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

### The role of witness testimony

The investigation did not dismiss the witnesses themselves. Pedley’s account remained the starting point of the case: a report of an object rising vertically from the lagoon area, followed by the discovery of a circular reed formation. The official approach was to separate the reliability of the observation from the interpretation placed on it. A person could accurately report seeing something unusual without that automatically proving the object’s nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/tully-saucer-nests-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

This distinction shaped the RAAF position. The investigation acknowledged that something had been reported and that unusual-looking vegetation patterns existed, but it did not find physical evidence requiring a non-conventional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/tully-saucer-nests-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

## Why authorities rejected a confirmed craft

The RAAF’s refusal to classify the Tully case as evidence of a landed craft was based on the absence of confirming physical indicators. Investigators found no material residue, no measurable radiation anomaly, and no botanical evidence that required a technological cause. The available tests instead supported explanations involving ordinary environmental effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thinkaboutitdocs.com/the-1966-tully-saucer-nest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the 1966 tully saucer nest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1966: The Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

## The lasting significance of the RAAF findings

The RAAF investigation remains central to the Tully case because it created an official evidence trail rather than leaving the incident as a purely anecdotal UFO story. It demonstrated a process familiar in many government investigations: collect statements, inspect the location, test samples, and determine whether extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.naa.gov.au/blog/flying-saucers-fact-or-fiction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naa.gov.au">[naa.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flying saucers – fact or fiction? &#124; naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flying saucers – fact or fiction? &#124; naa.gov.au</span></span></span>

For supporters of a UFO interpretation, the case remains notable because authorities investigated a physical trace and still could not provide a universally accepted reconstruction of exactly how the patterns formed. For sceptical investigators, the same record shows that the available evidence did not establish the presence of a spacecraft and that natural explanations were sufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/tully-saucer-nests-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Tully Saucer Nests · RAAF investigation · UAP Codex</span></span></span>
