## Introduction

The Maureen Puddy encounter of 1973 is difficult to classify because the central event was not a conventional physical observation but a reported experience of being taken aboard a UFO while other people were present. The unusual feature of the case is that [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-c7dc15/' | relative_url }}) around Puddy could observe her behaviour, fear, and reactions, but they did not report seeing the entity, craft, or “onboard” environment she described. This has made the incident a useful example in debates over whether abduction reports represent external events, altered states of consciousness, or powerful internally generated experiences.

Interpretations of Puddy’s reported abduction generally divide into three broad possibilities. Supporters of a physical encounter view argue that the intensity and coherence of her experience deserve consideration even without independent visual confirmation. Psychological and neurological explanations focus instead on mechanisms such as dissociation, vivid imagery, memory construction, and unusual states of perception. A third position treats the event as unresolved: a genuine experience for the witness, but one whose underlying cause remains uncertain. Research into other alleged abduction experiences has shown that such accounts can involve complex interactions between perception, memory, expectation, and cultural interpretation rather than simple deception or fabrication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16571535/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alien abduction experiences: Some clues from neuropsychology and neuropsychiatry - PubMed...</span></span></span>

## The reported onboard experience

### A physical event or a perceived reality?

In Puddy’s account, the “abduction” did not involve a publicly observed movement from one location to another. Instead, the experience was described as a transition into another setting while she remained with UFO investigators on the ground. This distinction became central to later interpretations: the physical circumstances and the experienced reality appeared to diverge.

For researchers who favour an extraordinary interpretation, the case is interesting because Puddy’s experience occurred in the presence of other people rather than being recalled years later through hypnosis or memory recovery. Her companions could assess her immediate reactions, including apparent distress and her attempts to communicate what she believed was happening. Supporters argue that this reduces the likelihood that the account was simply a later story created from imagination.

However, the same feature creates a problem for a literal physical explanation. The investigators’ observations confirmed that Puddy was experiencing something intense, but they did not independently confirm the objects or beings she described. The strongest evidence therefore concerns her state of consciousness and behaviour, not the existence of an external craft or occupants.

This distinction mirrors a broader issue in abduction research: a person can have a sincere, detailed, emotionally powerful experience without that experience necessarily proving that the perceived events occurred in the external world. Studies of alleged abductees have repeatedly examined this gap between the reality of the experience and the reality of the proposed explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16571535/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alien abduction experiences: Some clues from neuropsychology and neuropsychiatry - PubMed...</span></span></span>

## Physical encounter arguments

### Why some researchers consider an external explanation

Those who interpret Puddy’s experience as potentially involving an external phenomenon point to several features that make the case unusual.

First, the encounter was not described in isolation. Other people were present and documented her reactions during the episode. In UFO research, cases with contemporaneous witnesses are often considered more significant than accounts reconstructed solely from memory.

Second, supporters argue that the complexity of the experience is difficult to dismiss simply as invention. Puddy reportedly provided a structured description involving an apparent environment, entities, and interactions rather than only a brief visual sighting. From this perspective, the question is not whether she experienced something, but what mechanism produced that experience.

A further argument made by some UFO researchers is that unusual experiences may not fit ordinary categories of “physical” versus “imaginary”. Some proponents have suggested models involving altered consciousness, information transfer, or forms of interaction that do not leave conventional physical evidence. These interpretations remain speculative because they have not produced independently testable mechanisms.

The main weakness of the physical encounter interpretation is the evidence gap. There is no publicly verified physical trace from the reported abduction itself, and the people accompanying Puddy did not report seeing the entity or environment she described. As a result, the case remains dependent largely on testimony and interpretation.

## Alternative psychological interpretations

### Dissociation and altered states of perception

A major alternative explanation is that Puddy experienced a powerful altered state of consciousness. Dissociation refers to a temporary disruption in the normal integration of thoughts, memories, sensations, and awareness. In extreme forms, people can experience events that feel separate from ordinary reality while remaining deeply convincing to them.

Research into reported alien contact experiences has found associations with factors such as dissociative tendencies, absorption (the ability to become deeply immersed in mental experiences), unusual perceptual experiences, and susceptibility to memory distortion. These findings do not mean that experiencers are deliberately misleading others; rather, they suggest that human perception and memory can generate highly convincing experiences under certain conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635162/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Psychological aspects of the alien contact experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psychological aspects of the alien contact experience - PubMedJune 5, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2008</span></span></span>

Applied to Puddy’s case, a dissociative explanation would not require her to be pretending. It would propose that her mind produced an internally experienced event that was interpreted through the language of UFO encounters.

### Sleep-related mechanisms and vivid experiences

Sleep-related phenomena are another frequently discussed explanation for alien abduction reports. Sleep paralysis, which occurs when awareness returns while the body remains temporarily unable to move during transitions between sleep and wakefulness, can include vivid sensory experiences, a feeling of presence, and intense fear. Researchers have argued that such states may explain some abduction narratives, particularly those involving paralysis, entities, and unusual environments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15881271/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Sleep paralysis, sexual abuse, and space alien abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sleep paralysis, sexual abuse, and space alien abduction - PubMed...</span></span></span>

The fit with Puddy’s specific experience is debated because the encounter did not follow the classic pattern of a person waking in bed unable to move. Nevertheless, the broader point is that unusual conscious states can produce experiences that feel externally real and can later be interpreted through available cultural ideas.

### Memory, expectation, and interpretation

Another psychological explanation focuses on how memories are formed and interpreted. Human memory is not a perfect recording system; it is reconstructed over time and can be influenced by expectations, questions, and existing beliefs. Researchers studying abduction narratives have examined how hypnosis, suggestion, and memory reconstruction can contribute to detailed but inaccurate recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nspb.net/index.php/nspb/article/view/288" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nspb.net">[New School Psychology Bulletin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nspb.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New School Psychology BulletinThe -Files: A Review of the Psychological Literature Regarding False Memories of Alien Abduction &#124; The New...</span></span></span>

This issue is especially relevant to UFO abduction research because many famous cases have involved later attempts to recover hidden memories through hypnosis. Although Puddy’s 1973 experience was unusual because witnesses were present during the event itself, later interpretation of the experience could still have been shaped by the wider UFO culture of the period.

## What the Puddy case reveals about abduction debates


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The lasting importance of the Maureen Puddy encounter is not that it conclusively proves one explanation over another. Instead, it highlights a central problem in interpreting extraordinary experiences: evidence can establish that a person genuinely experienced something unusual without automatically establishing what caused it.

</div>

The physical encounter interpretation emphasises the sincerity of the witness and the unusual circumstances of the report. The psychological interpretation emphasises known mechanisms through which the brain can create vivid, meaningful experiences. Neither approach fully removes the central uncertainty: the investigators present could observe Puddy’s reactions, but not the reality she perceived.

For this reason, the case remains a boundary example in abduction research. It is stronger than a simple second-hand story because others witnessed the immediate episode, but weaker than a confirmed physical encounter because the key elements of the reported abduction remained accessible only to Puddy herself.
