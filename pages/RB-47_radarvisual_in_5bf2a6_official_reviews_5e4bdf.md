## Introduction

The RB-47 radar/visual incident of 1957 became one of the most debated cases associated with official UFO investigations because it combined pilot testimony, electronic countermeasures data, and reported ground radar involvement. Yet the official review history is more complicated than a simple “explained” or “unexplained” label. The U.S. Air Force’s Project Blue Book did not produce a public case resolution for the encounter, while the later University of Colorado study known as the Condon Report examined the incident and concluded that it remained unidentified because investigators could not recover enough original data to determine what occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

The case’s lasting importance comes from this gap between the quality of the reported observations and the limits of the surviving evidence. ## Air Force and Project Blue Book involvement

Project Blue Book was the U.S. Air Force’s principal public programme for investigating UFO reports between 1952 and 1969. Its surviving files are now held by the National Archives, including thousands of case records, administrative documents, and related materials from the investigation programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

A notable feature of the RB-47 case is that it was not originally handled as a standard Project Blue Book investigation. When the later Condon study examined the encounter, researchers reported that Project Blue Book had no case file for the incident. The Condon team attempted to reconstruct the event through interviews with available members of the RB-47 crew and searches for military records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

This absence became part of the controversy surrounding the case. Supporters of the case argued that the lack of a normal Blue Book record suggested that important military information may have remained outside the public investigation system. However, the Condon investigators found no surviving intelligence report, operational record, or archived data set that could verify the existence of withheld technical material. Their conclusion was not that the event was disproven, but that the available evidence was insufficient to identify the reported phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

### The problem of missing military records

The Condon investigation placed considerable emphasis on the difficulty of reconstructing an event that had occurred roughly a decade earlier. Investigators interviewed the RB-47 pilot, co-pilot, and electronic warfare personnel, finding that their accounts were generally consistent but that important details differed between witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

One disputed issue concerned whether recorded radar or electronic data existed. Some crew recollections referred to possible intelligence handling of information after the flight, while other crew members recalled that no recordings or photographs had actually been produced during the mission. The Condon investigators searched for supporting records but reported that they could not locate the supposed documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

For official reviewers, this created a central limitation: the case depended heavily on recollections of technical events rather than preserved instrument records. The combination of experienced military witnesses and incomplete documentation made the incident difficult to classify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

## The Condon Report assessment

The University of Colorado’s Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects, commonly called the Condon Report, was commissioned by the U.S. Air Force and published in 1969. It examined selected UFO cases in detail, including the RB-47 encounter, as part of a broader scientific evaluation of whether UFO reports showed evidence of extraordinary technology or phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1960s-condon-report-1968-ad0680975" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1960s condon report 1968 ad0680975</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyThe Condon Report (DTIC AD0680975), University of Colorado / U.S. Air Force (sponsor) · 1969 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

The RB-47 case appeared in the report as “Case 5,” where investigators reviewed the crew’s accounts of a bright object, radar-related observations, and electronic signals. The report described the incident as unresolved rather than confirming any extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

The investigators considered several possible explanations. One question was whether the aircraft’s electronic equipment could have detected radar signals from conventional sources, including ground installations, rather than an unknown airborne object. The report examined whether reflections, signal confusion, or equipment effects could account for some of the observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

At the same time, the Condon analysis noted why the case remained difficult. According to the report, the witnesses described a sequence in which visual observations and electronic indications appeared to correspond, including apparent changes in position and disappearance of the target. Without original recordings or radar data, however, investigators could not determine whether these reports represented a genuine unknown object, a technical effect, or a combination of misunderstandings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

The final assessment therefore reflected uncertainty rather than a definitive conclusion. ## Skeptical explanations and unresolved questions

Later analyses of the RB-47 encounter focused on whether conventional explanations could account for the reported radar and visual effects. A major line of sceptical interpretation has involved the possibility that electronic countermeasures equipment, radar emissions, or signal reflections produced confusing indications that were interpreted as an unusual object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>

The technical difficulty is that the RB-47 was not carrying ordinary civilian radar designed to create a picture of surrounding objects. Its electronic warfare equipment was intended to detect and analyse radar emissions. This made the interpretation of its displays dependent on understanding military systems, signal behaviour, and the operating environment of the 1957 mission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case05.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 5: B-47 Crew, Radar/Visual Sighting...</span></span></span>
