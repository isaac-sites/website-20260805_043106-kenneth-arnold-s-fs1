## Introduction

The witness accounts surrounding the Linda Cortile abduction case are the main reason the incident remains one of the most debated UFO cases of the late twentieth century. Unlike many abduction reports, which rely almost entirely on a single person’s memory, the Cortile case became famous because Budd Hopkins argued that several unrelated observers saw the same event from outside the apartment building. The strongest claims involved two men identified as “Richard” and “Dan”, who said they were security personnel travelling with a high-ranking United Nations official, as well as another alleged observer who reported seeing unusual activity from the Brooklyn Bridge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780671569150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publishersweekly.com">[PublishersWeekly.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publishersweekly.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Witnessed: The Ture Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions by Budd Hopkins, Carol RaineyMarch 29, 1999...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 29, 1999</span></span></span>

Supporters have viewed these accounts as unusual corroboration: if independent witnesses really observed a woman apparently rising from a 12th-floor apartment into a glowing object, the case would represent something very different from a typical private abduction narrative. Critics argue that the alleged witnesses were never adequately verified, that key testimony was filtered through Hopkins’s [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}), and that the lack of publicly available documentation prevents the claims from functioning as independent confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/linda-napolitano-had-23-corroborating-175553500.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yahoo.com">[Yahoo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yahoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Linda Napolitano Had 23 Corroborating Witnesses—The Real Story Behind The Manhattan Alien AbductionNovember 4, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 4, 2024</span></span></span> The dispute therefore centres less on whether people reported seeing something unusual and more on how much evidential weight those reports deserve.

## Richard and Dan’s Reported Observations

### The security witnesses who transformed the case

The most important witness claims came from two men using the pseudonyms Richard and Dan. According to Hopkins’s account in *Witnessed: The True Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions* (1996), the men were travelling in a vehicle near the Brooklyn Bridge area during the early hours of 30 November 1989. They allegedly saw a large glowing object near Cortile’s apartment building and observed what appeared to be a woman and small figures being lifted into the object by a beam of light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780671569150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publishersweekly.com">[PublishersWeekly.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publishersweekly.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Witnessed: The Ture Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions by Budd Hopkins, Carol RaineyMarch 29, 1999...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 29, 1999</span></span></span>

Hopkins presented their testimony as especially significant because the men were not initially seeking publicity. In his version of events, they contacted him after learning about Cortile’s account and feared that what they had witnessed involved something beyond ordinary explanation. Their alleged connection to a United Nations security detail made the testimony even more influential because it suggested that trained observers, rather than only UFO enthusiasts, had seen the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780671569150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publishersweekly.com">[PublishersWeekly.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publishersweekly.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Witnessed: The Ture Story of the Brooklyn Bridge UFO Abductions by Budd Hopkins, Carol RaineyMarch 29, 1999...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 29, 1999</span></span></span>

The identity of the official they were supposedly protecting was never formally established in Hopkins’s public presentation, although the case became widely associated with Javier Pérez de Cuéllar, who was Secretary-General of the United Nations at the time. Hopkins’s description of the unnamed official led many readers and researchers to infer this connection. Pérez de Cuéllar later denied being involved in any alien-abduction event, which became one of the major points of criticism against the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanityfair.com/culture/2013/05/americans-alien-abduction-science" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanityfair.com">[Vanity Fair]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanityfair.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vanity FairInside the Alien-Abduction Support-Group Annual Meeting in Newport, R.I. &#124; Vanity FairMay 10, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 10, 2013</span></span></span>

### Why Richard and Dan became the centre of the dispute

The evidential problem with Richard and Dan was not simply the extraordinary nature of their story; it was the difficulty of independently checking it. Critics noted that the two men were not publicly identified, and that Hopkins’s account relied heavily on communications that did not allow outside investigators to verify their backgrounds or circumstances. Some analyses of the case have highlighted that the most dramatic testimony came from witnesses whose identities remained protected, limiting independent examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/monicamercuri/2024/11/04/the-true-story-behind-netflixs-the-manhattan-alien-abduction-and-linda-napolitano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: forbes.com">[Forbes]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">forbes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The True Story Behind Netflix’s ‘The Manhattan Alien Abduction’ And Linda Napolitano...</span></span></span>

This created a fundamental divide in interpretation. Supporters argued that anonymity was understandable because the alleged witnesses feared professional consequences or ridicule. Skeptics argued that anonymity removed the normal safeguards used to evaluate eyewitness evidence, because investigators could not confirm employment records, travel schedules, personal histories, or whether the witnesses had independent reasons for making the claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/a-critique-of-linda-cortille-abduction/32816151" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: slideshare.net">[www.slideshare.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">slideshare.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a critique of linda cortille abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">critique of linda cortille abduction &#124; PDFJanuary 8, 1993...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 8, 1993</span></span></span>

## Claims of Outside Corroboration

### The Brooklyn Bridge observer

A further element of the case was the alleged testimony of a woman later identified by Hopkins with the pseudonym “Janet Kimball”. She reportedly contacted Hopkins claiming that she had been driving across the Brooklyn Bridge around the time of the incident and saw unusual lights and activity near the apartment building.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/a-critique-of-linda-cortille-abduction/32816151" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: slideshare.net">[www.slideshare.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">slideshare.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a critique of linda cortille abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">critique of linda cortille abduction &#124; PDFJanuary 8, 1993...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 8, 1993</span></span></span>

For believers in the case, this account appeared to strengthen the argument that the event was not created solely from Cortile’s memories or Hopkins’s interpretation. The reported position of the observer was important because it placed another person outside the immediate circle of the alleged abduction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/IUR%20issues/IUR%20Vol.%2022%20No.%202%20Summer%201997.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies IUR</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International UFO ReporterSummer 1997 Volume 2...</span></span></span>

However, critics raised similar concerns about verification. Like Richard and Dan, the witness was presented largely through Hopkins’s investigation rather than through a publicly documented chain of evidence. The question was therefore not simply whether the witness existed or described an unusual sighting, but whether the account could independently confirm Cortile’s specific claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eyeofthepsychic.com/cortile/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eyeofthepsychic.com">[Eye Of The Psychic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eyeofthepsychic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Eye Of The Psychic New York, New York: the Linda Napolitano “abduction”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eye Of The PsychicNew York, New York: the Linda Napolitano “abduction” - Eye Of The Psychic...</span></span></span>

### The “23 witnesses” claim

Over time, the case became associated with claims that as many as 23 people had witnessed aspects of the event. This figure helped make the Cortile case one of the most famous alleged “witnessed abductions” in UFO literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/linda-napolitano-had-23-corroborating-175553500.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yahoo.com">[Yahoo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yahoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Linda Napolitano Had 23 Corroborating Witnesses—The Real Story Behind The Manhattan Alien AbductionNovember 4, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 4, 2024</span></span></span>

The number, however, has remained controversial because most of those alleged observers were never publicly identified or independently examined. The strongest published discussions of the case generally focus on a much smaller group of claimed witnesses, particularly Richard, Dan, the unnamed UN-connected official, and the Brooklyn Bridge observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/monicamercuri/2024/11/04/the-true-story-behind-netflixs-the-manhattan-alien-abduction-and-linda-napolitano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: forbes.com">[Forbes]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">forbes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The True Story Behind Netflix’s ‘The Manhattan Alien Abduction’ And Linda Napolitano...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because eyewitness quantity alone does not automatically establish reliability. Multiple accounts can strengthen a case when witnesses are independent, identifiable, and consistent. When testimony is collected and presented through a single investigator, the evaluation depends heavily on that investigator’s methods, documentation, and transparency.

## Questions About Witness Reliability

The central controversy over the Cortile witnesses is not whether people reported seeing something unusual. Several people did provide accounts that became part of Hopkins’s investigation. The deeper question is whether those accounts provide independent corroboration strong enough to separate the case from other disputed abduction reports.

### The role of investigation methods

Budd Hopkins was a major figure in UFO abduction research and treated the Cortile case as one of the strongest examples of external confirmation. His 1996 book *Witnessed* presented the witness accounts as a breakthrough because an alleged abduction appeared to have been observed from outside the experience itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://goodreads.com/book/show/2123027.Witnessed_The_True_Story_of_the_Brooklyn_Bridge_UFO_Abductions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: goodreads.com">[Goodreads]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">goodreads.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on goodreads.com.</span></span></span>

Critics questioned whether Hopkins’s investigative approach introduced problems. They argued that the use of anonymous witnesses, private correspondence, and hypnosis-based testimony made it difficult to distinguish independently recovered information from material shaped by questioning or expectation. Skeptical analyses also focused on inconsistencies within the broader story and on the inability to verify important participants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/a-critique-of-linda-cortille-abduction/32816151" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: slideshare.net">[www.slideshare.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">slideshare.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a critique of linda cortille abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">critique of linda cortille abduction &#124; PDFJanuary 8, 1993...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 8, 1993</span></span></span>

### The Pérez de Cuéllar connection

The alleged involvement of the UN Secretary-General became one of the most memorable parts of the case but also one of its greatest weaknesses. Hopkins believed the unnamed official in the witness accounts was Pérez de Cuéllar, yet the diplomat rejected claims that he had experienced an abduction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/linda-napolitano-had-23-corroborating-175553500.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yahoo.com">[Yahoo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yahoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Linda Napolitano Had 23 Corroborating Witnesses—The Real Story Behind The Manhattan Alien AbductionNovember 4, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 4, 2024</span></span></span>

For supporters, the denial did not necessarily resolve the matter because they argued that security concerns or personal reasons could explain reluctance to discuss the event. For critics, the absence of confirmation from the supposed central public figure removed a potentially decisive piece of evidence.

### Later criticism and continuing disagreement

The dispute continued decades after the original investigation. Later critics, including Carol Rainey, Hopkins’s former wife and a researcher familiar with his work, questioned aspects of the case and argued that some evidence had not been subjected to sufficient scrutiny. These criticisms received renewed attention when the case was revisited in later media coverage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/linda-napolitano-had-23-corroborating-175553500.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yahoo.com">[Yahoo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yahoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Linda Napolitano Had 23 Corroborating Witnesses—The Real Story Behind The Manhattan Alien AbductionNovember 4, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 4, 2024</span></span></span>

Cortile and supporters of the case have continued to maintain that the witnesses are evidence that something extraordinary occurred. Their argument is that independent observers reported the same unusual event, making a purely private explanation difficult. Critics respond that the available public record does not allow those witnesses to be evaluated at the standard normally expected for strong corroboration.

The lasting significance of the witness dispute is therefore not that it produced agreement, but that it exposed the central challenge of extraordinary eyewitness cases: a story can become more compelling when more people are involved, yet the quality of corroboration depends on how independently and transparently those accounts can be verified.
