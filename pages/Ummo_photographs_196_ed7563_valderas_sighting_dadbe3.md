## Introduction

The San José de Valderas sighting of 1 June 1967 became the defining photographic episode of the Ummo controversy. Reports from the Madrid suburb of San José de Valderas described a strange disc-shaped object in the sky, followed by the appearance of photographs that were presented as evidence of an “Ummo” spacecraft. The event mattered because it connected an alleged physical sighting with an already developing network of Ummo claims, giving supporters what appeared to be a real-world confirmation of the earlier story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The historical difficulty is that the witness record is far less straightforward than later retellings often suggest. Some people reported seeing unusual objects around Madrid that day, and several names became attached to the photographs, but the chain linking independent observers, photographers, and the images themselves has remained disputed. Later investigations focused not only on the photographs but also on who actually witnessed, recorded, and promoted the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/ummo/guardiacivil01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: the claim that Guardia Civil authenticated alleged Ummo spaceship pictures...</span></span></span>

## The 1967 sighting reports

### From the reported observation to the famous photographs

The reported event took place on 1 June 1967 in the San José de Valderas area, then a developing district near Madrid. Accounts circulated by UFO investigators described a lenticular or disc-like object seen over the area, with photographs later attributed to the sighting. Contemporary summaries of the case place the first newspaper attention in the days immediately after the event, when Spanish publications reported the photographs and alleged observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/cronologia-de-ummo-segun/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cronologia de ummo segun</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.Cronología de Ummo según David Lozano Márquez (Abril 1995) - Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos...</span></span></span>

The timing gave the event unusual influence within the Ummo story. By June 1967, the Ummo contact narrative had already generated interest through alleged letters and communications claiming to come from extraterrestrial visitors. The San José de Valderas photographs appeared to provide a visible object that supporters could associate with those claims. A reported sighting followed by physical images created a stronger impression than the earlier documentary claims alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

However, the same connection later became a reason for scepticism. Critics argued that the photographs emerged inside an existing belief network rather than as an isolated discovery. The question was not simply whether people saw something unusual, but whether the witnesses and photographs provided independent confirmation of an extraordinary event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/ummo/guardiacivil01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: the claim that Guardia Civil authenticated alleged Ummo spaceship pictures...</span></span></span>

## Photographers and witnesses

### Antonio Pardo and the uncertain photographic chain

One of the central names associated with the photographs is Antonio Pardo. A letter attributed to Pardo and later reproduced in Ummo-related literature described his account of taking photographs during the 1 June 1967 event. In that account, he presented himself as an observer who had photographed the object and provided details about what he believed he had seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummo-sciences.org/fr/data-E/E19.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummo-sciences.org">[UMMO Sciences]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummo-sciences.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UMMO SciencesE19August 26, 1967...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 26, 1967</span></span></span>

The problem for later investigators was the limited independent documentation surrounding this role. Sceptical researchers noted that Pardo’s identity and involvement were difficult to verify beyond the material connected with the case itself. The dispute became particularly important because the photographs were the strongest physical element of the incident, yet the photographic provenance depended heavily on personal testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/ummo/guardiacivil01f.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: la prétendue authentification de photos d&#x27;une soucoupe Ummite par la Guardia Civil...</span></span></span>

This does not mean that every reported observer was necessarily insincere. Rather, the evidential weakness came from the difficulty of reconstructing the event: who saw the object directly, who took the photographs, who handled the negatives, and how the images entered UFO publications. Those gaps became central to later assessments of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/ummo/guardiacivil01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: the claim that Guardia Civil authenticated alleged Ummo spaceship pictures...</span></span></span>

### José Luis Jordán Peña and the later controversy

A second major figure in the history of the sighting is José Luis Jordán Peña, an engineer and prominent participant in Spanish UFO circles who later claimed responsibility for creating the wider Ummo deception. In 1993, Peña stated that he had been behind the Ummo fraud, a claim that became one of the most important developments in later interpretations of the San José de Valderas photographs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummo-sciences.org/fr/data-E/E50.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummo-sciences.org">[UMMO Sciences]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummo-sciences.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UMMO SciencesE50April 11, 1993...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 11, 1993</span></span></span>

Accounts of the case have linked Peña to the production of the photographs, often describing a model-based explanation involving a small object suspended for photography. Some investigators accepted this as the solution to the mystery, while others disputed the extent of Peña’s role and the reliability of his confession.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/analisis-tecnico-de-las-fotografias-de-san-jose-de-valderas-1-junio-1967/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">analisis tecnico de las fotografias de san jose de valderas 1 junio 1967</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.ANÁLISIS TÉCNICO DE LAS FOTOGRAFÍAS DE SAN JOSÉ DE VALDERAS UMMO (1 JUNIO 1967) - Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-M...</span></span></span>

The controversy illustrates a recurring problem in historical UFO cases: later admissions can clarify events, but they can also introduce new questions. Peña’s statements became important evidence against the authenticity of the photographs, yet debates continued over whether he acted alone, whether all parts of the Ummo affair could be attributed to him, and how much weight should be given to a retrospective confession.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummowiki.fr/index.php/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Jord%C3%A1n_Pe%C3%B1a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummowiki.fr">[Ummo Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummowiki.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ummo Wiki José Luis Jordán Peña — UMMO WIKI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ummo Wiki José Luis Jordán Peña — UMMO WIKI</span></span></span>

### The wider group of alleged witnesses

Later accounts sometimes referred to numerous witnesses in Madrid who supposedly observed the object. However, investigators attempting to verify these claims found that many references were vague or difficult to trace to named individuals with documented statements. ## How the story spread

The San José de Valderas episode gained influence because several narratives converged at once: a reported sighting, photographs, existing Ummo beliefs, and media interest. Newspaper coverage in early June 1967 helped move the event beyond a local observation and into the wider Spanish UFO community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/cronologia-de-ummo-segun/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cronologia de ummo segun</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.Cronología de Ummo según David Lozano Márquez (Abril 1995) - Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos...</span></span></span>

The enduring importance of the San José de Valderas sighting is therefore not the strength of its witness record alone, but the way witnesses, photographs, and an existing belief system interacted.
