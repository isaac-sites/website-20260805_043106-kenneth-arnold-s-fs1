## Introduction

The RAF Venom interception was the defining moment of the [Lakenheath episode]({{ 'lakenheath-episode-1956/' | relative_url }}) of 1956 because it transformed a series of radar reports into a reported air-to-air encounter. In the early hours of 14 August 1956, an RAF de Havilland Venom night fighter was directed towards an unidentified radar contact near RAF Lakenheath. According to later accounts from controllers and the official investigation record, the aircraft’s crew obtained radar contact with the target, briefly appeared to achieve a lock-on, and then reported that the object had moved into a position behind the fighter. The encounter became one of the most discussed parts of the case because it involved trained military personnel, ground radar operators, and an airborne interception system rather than only civilian observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case02.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting...</span></span></span>

## The Venom fighter mission

### A Cold War interception scramble

The interception took place during heightened Cold War air-defence operations. RAF fighter controllers at RAF Neatishead, the United Kingdom’s main eastern England radar control station, were responsible for directing aircraft against unidentified contacts. According to RAF controller F. H. C. Wimbledon’s later account, a request from Lakenheath led to the scrambling of a Venom night fighter from the RAF Battle Flight system. The aircraft was controlled towards the reported target by ground radar operators before attempting its own acquisition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/lakenwimb.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lakenheath/RAF Bentwaters UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lakenheath/RAF Bentwaters UFO - Wimbledon...</span></span></span>

The aircraft involved was a de Havilland Venom night fighter, operated by No. 23 Squadron RAF from RAF Waterbeach. The first aircraft was crewed by Flying Officer David Chambers as pilot and Flying Officer John Brady as navigator. The Venom carried an airborne interception radar system, allowing the crew to search for and track targets independently of ground controllers. Later interviews with the aircrew confirmed their involvement in the mission, although their recollections introduced important differences from the earlier controller-based narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1956-lakenheath-bentwaters/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1956 lakenheath bentwaters</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileTwo Bases, Three Radars, One Interceptor: The Lakenheath-Bentwaters Incident, 1956 &#124; The Cold FileMay 28, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2026</span></span></span>

The official Air Force material later examined by the Condon Committee described the sequence as follows: the Venom was vectored towards a radar target, the pilot reported a bright white light, then lost the initial contact. Controllers subsequently directed the aircraft towards another radar return, after which the pilot reported that the target appeared on his airborne radar and that he was “locking on”. The report then described the target moving behind the fighter and remaining in pursuit until the Venom eventually broke away because of fuel limitations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case02.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting...</span></span></span>

### The reported radar lock and tail chase

The most famous part of the encounter concerns the alleged moment when the Venom’s radar acquired the unknown object. According to the Project Blue Book-related account later reproduced in the Condon Report, the pilot described the contact as an exceptionally clear radar return. The same account stated that, after passing the target, the object appeared to move behind the aircraft and follow it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case02.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting...</span></span></span>

The reported chase became the central mystery of the Lakenheath case. Controllers described the target as remaining behind the Venom despite evasive manoeuvres. The pilot was said to have attempted turns and other manoeuvres to regain position, while ground controllers continued to observe two radar returns: the interceptor and the unidentified target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Lakenheath-Bentwaters_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Lakenheath-Bentwaters Incident &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lakenheath-Bentwaters Incident &#124; WikiDiscAugust 13, 1956...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 13, 1956</span></span></span>

If the account is accepted at face value, the encounter would represent a rare example of a reported unidentified object interacting tactically with a military aircraft. However, the evidence does not establish that the target performed those manoeuvres in the physical world. The surviving record primarily consists of radar interpretations and witness testimony rather than recorded flight data or recovered material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1956-lakenheath-bentwaters/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1956 lakenheath bentwaters</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileTwo Bases, Three Radars, One Interceptor: The Lakenheath-Bentwaters Incident, 1956 &#124; The Cold FileMay 28, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2026</span></span></span>

## Pilot and controller accounts

### What controllers reported from the ground

The ground controllers provided the most detailed descriptions of the encounter. RAF controller Freddie Wimbledon later recalled that several trained radar personnel were watching the event unfold and that the target appeared to move from ahead of the Venom to behind it. His account described the pilot calling that he had contact with the target, followed by a loss of contact and a request for further assistance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/lakenwimb.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lakenheath/RAF Bentwaters UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lakenheath/RAF Bentwaters UFO - Wimbledon...</span></span></span>

At Lakenheath, USAF radar controller Forrest Perkins gave a similar account. He reported that the Venom was directed towards the target and that the pilot achieved a radar lock before the object apparently moved behind the aircraft. Perkins later described the target as staying behind the fighter while the pilot attempted to manoeuvre away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Lakenheath-Bentwaters_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Lakenheath-Bentwaters Incident &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lakenheath-Bentwaters Incident &#124; WikiDiscAugust 13, 1956...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 13, 1956</span></span></span>

The importance of these accounts lies in their military setting. The witnesses were not casual observers describing lights in the sky; they were personnel operating radar equipment and fighter-control systems. This contributed heavily to the case’s later reputation. At the same time, radar operators were interpreting electronic displays rather than observing the target directly, leaving room for questions about how accurately the displays represented an external object’s movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/laken.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Lakenheath Case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Lakenheath Case - ThayerSeptember 1, 1971...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 1971</span></span></span>

### What the aircrew later remembered

Later interviews with the Venom crew complicated the traditional version of events. Researchers including David Clarke and other investigators located former members of No. 23 Squadron and obtained accounts from the aircrew decades after the incident. These interviews suggested that the timing and details of the interception were less straightforward than earlier summaries implied.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.creativespirits.net/lakenheath-bentwaters-incident/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: creativespirits.net">[Creativespirits.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">creativespirits.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lakenheath-Bentwaters incident &#124; Creativespirits.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lakenheath-Bentwaters incident &#124; Creativespirits.net</span></span></span>

Navigator John Brady’s recollections confirmed that the Venom was involved in a night interception but provided a more cautious perspective than some popular retellings. ## Questions around the target movement

### Was the object really manoeuvring around the Venom?

The reported movement of the target is the main reason the Venom interception remains famous. According to the traditional account, the object did not merely travel quickly; it appeared to respond to the fighter’s actions, moving behind the aircraft and maintaining position. Such behaviour was interpreted by some investigators as evidence of controlled flight by an unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case02.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting...</span></span></span>

The absence of preserved raw radar recordings means later researchers must rely largely on written reports and witness recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1956-lakenheath-bentwaters/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1956 lakenheath bentwaters</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileTwo Bases, Three Radars, One Interceptor: The Lakenheath-Bentwaters Incident, 1956 &#124; The Cold FileMay 28, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2026</span></span></span>

### Why the interception remains important


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The RAF Venom chase remains the strongest individual episode within the wider Lakenheath report because it introduced direct interaction between an unknown radar target and a military interceptor. Many UFO cases rely on a single witness or a distant observation; this one involved fighter control procedures, radar tracking, and an attempted interception under operational conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/case02.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Files Condon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS FilesCondon Report, Case 2: USAF/RAF Radar Sighting...</span></span></span>
