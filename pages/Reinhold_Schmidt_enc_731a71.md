## What Schmidt reported on 5 November 1957

According to Schmidt’s account, he was driving near Kearney, Nebraska, on 5 November 1957 when he saw a large cigar-shaped or blimp-like object resting in a field. He said two men approached him and invited him inside the craft, where he encountered a group of six occupants: four men and two women. Schmidt described them as human-looking and claimed they spoke a form of German among themselves, communicating with him in English with a German accent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_O._Schmidt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span></span></span>

Schmidt said the occupants told him that they were from another world. Sources differ on some details of the reported origin, with later accounts associating the beings with Venus or Saturn, reflecting changes and variations in Schmidt’s retellings and later publications. His own published account, including *The Kearney Incident Up To Now* and later versions titled *Edge of Tomorrow*, expanded the story into a broader contact narrative involving messages from advanced beings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_O._Schmidt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span></span></span>

After leaving the alleged craft, Schmidt reported the encounter to local authorities. He brought officials to the area where he claimed the object had landed and said there were unusual marks and a greenish substance at the site. The report quickly attracted journalists, and Schmidt gave interviews describing the encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_O._Schmidt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reinhold O. ## How Project Blue Book handled the case

The US Air Force included the Kearney, Nebraska, incident among the notable UFO cases reported in November 1957. Project Blue Book investigated the claim as part of its wider effort to classify UFO reports as identified phenomena, insufficient information, or unexplained cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/ufoe/section_9.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">section_9...</span></span></span>

The investigation did not find evidence confirming that a spacecraft had landed. Accounts of the investigation state that officials examined the alleged landing area but did not establish the presence of a craft or extraterrestrial material. Reports also mention that investigators found a possible ordinary explanation for the greenish substance associated with the site, involving motor oil rather than an unknown substance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_O._Schmidt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span></span></span>

The Air Force’s treatment of the case reflected the broader pattern of the period: extraordinary claims were recorded and investigated, but the standard for confirmation required evidence beyond a single witness statement. The Schmidt case therefore remained an alleged close encounter rather than an officially verified event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/ufoe/section_9.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">section_9...</span></span></span>

## The evidence: testimony versus physical proof

### The strongest element was Schmidt’s detailed account

Supporters of the case have focused on the specificity of Schmidt’s description. He provided a detailed narrative involving the craft’s appearance, the number and behaviour of occupants, the language they used, and the circumstances of the meeting. Some UFO researchers considered the report significant because it was not merely a sighting but a claimed interaction with occupants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theufochronicles.com/2018/04/occupants-of-downed-alien-craft-spoke.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufochronicles.com">[The UFO Chronicles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufochronicles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">occupants of downed alien craft spoke</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO ChroniclesOccupants of Downed Alien Craft Spoke to Witness in German &#124; UFO CHRONICLE – 1957...</span></span></span>

### The physical evidence remains weak

The main difficulty for the case is the lack of independently verifiable evidence. No confirmed spacecraft material, photographs, recordings, or physical samples from the alleged landing site have been produced. The reported traces were disputed and did not provide a reliable basis for establishing that an unknown vehicle had been present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_O._Schmidt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span></span></span>

Because the case depends almost entirely on Schmidt’s account, evaluations of his reliability became central. Investigators and later writers examined his personal history, including a previous embezzlement conviction, as part of broader questions about whether his claims were truthful. Critics argued that these factors weakened the credibility of the encounter report, while some UFO researchers treated them as separate from the question of what Schmidt believed he experienced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_O._Schmidt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reinhold O. ## The later controversy surrounding Schmidt

After the 1957 incident, Schmidt continued promoting his UFO claims. He became associated with the contactee movement, gave lectures, and published accounts of his experience. His later claims expanded beyond the original Kearney encounter, including assertions about extraterrestrial knowledge and advanced technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_O._Schmidt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span></span></span>

His reputation suffered further after he was convicted in the early 1960s of grand theft connected to schemes involving alleged “free energy” crystals supposedly connected to his UFO contacts. The prosecution and conviction became a major reason later sceptical writers viewed the 1957 encounter as unreliable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_O._Schmidt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reinhold O. Schmidt</span></span></span>

For some UFO enthusiasts, however, the later controversy did not automatically disprove the original event. They argued that a person’s later behaviour and the factual question of whether an encounter occurred are separate issues.
