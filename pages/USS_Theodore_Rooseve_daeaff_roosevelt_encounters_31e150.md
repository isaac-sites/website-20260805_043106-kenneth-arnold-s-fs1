## Introduction

The USS Theodore Roosevelt encounters of 2014–2015 became a turning point in modern UAP reporting because they combined trained military witnesses, multiple sensor systems, and repeated observations during an operational Navy deployment. The incidents did not consist of a single video alone: the later “Go Fast” footage was one recorded episode within a wider pattern of reports from F/A-18 pilots and other aircrew operating with the Roosevelt carrier strike group. The central importance of the pilot testimony is not that it proves a particular explanation for the objects, but that it shows how experienced aviators, using advanced military equipment, encountered observations they could not immediately identify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irishtimes.com/news/world/us/wow-what-is-that-us-navy-pilots-report-unexplained-flying-objects-1.3906936" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irishtimes.com">[The Irish Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irishtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2019</span></span></span>

The Roosevelt reports helped change how the U.S. Navy approached unidentified aerial observations. They contributed to renewed attention on reporting procedures, sensor interpretation, and aviation safety rather than treating unusual sightings as simply pilot mistakes or isolated stories. At the same time, later analysis of individual recordings, including Go Fast, showed that a credible witness report and an unexplained object do not automatically mean extraordinary performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission Brief</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission BriefJune 20, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2025</span></span></span>

## The 2014–2015 carrier deployment

The Roosevelt encounters occurred during a period of Navy training activity along the U.S. East Coast involving the carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt and its aviation units. Pilots reported unusual objects during training operations from roughly 2014 into 2015, with some accounts describing repeated radar contacts and attempts to identify objects using airborne sensors. The reports attracted attention because the observations involved military pilots rather than only distant civilian witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irishtimes.com/news/world/us/wow-what-is-that-us-navy-pilots-report-unexplained-flying-objects-1.3906936" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irishtimes.com">[The Irish Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irishtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2019</span></span></span>

A major feature of the reports was the combination of different forms of observation. Pilots described detections through aircraft systems, including radar and infrared sensors, alongside occasional visual observations. In principle, this type of multi-sensor reporting can be valuable because independent systems may help confirm whether an observation is a real object rather than a single sensor error. However, each system also has limitations: radar returns can be affected by atmospheric conditions or processing issues, while infrared systems require careful interpretation of distance, angle, and background conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irishtimes.com/news/world/us/wow-what-is-that-us-navy-pilots-report-unexplained-flying-objects-1.3906936" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irishtimes.com">[The Irish Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irishtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2019</span></span></span>

The deployment period also became significant because it represented a shift away from informal handling of unusual encounters. Navy aviators increasingly argued that unidentified objects should be documented as potential aviation safety issues, regardless of their eventual explanation. This approach later aligned with broader U.S. government efforts to create more formal UAP reporting channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-implications-on-national-security-public-safety-and-government-transparency/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oversight.house.gov">[Oversight Committee]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oversight.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight CommitteeUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena: Implications on National Security, Public Safety, and Government Transparency - Unit...</span></span></span>

## Pilot observations and recordings

### What the aircrews reported seeing

The Roosevelt pilots’ accounts centred on objects that did not immediately match their expectations of known aircraft. Reporting described objects appearing at different altitudes and locations, with some pilots noting unusual radar or infrared observations and a lack of obvious conventional features such as visible propulsion systems. These reports were later discussed publicly by former Navy pilot Ryan Graves and other aviators who had been involved in the broader UAP reporting environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irishtimes.com/news/world/us/wow-what-is-that-us-navy-pilots-report-unexplained-flying-objects-1.3906936" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irishtimes.com">[The Irish Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irishtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2019</span></span></span>

The most widely known recordings from this period became the Navy videos later associated with UAP discussions, including the Go Fast clip. The footage itself shows an infrared sensor view recorded by a Navy F/A-18F aircrew in 2015. The video became influential because it connected an official military recording with the spoken reactions of pilots who were surprised by what they were observing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

However, the meaning of pilot reactions requires careful interpretation. A trained aviator can accurately report that an object is unidentified without being able to determine its identity or physical capabilities. Surprise, uncertainty, and professional concern are important evidence about the encounter, but they are not by themselves measurements of speed, propulsion, or technology. The Go Fast analysis demonstrates this distinction: the object remained unidentified in terms of its exact identity, but the U.S. Department of Defense’s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) concluded that it did not show anomalous speed or flight behaviour after analysing sensor geometry and environmental factors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission Brief</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission BriefJune 20, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2025</span></span></span>

### The role of sensor data

The Roosevelt cases became important partly because the testimony was linked to technical systems rather than memory alone. Modern fighter aircraft use combinations of radar, infrared targeting pods, and other displays to build a picture of the surrounding airspace. When pilots reported objects through these systems, investigators had more information to examine than would exist in a purely visual sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irishtimes.com/news/world/us/wow-what-is-that-us-navy-pilots-report-unexplained-flying-objects-1.3906936" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irishtimes.com">[The Irish Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irishtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2019</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that sensor evidence is not automatically self-explanatory. The Go Fast investigation showed how apparent motion can be misleading when analysts lack complete positional information. A camera mounted on a moving aircraft can create an impression of rapid movement even when an object’s actual speed is modest. AARO’s analysis assessed that the Go Fast object was likely around 13,000 feet above sea level and moving within a range consistent with wind conditions rather than displaying extraordinary acceleration or speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission Brief</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission BriefJune 20, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2025</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to understanding the Roosevelt encounters. The strongest evidence establishes that Navy personnel observed and recorded something they could not immediately identify. The harder question—what the objects physically were—requires separate analysis for each incident rather than relying only on the credibility of the witnesses.

## Why eyewitness reports matter


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Military pilot testimony has played a major role in UAP discussions because pilots are trained observers operating in environments where accurate identification matters. They routinely distinguish aircraft types, estimate threats, and interpret complex sensor information. When such witnesses report unusual observations, the reports deserve examination rather than automatic dismissal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irishtimes.com/news/world/us/wow-what-is-that-us-navy-pilots-report-unexplained-flying-objects-1.3906936" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irishtimes.com">[The Irish Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irishtimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2019</span></span></span>

</div>

At the same time, pilot expertise has limits. Aviation professionals are highly skilled at observing aircraft, but unusual aerial events can involve unfamiliar combinations of distance, lighting, sensor behaviour, atmospheric effects, and incomplete information. The most reliable assessment comes when testimony is combined with detailed technical data, including accurate range, position, speed calculations, and environmental records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission Brief</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission BriefJune 20, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2025</span></span></span>

Former Navy pilot Ryan Graves later brought these issues into public discussion, including during a 2023 congressional hearing on UAP reporting. He and other witnesses argued that military and civilian pilots needed clearer reporting systems and that stigma around reporting unusual encounters could create safety problems. The hearing reflected a broader shift: the debate moved from whether pilots should report such events to how governments should collect, analyse, and communicate the information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-implications-on-national-security-public-safety-and-government-transparency/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oversight.house.gov">[Oversight Committee]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oversight.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight CommitteeUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena: Implications on National Security, Public Safety, and Government Transparency - Unit...</span></span></span>

The lasting significance of the Roosevelt encounters is therefore not limited to whether any individual object was conventional or unexplained. The case illustrates a broader challenge in modern aviation: advanced sensors and experienced observers can identify that something unusual has occurred, while still leaving open questions about the object’s true nature. The value of the testimony lies in documenting the encounter accurately and providing evidence for further analysis, not in replacing analysis with assumption.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission Brief</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission BriefJune 20, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2025</span></span></span>

      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {

    });

      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }

      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;

        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);

        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });

        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
