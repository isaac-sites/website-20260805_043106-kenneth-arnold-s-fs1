## Introduction

The police witnesses and chase timeline are the central reason the 1966 [Spaur/Neff Ravenna]({{ 'spaur-neff-ravenna-sighting-1966/' | relative_url }}) sighting remains one of the most discussed UFO reports involving law enforcement. Unlike many historical UFO cases based on a single observer, the Ravenna incident developed through a sequence of police radio calls, vehicle pursuit, and additional officer sightings over roughly 85 miles from Ohio into Pennsylvania. The core evidence is not a recovered object or physical trace, but a documented chain of observations by trained witnesses who reported following the same unusual light for an extended period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>

The significance of the chase timeline lies in the way it created both support and difficulty for investigators. Supporters of the witnesses point to multiple officers, contemporaneous reports, and a moving observation that appeared to behave like a nearby object. Skeptical analyses focus on the limits of night-time distance estimates, the possibility of astronomical misidentification, and the lack of independent physical measurements. The dispute has endured because the timeline is detailed enough to examine, yet the available evidence does not provide a definitive identification of the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>

## The first sighting near Ravenna

The incident began in the early hours of 17 April 1966, when Portage County Sheriff’s deputies Dale Spaur and Wilbur “Barney” Neff were patrolling near Ravenna, Ohio. The officers had stopped to investigate an apparently abandoned vehicle on Route 224 when they noticed a bright object in the area. Their written statements described a luminous object approaching from over the trees, illuminating the ground and their patrol vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/newsclippings/1966/1966_04_17_US_OH_Ravenna.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County (Ravenna), Ohio,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County (Ravenna), Ohio,March 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2026</span></span></span>

According to the officers’ accounts, the object was not simply a distant light. Spaur described seeing what he believed was a structured object at relatively close range, with a bright underside and a humming sound. The officers estimated its size and altitude, although such estimates were based on visual judgement during a brief and unusual night-time encounter. Those measurements became one of the main points of disagreement between witnesses and later investigators: the officers regarded the object as close and physical, while sceptics argued that apparent size and distance are especially unreliable under such conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>

A key feature of the case was the immediate use of police communications. The officers reported the object over radio, and their dispatcher encouraged them to follow it. This transformed the event from an isolated observation into a moving incident involving other law-enforcement personnel who received information while the chase was underway.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>

## The pursuit across Ohio and Pennsylvania

The reported pursuit covered approximately 80–85 miles and continued from Portage County, Ohio, into western Pennsylvania. Spaur and Neff followed the object by patrol car while reporting its apparent movements through radio communications. Contemporary accounts and later case files describe other officers joining or observing portions of the pursuit after hearing the reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>

The timeline is often presented as the strongest part of the case because it involved a sequence rather than a single moment:

* **Around 5:00 a.m.** — Spaur and Neff encountered the object while investigating the roadside vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/newsclippings/1966/1966_04_17_US_OH_Ravenna.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County (Ravenna), Ohio,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County (Ravenna), Ohio,March 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2026</span></span></span>
* **Shortly afterwards** — The deputies notified dispatch and began following the object after receiving instructions to continue observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>
* **During the drive eastward** — Other police officers reportedly saw the same unusual light, creating additional witness accounts beyond the original two deputies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>
* **Near the Pennsylvania border and beyond** — The pursuit continued until the officers lost sight of the object in the Freedom, Pennsylvania area after a long-distance chase.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufodossier.com/incident/1966-gov-portage-county-sheriff-deputy-chase-c9d6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufodossier.com">[UFO Dossier]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufodossier.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Dossier Portage County Sheriff Deputy Chase — UFO Dossier</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Dossier Portage County Sheriff Deputy Chase — UFO Dossier</span></span></span>

The moving nature of the event became important in later debates. Witnesses argued that an astronomical object such as Venus could not explain a chase in which the object appeared to change position relative to roads, terrain, and the pursuing vehicles. Investigators countered that human observers can misjudge the movement of bright objects at night, particularly when driving and repeatedly changing viewing angles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>

## Other officers and civilian witnesses

The Ravenna chase gained attention partly because the police witnesses were not alone. Mantua Police Chief Gerald Buchert reported observing the object and attempted to photograph it, while other officers in the region also reported seeing an unusual aerial phenomenon during the same period. These additional observations strengthened claims that the event was a regional sighting rather than simply a private misperception by two deputies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oreohio.org/portage-county-ufo-chase" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oreohio.org">[OREohio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oreohio.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portage County UFO Chase &#124; OREohio</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Portage County UFO Chase &#124; OREohio...</span></span></span>

However, the supporting witnesses did not all provide identical descriptions. Differences appeared in reported shape, distance, brightness, and movement. For investigators, this created a familiar problem in UFO investigations: multiple witnesses can increase confidence that something unusual was seen, but they do not automatically prove that all witnesses interpreted the object correctly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Allen_Hynek" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">J. Allen Hynek</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">J. Allen Hynek</span></span></span>

The role of police credibility became a major theme in later discussion of the case. Supporters argued that officers were trained observers accustomed to assessing unusual situations and were less likely than casual witnesses to invent or exaggerate a report. ## The timeline dispute: object in motion or mistaken identification?

The Air Force investigation through Project Blue Book eventually concluded that the officers had misidentified ordinary objects, proposing that the sightings involved a combination of a satellite and the planet Venus. This explanation became controversial because it did not match the officers’ own interpretation of the event as a low, nearby object that travelled along their route.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Blue_Book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book</span></span></span>

Astronomer J. Allen Hynek, who served as a scientific consultant to Project Blue Book, reportedly disagreed with parts of the official interpretation of the Ravenna case. His later criticism reflected a broader concern that some investigations did not sufficiently examine witness statements before assigning conventional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Allen_Hynek" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">J. Allen Hynek</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">J. Allen Hynek</span></span></span>

The main unresolved issue is therefore not whether police officers saw something unusual in the sky — several did — but what the observed phenomenon actually represented. The timeline provides a strong record of human observation and communication: patrol movements, radio reports, and multiple witnesses across a wide area. What remains contested is whether that timeline is best explained by a misidentified astronomical event, another conventional object, or an unidentified aerial phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>

## Why the police chase remains the case’s defining evidence

The Ravenna incident continues to attract attention because it sits between two types of evidence. On one side is unusually detailed testimony from law-enforcement witnesses, preserved through reports and interviews. On the other is the absence of [physical evidence]({{ 'evidence-gap-33b435/' | relative_url }}) that could independently confirm the object’s nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/raven2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portage County Documents Page 35</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Portage County Documents Page 35...</span></span></span>

The chase timeline remains the strongest branch of the case because it allows comparison between witness accounts, official investigation methods, and alternative explanations. It does not by itself prove what the object was, but it explains why the Spaur/Neff sighting became a landmark police UFO report: a short observation became a documented pursuit involving multiple officers, radio communication, and a cross-state sequence of events that could be examined long after the night ended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports2/660417portage.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Portage County Ohio UFO Chase</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Portage County Ohio UFO ChaseApril 17, 1966...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 17, 1966</span></span></span>
