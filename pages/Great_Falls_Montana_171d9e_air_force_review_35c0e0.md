## Introduction

The U.S. Air Force investigation of the Great Falls, Montana film of 1950 centred on a conventional explanation: the two bright objects recorded by Nick Mariana were most likely sunlight reflecting from two F-94 Starfire jet fighters operating near Great Falls. The explanation emerged through the Air Force’s early UFO programmes, first under Project Grudge and later through a renewed review under Project Blue Book. Investigators did not claim the film showed anything extraordinary; instead, they attempted to match the images to known aircraft activity and photographic effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/mariana.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Great Falls (Montana) UFO Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Great Falls (Montana) UFO Film...</span></span></span>

## Project Blue Book examined the film

### From Project Grudge to a renewed review

When Nick Mariana submitted his 16mm colour film to the Air Force in 1950, the footage was examined during the period of Project Grudge, the predecessor to Project Blue Book. The initial assessment was brief and concluded that the images were reflections from two F-94 jet fighters believed to have been in the area. The Air Force’s interest in UFO reports was still developing at the time, and many cases received limited analysis compared with later investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects/Chapter_16" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 16</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 16 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

The case received a more detailed examination after the Air Force reorganised its UFO work under Project Blue Book in 1952. Edward J. Ruppelt, who led Blue Book during this period, reopened the investigation and obtained another copy of the film for analysis. Investigators reviewed the footage alongside witness accounts and available information about military aircraft movements near Great Falls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ARuppelt_-_The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects.djvu/293" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:Ruppelt - The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects.djvu/293 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

A key feature of the review was the presence of a fixed reference point in the footage: a water tower that the objects appeared to pass behind. Analysts used this feature to study the objects’ apparent movement and eliminate some possibilities. Ruppelt reported that investigators considered explanations such as birds, balloons and meteors before focusing on aircraft as the remaining conventional category.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects/Chapter_16" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 16</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 16 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

The Air Force’s reasoning was based less on identifying a detailed shape in the film and more on matching the circumstances surrounding the sighting. The objects appeared as bright points of light without enough visible surface detail to directly identify them, so investigators relied on flight information, timing and the behaviour of the images.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ARuppelt_-_The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects.djvu/293" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:Ruppelt - The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects.djvu/293 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

## Why the Air Force linked it to F-94 jets

The official explanation proposed that the film did not show solid unknown objects but rather bright reflections caused by sunlight striking the surfaces of two F-94 fighters. From below, the aircraft could theoretically have appeared as bright circular lights, with glare obscuring the aircraft structure and creating an unusual appearance on film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/mariana.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Great Falls (Montana) UFO Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Great Falls (Montana) UFO Film...</span></span></span>

The F-94 connection came from records of military aircraft activity around Great Falls. Investigators identified two F-94 jets associated with the local air base as possible sources for the reflections. The explanation fitted one important feature of the report: Mariana and his secretary Virginia Raunig had seen two bright objects, and two aircraft could account for two separate points of light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/mariana.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Great Falls (Montana) UFO Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Great Falls (Montana) UFO Film...</span></span></span>

The proposed mechanism was a known photographic problem rather than an unusual physical event. Bright sunlight reflecting from polished aircraft surfaces could produce glare effects, especially when captured by a colour movie camera at a distance. Under this interpretation, the film recorded an optical appearance created by ordinary aircraft rather than the aircraft themselves as clearly recognisable shapes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/mariana.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Great Falls (Montana) UFO Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Great Falls (Montana) UFO Film...</span></span></span>

However, the explanation depended on several assumptions: that the aircraft were positioned correctly, that their movements matched the filmed objects, and that reflections could account for the appearance and motion recorded. Those assumptions became the main point of later disagreement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/1950-the-great-falls-montana-ufo-film/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1950 the great falls montana ufo film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1950 Great Falls UFO Film: The Mariana Footage ExplainedApril 8, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

## Questions left unresolved by investigators

The F-94 explanation was accepted as the Air Force’s official conclusion, but not all analysts considered it proven. Later studies examined whether the aircraft hypothesis matched the film’s apparent movement and geometry.
